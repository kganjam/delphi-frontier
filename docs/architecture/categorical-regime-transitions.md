# Search is not discovery: the regime-transition layer

*Why the evaluation kernel, on its own, cannot discover anything — and what to add.*

Source: **[B2]** Wang & Buehler, *Self-Revising Discovery Systems for Science: A Categorical
Framework for Agentic Artificial Intelligence*, arXiv:2606.01444v2 (21 Aug 2026), archived at
`docs/sources/pdf/arxiv-2606.01444.pdf`. **Read-level: partial-text** — abstract and §1 read; the
Builder/Breaker and CategoryScienceClaw case studies are not yet read, and this document will need
revising once they are.

---

## 1. The critique, which lands on our own design

[The design loop](the-design-loop.md) proposes candidates, characterises them, grades them against a
**frozen rubric**, and ranks them. Freezing the rubric is right, for the reason given there: it stops
the criteria being adjusted to fit the winner you liked.

But Wang & Buehler identify precisely what that buys and what it cannot:

> "probabilistic prediction and optimization operate within a chosen hypothesis space: they can rank
> alternatives once the admissible variables, artifacts, and tests are fixed, but they do not by
> themselves identify when those representational commitments have become inadequate."

Our loop is, in their terms, **search**. A frozen rubric *is* a fixed set of admissible tests. The
loop is constitutionally unable to notice that the rubric is asking the wrong questions — and it will
keep producing confident, well-ordered rankings while it does.

That is not a small gap. It is the difference between optimising a wing and realising the problem was
never the wing.

## 2. The distinction, as they formalise it

*Their claims, stated as theirs:*

- In a fixed regime with schema category `Ŝ`, the committed scientific state is a **copresheaf**
  `I_t : Ŝ → Set`. Realised provenance is the **category of elements** `∫_Ŝ I_t`, or a
  multicategorical analogue when artifacts have several parents.
- **Search** updates this state within the same schema.
- **Discovery** is a verified transition `u : Ŝ → Ŝ′` which:
  1. **preserves** accepted prior artifacts,
  2. **transports** old evidence by **left Kan extension**, and
  3. **exposes residual content not generated by transport**.
- Hence discovery is "a change in admissible representation, rather than a subjective novelty
  judgment or a higher model score."

*Our reading, marked as ours:* the third condition is the operative one. The **residual** — what
exists in the new regime that could not be carried over from the old — is the discovery, and it is
*constructed* rather than judged. This replaces "is this novel?", which is a matter of taste and which
language models answer agreeably, with "what here is not in the image of the transport?", which has an
answer.

## 3. Why this is worth the formalism

Three practical consequences, and they are the reason this is not decoration:

**It makes "we need a new variable" a recordable event.** Today, when an agent concludes that the
whole framing is wrong, there is nowhere to put that. It becomes prose in a report and is lost. A
`regime_transition` row holds the trigger (the evidence that was *inexpressible* in the old regime),
what transported, and the residual.

**It preserves rejected work on purpose.** Builder/Breaker "retain[s] rejected edits and retractions".
Our `retraction` table does the same. A record of what was tried and refused is part of the scientific
state, not waste — and it is the only thing that stops a swarm re-proposing the same refuted design
forever, which is a failure mode we should expect at this scale.

**It supplies a gate that is not a vote.** Builder/Breaker revises under a **minimum description
length** gate. MDL is a defensible criterion for "is the new representation actually better, or just
bigger?", and it is not a language model's opinion. `regime_transition.mdl_before/after` records it.

## 4. The fish, concretely

The project has already produced a candidate regime transition, which is the clearest evidence this
layer is needed rather than ornamental.

**Regime R1** — *"a zebrafish is a glider"*. Admissible objects: wing area, span, aspect ratio, wing
loading, lift coefficient, glide ratio. Admissible verifiers: the stall-speed equation, low-Reynolds
polars. This is the regime `site/js/fish.js` encodes, inherited from flying-fish allometry.

Inside R1, the loop works perfectly and returns a **10.4× speed deficit and a 109× kinetic-energy
deficit**. Searching harder within R1 — more spans, more aspect ratios, better rubrics — cannot fix
that, and would generate arbitrarily many well-ranked impossible designs.

The evidence that is *inexpressible* in R1 is the observation that exocoetids do not glide from rest:
they **taxi**, beating the lower caudal lobe while the body is already airborne. R1 has no object for
"thrust applied during the transition" and no morphism from caudal kinematics to exit velocity. It
cannot state the mechanism, so it cannot evaluate it.

**Regime R2** — *"a zebrafish is a thrust-augmented launcher"* — adds those objects. Under the
transition: the wing-geometry results **transport** (the stall-speed relation is unchanged); the
deficit figure transports as a *bound on the no-taxi case*; and the **residual** is the taxi thrust
budget, which did not exist before and which subproblem **S24** now exists to characterise.

Note what this is not: it is not optimism. R2 may also fail, and the honest outcome may be that no
regime admits a flying 0.4 g fish. But the failure will then be a *property of the problem* rather
than an artifact of the first representation anyone happened to write down.

## 5. What we are adopting, and what we are not

| Adopted | Not adopted (yet) |
|---|---|
| The search/discovery distinction as a recorded event | The full copresheaf implementation — our schema is relational, not a categorical database |
| Residual content as the definition of novelty | Left Kan extension computed mechanically; transport is currently asserted by an agent and checked by a human reader |
| Retaining rejected edits and retractions | Proof-carrying artifacts in the CategoryScienceClaw sense |
| An MDL-style gate on representation change | MDL computed automatically; currently a recorded number, not an enforced threshold |

The honest summary of the gap: we have adopted the **vocabulary and the discipline**, not the
mathematics. `regime_transition.transported` and `.residual` are text fields an agent fills in. That
makes them auditable, which is worth a lot, and it does **not** make them verified, which the paper's
formalism would. Treating a text field as though it were a left Kan extension would be exactly the
kind of borrowed authority this repository refuses elsewhere.

## 6. What this does not establish

- Two of the paper's three conditions (preservation, transport) are currently unchecked assertions.
- No regime transition has been executed through the schema. R1 → R2 above is worked as an example
  from real results, but has not been recorded as a row.
- The MDL gate has no implementation, and description length for a mixed symbolic/empirical
  representation is not obviously well-defined.
- The case studies in [B2] that would tell us how they handled all three points are **unread**.
