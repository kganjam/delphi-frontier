# 13. Limbless mouse: the design space

Generated by `scripts/limbless_doc.py` from the same files the live page reads, so the two cannot drift.
The interactive version, with the figures and the filterable route list, is at <https://studio.morphoswarm.ai/#/studio/limbless-mouse>.

**The question.** A judge asked whether we could make a limbless mouse. Rather than answer with one gene, map the whole design space: every route we can source, at every scale, with what each one would actually produce.

## Yes, several ways — and they do not give you the same animal.

The honest answer to “could you make a limbless mouse?” is that the mouse literature already contains more than one way, and the first question back is which phenotype is wanted. Complete absence of all four limbs (amelia) is the Fgf10-null phenotype, and in humans the WNT3 Q83X tetra-amelia phenotype: the apical ectodermal ridge never forms, so there is no limb at all. Truncation is a different outcome reached by a different lesion: the limb bud starts, the SHH–GREM1–FGF loop fails to sustain it, and a stunted proximal element is left behind.

The sharpest precedent for getting there by editing regulation rather than by deleting a gene is Kvon et al. 2016. Replacing the mouse ZRS — the limb enhancer of Shh, about a megabase away inside an intron of Lmbr1 — with the king cobra ortholog abolished limb-bud Shh expression and truncated both limb pairs, while the human and coelacanth orthologs in the same assay gave normal limbs. A 17-base-pair stretch spanning an ETS site was enough to change the outcome. That is the resolution at which this is a design problem rather than a knockout.

If the goal is a viable adult animal rather than an embryonic phenotype, the realistic route is one limb pair, not four: a conditional Tbx5 allele for the forelimb, Tbx4 or Pitx1 for the hindlimb, switched after the body plan is laid down. Tetra-amelia alleles are generally severe and often lethal for reasons that have nothing to do with limbs.

And if the point is the mechanism rather than the mouse, the first thing a reviewer will ask for in 2026 is a limb-bud explant or organoid, not an animal. That route is on this page too.

> Truncation is not amelia, and a page that blurs them is answering a question nobody asked. Every route below states which of the two — or which of micromelia, digit loss, or one-pair loss — it predicts.

## If you asked us to actually do it

**The route.** RECOMMENDED: limb-restricted, stage-selected Fgf10 removal (`timing-prx1-creer-fgf10`)

**Headline.**

If the goal is a limbless MOUSE EMBRYO with an unambiguous phenotype: conditional Fgf10 deletion restricted to limb-bud mesenchyme (Prx1-CreER) and induced at a chosen stage. If the goal is a VIABLE ADULT ANIMAL: do not attempt tetra-amelia at all - take one pair, with Tbx5-conditional for forelimb or Tbx4-conditional for hindlimb, or take the ZRS-null, which is the only limb-reduced mouse in this literature with a published photograph of it alive and of normal body size at two weeks.

**Why.**

- It starts from the strongest phenotype in the literature. Fgf10 loss abolishes AER formation and gives complete absence of both fore- and hindlimbs; it is the reference amelia and it is upstream of Fgf8.
- It removes the thing that makes that phenotype useless. The germline Fgf10 null dies perinatally from complete absence of lungs - a defect with nothing to do with limbs. Prx1-Cre recombines limb-bud mesenchyme from E9.5 and is never detected in limb-bud ectoderm, so the lung, thyroid, pituitary and salivary gland requirements are outside the domain.
- It adds the one axis that the whole field has shown to matter. Naiche and Papaioannou removed Tbx4 at three different times and got three different outcomes, one of which was no phenotype at all. A germline allele cannot express that; an inducible one turns the lesion into an ordered series.
- Every component is an existing, published reagent. This is a breeding and dosing programme, not an invention.
- It gives a scoreable readout. A skeletal preparation either has an autopod or it does not - which is the property that makes this subject a better benchmark than the open-ended design problems our demo is built on.

**Honest alternative for a viable adult.**

- **Preferred:** Forelimb-only (Tbx5 conditional) or hindlimb-only (Tbx4 conditional). A mouse missing one pair retains the other and is far more likely to thermoregulate, locomote, groom and reach food than a tetra-amelic animal. Tbx5 is the cleaner of the two in principle because the limb FIELD is correctly patterned in its absence - only the bud is never started - and because the germline Tbx4 null dies at E10.5 from chorioallantoic failure, so Tbx4 work is conditional-only by necessity rather than by choice.
- **Already demonstrated:** The ZRS-null is the one allele in this literature where a limb-reduced mouse has been shown alive: Kvon et al. photographed ZRS-/- animals at two weeks with body size comparable to wild type and truncated limbs. But note what it is - TRUNCATION with a retained stylopod and girdle, not amelia. If the ask is 'a mouse with no limbs that lives', nobody in this literature has published one, and the page should not imply otherwise.
- **The trade off stated plainly:** Amelia and viability pull against each other at every layer. Every route that reliably abolishes the limb bud (Fgf10, Fgfr2b, Grem1, WNT3, RSPO2, Meis1/2) takes out lung, kidney or the whole WNT system with it, because the genes that start a limb also start other organs. The only two ways out are tissue restriction (Prx1-Cre) and cis-regulation (ZRS, Pitx1 enhancer) - and cis-regulation, the route evolution actually used twice, delivers truncation rather than absence.

**What we would run ourselves.**

- Score the Kvon 2016 ZRS ortholog series with a genome language model, pre-registering the prediction that it separates human and coelacanth (normal limbs) from cobra (none) and places python between them. The coelacanth case is the sharpest test: it shares LESS sequence identity with human ZRS (57%) than python does (59%) and yet behaves like wild type, so identity alone cannot produce the right answer.
- Score the 17 bp E1 indel in both directions on both backgrounds. The published result is asymmetric - reintroducing it into python ZRS restores normal limbs, removing it from mouse ZRS does not alter limb-bud expression. A model that returns a symmetric effect has failed a test whose answer is known in advance.
- Record the Ushiki human-to-mouse CTCF discordance as an explicit REFUSAL in our cross-scale coupling layer rather than scoring it.
- State the dose law in Lean as a law about a model - a monotone map from an activity variable with a threshold band to a distal-element count - and show Lean rejects mutants with the monotonicity flipped and the threshold moved. That proves our reasoning is consistent. It proves nothing about mice, and we would write both sentences together.

**What we will not claim.**

- We have never scored a limb enhancer. Everything above is proposed, not done.
- No registered calibration exists anywhere in our system, so no quantitative bridge from a sequence score to a skeletal phenotype is composable today. The strongest honest output is a RANKING over sequences.
- A sequence score is not transcription and transcription is not a skeleton.
- We have no animal facility, no IACUC protocol and no wet-lab arm. A prediction we cannot test is a hypothesis and we would label it as one.

**Replace the mouse ZRS with a snake ortholog (king cobra or Burmese python).** The ZRS is a limb-specific enhancer of Shh sitting ~1 Mb from the Shh promoter inside Lmbr1 intron 5, and it is the sole driver of Shh expression in the posterior limb bud mesenchyme. Snake ZRS orthologs have accumulated substitutions that destroy the transcription-factor binding sites the enhancer needs, so an otherwise wild-type mouse carrying a snake ZRS makes no limb-bud Shh. Every protein in the genome is left intact; only 1.3 kb of non-coding DNA changes.
Sources: Kvon EZ et al. 2016, Cell 167:633-642.e11, 'Progressive Loss of Function in a Limb Enhancer during Snake Evolution' [PMID 27768887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27768887/) (full-text)

**Delete the ZRS outright.** Removing the enhancer removes the only known driver of limb-bud Shh. Shh protein is still made everywhere else it is normally made (notochord, floor plate, gut), so the animal has no midline, neural-tube or axial defect. This is the cleanest available dissociation between 'Shh the gene' and 'Shh in the limb'.
Sources: Kvon EZ et al. 2016, Cell 167:633-642.e11 [PMID 27768887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27768887/) (full-text); Sagai T et al. 2005, Development 132:797-803, 'Elimination of a long-range cis-regulatory module causes complete loss of limb-specific Shh expression and truncation of the mouse limb' [PMID 15677727](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15677727/) (abstract)

**Edit the single E1 ETS binding site - a 17 bp switch.** A 17 bp deletion overlapping the E1 ETS1 recognition motif is the only sequence present in all examined limbed vertebrates and fish and absent from all examined snakes. Reintroducing those 17 bp into an otherwise-python ZRS restores full enhancer activity in the posterior limb-bud mesenchyme and rescues normal limb development in the mouse. Conversely the site is the smallest known handle on the whole system.
Sources: Kvon EZ et al. 2016, Cell 167:633-642.e11 [PMID 27768887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27768887/) (full-text)

**Design a graded ZRS hypomorphic allelic series from the in-vivo variant panel.** Nearly a thousand human ZRS variants have been assayed in vivo in a high-throughput mouse reporter, and selected variants were confirmed in knock-in mice. That panel is a measured sequence-to-activity map over the exact element that sets limb-bud Shh. In principle one can pick combinations that place enhancer activity at a chosen point between wild type and null and so dial the degree of limb reduction.
Sources: Kvon EZ et al. 2020, Cell 180:1262-1271.e15, 'Comprehensive In Vivo Interrogation Reveals Phenotypic Impact of Human Enhancer Variants' [PMID 32169219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32169219/) (abstract); Gurnett CA et al. 2007, Am J Med Genet A 143A:27-32, 'Two novel point mutations in the long-range SHH enhancer in three families with triphalangeal thumb and preaxial polydactyly' [PMID 17152067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17152067/) (abstract)

**Break the CTCF-mediated contact between the ZRS and the SHH promoter.** The ZRS acts across ~1 Mb, and that action depends on physical contact. Three CTCF sites inside a 12 kb interval of LMBR1 mediate the ZRS-to-SHH-promoter interaction. Homozygous deletion of that interval in humans causes acheiropodia, congenital limb truncation, and the interval does not itself function as an enhancer - the lesion is architectural, not transcriptional.
Sources: Ushiki A et al. 2021, Nat Commun 12:2282, 'Deletion of CTCF sites in the SHH locus alters enhancer-promoter interactions and leads to acheiropodia' [PMID 33863876](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33863876/) (abstract)

**Move a TAD boundary so the limb enhancer cluster drives the wrong gene.** Limb enhancers are confined to a topologically associating domain by CTCF-associated boundaries. Deletions, inversions and duplications that disrupt those boundaries let a cluster of limb enhancers contact a promoter it should never reach, producing ectopic limb expression of the wrong gene. The rewiring occurs only when the variant disrupts a CTCF-associated boundary - the structural change, not the sequence content, is the lesion.
Sources: Lupianez DG et al. 2015, Cell 161:1012-1025, 'Disruptions of topological chromatin domains cause pathogenic rewiring of gene-enhancer interactions' [PMID 25959774](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25959774/) (abstract)

**Delete the Pitx1 hindlimb enhancer - nature's own cis-regulatory limb removal.** In threespine stickleback, pelvic (hindlimb-homologue) loss has evolved repeatedly through deletion of a tissue-specific enhancer of Pitx1. Pitx1 null alleles are lethal in laboratory animals; the enhancer deletion is not, and it carries molecular signatures of positive selection. This is the proof of principle that evolution solves 'remove an appendage without killing the animal' by cis-regulation, not by knockout.
Sources: Chan YF et al. 2010, Science 327:302-5, 'Adaptive evolution of pelvic reduction in sticklebacks by recurrent deletion of a Pitx1 enhancer' [PMID 20007865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20007865/) (abstract); Lanctot C et al. 1999, Development 126:1805-10, 'Hindlimb patterning and mandible development require the Ptx1 gene' [PMID 10101115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101115/) (abstract)

**Fgf10 germline null - the canonical tetra-amelia mouse.** FGF10 is the mesenchymal signal that induces and sustains the apical ectodermal ridge (AER); it acts epistatically upstream of Fgf8, the earliest AER marker. Without it the AER never forms, the ZPA never forms, and the limb bud makes no outgrowth. The same ligand drives branching morphogenesis in lung, so the mouse has no lungs either.
Sources: Min H et al. 1998, Genes Dev 12:3156-61, 'Fgf-10 is required for both limb and lung development...' [PMID 9784490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784490/) (abstract); Sekine K et al. 1999, Nat Genet 21:138-41, 'Fgf10 is essential for limb and lung formation' [PMID 9916808](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9916808/) (abstract)

**Fgfr2-IIIb (FGFR2b) isoform null - the receptor side of the same axis.** Fgfr2 is alternatively spliced into IIIb and IIIc isoforms with different ligand specificities and mutually exclusive tissue distributions. FGFR2b is the epithelial receptor that reads mesenchymal FGF10. Removing only IIIb leaves IIIc intact and so removes one arm of the mesenchymal-epithelial dialogue rather than the whole receptor.
Sources: De Moerlooze L et al. 2000, Development 127:483-92, 'An important role for the IIIb isoform of fibroblast growth factor receptor 2 (FGFR2) in mesenchymal-epithelial signalling during mouse organogenesis' [PMID 10631169](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10631169/) (abstract); Ohuchi H et al. 2000, Biochem Biophys Res Commun 277:643-9, 'FGF10 acts as a major ligand for FGF receptor 2 IIIb in mouse multi-organ development' [PMID 11062007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11062007/) (abstract)

**Ectodermal WNT3 / beta-catenin - the human tetra-amelia gene.** Wnt3 is expressed ubiquitously in limb ectoderm and signals through beta-catenin to establish the AER; beta-catenin is also required continuously to MAINTAIN the ridge once induced. Wnt/beta-catenin sits upstream of BMP signalling in AER establishment and interacts with BMPR1A in dorsoventral patterning. In humans a homozygous nonsense allele of WNT3 causes complete absence of all four limbs.
Sources: Niemann S et al. 2004, Am J Hum Genet 74:558-63, 'Homozygous WNT3 mutation causes tetra-amelia in a large consanguineous family' [PMID 14872406](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/14872406/) (abstract); Barrow JR et al. 2003, Genes Dev 17:394-409, 'Ectodermal Wnt3/beta-catenin signaling is required for the establishment and maintenance of the apical ectodermal ridge' [PMID 12569130](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12569130/) (abstract); Soshnikova N et al. 2003, Genes Dev 17:1963-8, 'Genetic interaction between Wnt/beta-catenin and BMP receptor signaling during formation of the AER and the dorsal-ventral axis in the limb' [PMID 12923052](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12923052/) (abstract)

**RSPO2 / RNF43 / ZNRF3 - the master switch for limb specification.** RSPO2 potentiates WNT signalling. An allelic series of recessive human RSPO2 mutations causes tetra-amelia syndrome with lung aplasia, with severity correlating with impaired binding to LGR4/5/6 and to the RNF43 and ZNRF3 transmembrane ligases. Unexpectedly, triple knockout of Lgr4/5/6 in mice does NOT reproduce the Rspo2 phenotype; instead RSPO2 acts as a direct antagonistic ligand of RNF43 and ZNRF3, and deleting rnf43 and znrf3 together in Xenopus triggers outgrowth of SUPERNUMERARY limbs.
Sources: Szenker-Ravi E et al. 2018, Nature 557:564-569, 'RSPO2 inhibition of RNF43 and ZNRF3 governs limb development independently of LGR4/5/6' [PMID 29769720](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29769720/) (abstract)

**Tbx5 - forelimb only, and the cleanest single-pair route.** TBX5 is expressed exclusively in the prospective forelimb mesenchyme before any bud is visible. It directly activates Fgf10 through a conserved binding site, which is how it initiates the FGF/WNT loop. Critically, the limb FIELD is still patterned correctly in its absence: the territory is specified, the bud is simply never started. Tbx5 is also required later for continued outgrowth.
Sources: Agarwal P et al. 2003, Development 130:623-33, 'Tbx5 is essential for forelimb bud initiation following patterning of the limb field in the mouse embryo' [PMID 12490567](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12490567/) (abstract); Rallis C et al. 2003, Development 130:2741-51, 'Tbx5 is required for forelimb bud formation and continued outgrowth' [PMID 12736217](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12736217/) (abstract)

**Tbx4 (with Pitx1) - hindlimb only, and a very narrow time window.** Tbx4 is the hindlimb counterpart of Tbx5 and is required for hindlimb bud outgrowth; Pitx1 sits with it in the posterior lateral plate and determines hindlimb morphological identity. The conditional analysis is the interesting part: ablating Tbx4 before hindlimb-field expression blocks outgrowth, ablating it shortly after onset leaves outgrowth and Fgf10 expression unaffected but reduces limb core tissue, and ablating it later produces no defect at all.
Sources: Naiche LA & Papaioannou VE 2003, Development 130:2681-93, 'Loss of Tbx4 blocks hindlimb development and affects vascularization and fusion of the allantois' [PMID 12736212](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12736212/) (abstract); Naiche LA & Papaioannou VE 2007, Development 134:93-103, 'Tbx4 is not required for hindlimb identity or post-bud hindlimb outgrowth' [PMID 17164415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17164415/) (abstract); Lanctot C et al. 1999, Development 126:1805-10 [PMID 10101115](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10101115/) (abstract); DeLaurier A, Schweitzer R & Logan M 2006, Dev Biol 299:22-34, 'Pitx1 determines the morphology of muscle, tendon, and bones of the hindlimb' [PMID 16989801](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16989801/) (abstract)

**Meis1 + Meis2 in the lateral plate mesoderm - limb agenesis upstream of everything.** MEIS factors act before limb induction. They co-bind genomic sites extensively with TBX factors and co-regulate Fgf10 enhancers, so removing both from the lateral plate mesoderm removes the input that lets Tbx5/Tbx4 start a bud at all. A partial allele also disrupts the antero-posterior prepattern that Shh activation depends on, which is a second, separable requirement.
Sources: Delgado I et al. 2021, Nat Commun 12:3086, 'Control of mouse limb initiation and antero-posterior patterning by Meis transcription factors' [PMID 34035267](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34035267/) (abstract); Berenguer M & Duester G 2021, Biomolecules 11:80, 'Role of Retinoic Acid Signaling, FGF Signaling and Meis Genes in Control of Limb Development' (review) [PMID 33435477](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33435477/) (abstract)

**Fgf4 + Fgf8 in the AER - remove the ridge's output and limb development fails.** The AER's job is to make FGFs. Four Fgf genes have AER-specific expression; only Fgf8 is expressed throughout the ridge and earliest. Inactivating Fgf8 alone reduces bud size, delays Shh and gives hypoplasia or aplasia of specific skeletal elements. Inactivating Fgf4 and Fgf8 together in AER cells or their precursors removes the output entirely: the bud has too few progenitors and cells far from the ridge die.
Sources: Sun X, Mariani FV & Martin GR 2002, Nature 418:501-8, 'Functions of FGF signalling from the apical ectodermal ridge in limb development' [PMID 12152071](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12152071/) (abstract); Lewandoski M, Sun X & Martin GR 2000, Nat Genet 26:460-3, 'Fgf8 signalling from the AER is essential for normal limb development' [PMID 11101846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11101846/) (abstract); Lu P, Minowada G & Martin GR 2006, Development 133:33-42, 'Increasing Fgf4 expression in the mouse limb bud causes polysyndactyly and rescues the skeletal defects that result from loss of Fgf8 function' [PMID 16308330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16308330/) (abstract)

**Grem1 - remove the BMP antagonist and the mesenchyme kills itself.** Mesenchymal GREMLIN1 antagonises BMP, and that antagonism is what permits a functional AER to form and the SHH/FGF feedback loop to propagate. Without it the epithelial-mesenchymal feedback never gets going and the core limb-bud mesenchyme is eliminated by apoptosis - the same fate as the metanephric mesenchyme in the same mutant.
Sources: Michos O et al. 2004, Development 131:3401-10, 'Gremlin-mediated BMP antagonism induces the epithelial-mesenchymal feedback signaling controlling metanephric kidney and limb organogenesis' [PMID 15201225](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15201225/) (abstract)

**Shh null (and why Gli3 shows this is the wrong route).** Deleting Shh removes distal limb structures, but it also removes the organiser of the notochord, floor plate, neural tube and axial skeleton, giving cyclopia and loss of the spinal column and most ribs. More importantly, the limb part of the phenotype is PERMISSIVE rather than instructive: disrupting one or both Gli3 alleles in a Shh-null background progressively restores distal limb development and digit formation. SHH's limb role is to counteract GLI3 repression, not to build a limb.
Sources: Chiang C et al. 1996, Nature 383:407-13, 'Cyclopia and defective axial patterning in mice lacking Sonic hedgehog gene function' [PMID 8837770](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8837770/) (abstract); te Welscher P et al. 2002, Science 298:827-30, 'Progression of vertebrate limb development through SHH-mediated counteraction of GLI3' [PMID 12215652](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12215652/) (abstract); Buscher D et al. 1997, Mech Dev 62:175-82, 'Evidence for genetic control of Sonic hedgehog by Gli3 in mouse limb development' [PMID 9152009](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9152009/) (abstract); Hill P, Gotz K & Ruther U 2009, Dev Biol 328:506-16, 'A SHH-independent regulation of Gli3 is a significant determinant of anteroposterior patterning of the limb bud' [PMID 19248778](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19248778/) (abstract)

**p63 - limbs absent or truncated because the AER never differentiates.** p63 is expressed in the ectodermal surfaces of the limb buds and is required for the AER to differentiate and to be maintained as a stratified epithelium. The lesion is not induction of the limb field and not mesenchymal signalling: it is the failure of a progenitor epithelium to sustain itself. Mice lacking p63 are BORN ALIVE, which makes this the only amelia-class route on this page that is not immediately lethal for a non-limb reason.
Sources: Mills AA et al. 1999, Nature 398:708-13, 'p63 is a p53 homologue required for limb and epidermal morphogenesis' [PMID 10227293](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10227293/) (abstract); Yang A et al. 1999, Nature 398:714-8, 'p63 is essential for regenerative proliferation in limb, craniofacial and epithelial development' [PMID 10227294](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10227294/) (abstract)

**Hand2 - the posterior prepattern, and the gene cetaceans lost.** HAND2 is a bHLH regulator that establishes anterior and posterior polarity of the nascent limb-bud mesenchyme and sits upstream of SHH. It controls expression of proximal limb genes and sets the Gli3 and Tbx3 expression boundaries that define where the ZPA can form. It functions in dimers with other bHLH factors (TWIST1) in balance with ETS factors, and that balance sets the anterior-posterior pattern.
Sources: Osterwalder M et al. 2014, Dev Cell 31:345-357, 'HAND2 targets define a network of transcriptional regulators that compartmentalize the early limb bud mesenchyme' [PMID 25453830](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25453830/) (abstract); Zhang Z et al. 2010, Development 137:3417-26, 'Preaxial polydactyly: interactions among ETV, TWIST1 and HAND2 control anterior-posterior patterning of the limb' [PMID 20826535](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20826535/) (abstract); Thewissen JGM et al. 2006, PNAS 103:8414-8, 'Developmental basis for hind-limb loss in dolphins and origin of the cetacean bodyplan' [PMID 16717186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16717186/) (abstract)

### Cellular · signalling — 4 routes

Remove or fail to maintain the signalling centres themselves — the apical ectodermal ridge and the zone of polarising activity — or exhaust the progenitor pool.

| Route | Intervention | Predicted phenotype | Evidence | Reversible | Lethality risk | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Cut the AER off at a chosen hour - the original timing experiment** | Microsurgical excision of the AER from a chick limb bud at a staged time point. In mouse this is not practical in utero; the equivalents are the genetic routes (gene-aer-fgf4-fgf8) or explant culture (invitro-limb-bud-explant). | Progressive distal truncation whose level tracks the stage of excision: earlier removal leaves less limb. The classical series is quantitative. | direct, another species | Not reversible once cut, but FGF protein beads substitute for the removed ridge, which is the sufficiency half of the experiment and makes the manipulation effectively two-way in chick. | Low in chick; the manipulation is local. | low in chick (a classical embryology technique), impractical in mouse in utero. |
| **Kill the AER genetically with a cell-autonomous toxin** | An AER-restricted promoter (for example an Msx2 or limb-ectoderm element) driving attenuated DTA, or a Cre driver crossed to a ROSA26-LoxP-DTA reporter. | PROPOSED, NOT OBSERVED. By analogy with AER excision the expectation is distal truncation set by the onset of toxin expression, with the proximal skeleton retained. We found NO published AER DTA ablation in our searches and we would not state a phenotype for it. | proposed | None - toxin-killed cells do not return. The control is over WHEN, not whether. | Depends entirely on promoter leakiness; an attenuated toxin is used precisely to keep ablation local. | medium-high, and the honest difficulty is finding a driver that is on in AER precursors and nowhere that matters. |
| **Remove the ZPA's signal at a chosen stage - digits disappear in a defined order** | Tamoxifen-inducible Cre (ShhCreER) with a conditional Shh allele, dosed at a chosen embryonic day. | Progressive loss of digits, with the number lost set by how early Shh is removed. Not limb loss: the stylopod and zeugopod are not the target. This route tunes AUTOPOD CONTENT, precisely and reproducibly. | direct mouse phenotype | Reversible in the sense that matters for experimental design: the lesion is applied at a time of the experimenter's choosing, not at fertilisation. It is not reversible after the fact. | Low when induction is late enough to miss the early Shh requirements in notochord and neural tube - which is exactly the advantage of the inducible allele over the germline null (compare gene-shh-gli3, which is catastrophic). | low-medium; the alleles are published and tamoxifen dosing is routine. |
| **Tune the limb by changing how fast the morphogen falls apart** | Knock in a stabilised (or, for limb reduction, a destabilised) Fgf10 allele. Stabilised FGF10 variants implanted into chicken limb buds markedly altered limb morphogenesis and inhibited growth of mouse embryonic tibias. | Stabilised direction (measured): suppressed lung branching with increased alveolarisation and epithelial expansion; inhibited embryonic tibia growth; markedly altered limb morphogenesis in chick bud implants. Destabilised direction (PROPOSED, not measured): a graded hypomorphic FGF10 signal, predicted to give partial limb reduction rather than the all-or-nothing amelia of the null. | direct, another species | A knock-in allele is not reversible; but recombinant protein delivery is, and the published work is a bead/implant experiment, which is the acute, dose-controlled version. | Unknown for a destabilised germline allele. If it behaves as a graded Fgf10 hypomorph the lung phenotype scales with it, so the lethality that sinks the Fgf10 null is likely to scale too. | high, and genuinely novel - we found no published destabilised-FGF10 animal. |

**Cut the AER off at a chosen hour - the original timing experiment.** The AER is a physical strip of thickened ectoderm at the distal tip of the bud. Excising it stops distal outgrowth from that moment, and what is already specified proximally still forms. The level of the resulting truncation is therefore a function of WHEN the ridge is removed, which makes this the oldest and most direct demonstration that limb loss is a timing problem rather than only a genetic one.
Sources: Summerbell D 1974, J Embryol Exp Morphol 32:651-60, 'A quantitative analysis of the effect of excision of the AER from the chick limb-bud' [PMID 4463222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/4463222/) (listing); Lewandoski M, Sun X & Martin GR 2000, Nat Genet 26:460-3 [PMID 11101846](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11101846/) (abstract)

**Kill the AER genetically with a cell-autonomous toxin.** Instead of cutting the ridge off, express diphtheria toxin A under a promoter active in AER precursors, so the signalling centre is deleted cell-autonomously and without surgery. This is the standard technique for ablating an organiser in the mouse; it has been used to remove the roof plate from the neural tube and to ablate lens precursors before placode formation, both of which produced the expected loss of the structures that depended on the organiser.
Sources: Lee KJ, Dietrich P & Jessell TM 2000, Nature 403:734-40, 'Genetic ablation reveals that the roof plate is essential for dorsal interneuron specification' [PMID 10693795](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10693795/) (abstract); Zhang Y et al. 2008, Dev Biol 323:53-63, 'Dominant inhibition of lens placode formation in mice' [PMID 18778700](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18778700/) (abstract)

**Remove the ZPA's signal at a chosen stage - digits disappear in a defined order.** SHH from the ZPA does two separable jobs: a very transient early patterning job that assigns digit identity, and an extended growth-promoting job during which the digit precursor mesenchyme expands and is recruited into condensing digit primordia. Deleting Shh progressively earlier with an inducible Cre removes progressively more digits, and the order in which they are lost mirrors an alternating anterior-posterior sequence of normal digit formation.
Sources: Zhu J et al. 2008, Dev Cell 14:624-32, 'Uncoupling Sonic hedgehog control of pattern and expansion of the developing limb bud' [PMID 18410737](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18410737/) (abstract); Scherz PJ et al. 2004, Science 305:396-9, 'The limb bud Shh-Fgf feedback loop is terminated by expansion of former ZPA cells' [PMID 15256670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15256670/) (abstract)

**Tune the limb by changing how fast the morphogen falls apart.** FGF10 is an intrinsically unstable protein that unfolds and is rapidly inactivated at 37 C, and the thermodynamic stability of the 22 mammalian FGFs varies widely. Engineered FGF10 variants with a melting temperature more than 19 C above wild type bind receptors, activate signalling and induce gene expression identically in cell assays, yet behave completely differently in tissue - which implies that protein half-life, not receptor affinity, is part of how the limb reads this signal.
Sources: Czyrek AA et al. 2025, Cell Mol Life Sci 82:167, 'Increased thermal stability of FGF10 leads to ectopic signaling during development' [PMID 40257501](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40257501/) (abstract)

### Tissue · feedback — 3 routes

Break the self-sustaining SHH–GREM1–FGF loop that keeps the bud growing, instead of removing any one of its parts.

| Route | Intervention | Predicted phenotype | Evidence | Reversible | Lethality risk | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Trigger the self-terminating loop early** | Raise FGF activity in the early bud to engage the Fgf/Grem1 inhibitory loop before the limb has grown (a conditional Fgf4 gain-of-function allele exists and is known to raise FGF signalling in the bud); or force premature expansion of the former-ZPA lineage. | PROPOSED for the deliberate-early-termination direction. The components are measured: compound mutants defective in the positive loop show that FGF signalling represses Grem1 in a manner dependent on high FGF activity, in both mouse and chick. Note that the one published high-FGF4 gain-of-function gave postaxial POLYDACTYLY and syndactyly, not truncation - so 'more FGF' is not simply 'earlier stop', and we would not assert the predicted direction. | proposed | In principle fully reversible, because the target is a dynamic circuit state rather than a genomic lesion - but no published experiment turns the loop off and back on. | Low if limb-restricted. | high. The circuit is the thing everyone understands least well in quantitative terms, which is why 'trigger it early' is a sentence and not a protocol. |
| **Detune the digit-patterning Turing network** | Pharmacological or genetic modulation of BMP and WNT in the autopod field, guided by the published model. | Changes in DIGIT PATTERN - number, spacing, thickness. NOT limb loss. This route is in the design space because it is the mechanism that decides what the autopod looks like, and because it is the only route here with a published, runnable, quantitative model attached. Claiming it produces a limbless mouse would be unsupported. | model only | Pharmacological modulation is acutely reversible in explant; genetic modulation is not. | BMP and WNT are systemic; any whole-animal manipulation is dangerous and any explant manipulation is not an animal. | medium in explant, high in vivo. |
| **Use Hox dose to set the wavelength of the digit pattern** | Graded removal of distal Hox genes, on a Gli3-null background, as published. | MORE digits, thinner and more densely packed, as distal Hox dose falls - the opposite of limb reduction. Included because it is the only published, dose-resolved control knob on a limb pattern-forming mechanism, and because it defines what the pattern-level knob actually does. | direct mouse phenotype | None; germline allelic series. | Not retrieved; the compound mutants are described as analysed, not as viable adults, and we will not assert either. | high - this is a large multi-allele breeding programme. |

**Trigger the self-terminating loop early.** Limb outgrowth is driven by a positive feedback loop between Shh, Gremlin1 and AER-FGFs. The loop is not stopped by an external clock: rising FGF activity itself represses Grem1, so the circuit is self-promoting AND self-terminating. Independently, the descendants of former Shh-expressing cells cannot express Gremlin and physically interpose between the Shh source and the Gremlin domain, breaking the loop geometrically. Either mechanism, triggered early, should stop outgrowth early.
Sources: Verheyden JM & Sun X 2008, Nature 454:638-41, 'An Fgf/Gremlin inhibitory feedback loop triggers termination of limb bud outgrowth' [PMID 18594511](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18594511/) (abstract); Scherz PJ et al. 2004, Science 305:396-9 [PMID 15256670](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/15256670/) (abstract); Lu P, Minowada G & Martin GR 2006, Development 133:33-42 [PMID 16308330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16308330/) (abstract)

**Detune the digit-patterning Turing network.** Digits emerge from undifferentiated mesenchyme through a self-organising periodic mechanism. Combined experiment and modelling implicate a Turing network implemented by BMP, SOX9 and WNT, modulated by morphogen gradients; a two-dimensional simulation of this network reproduces wild-type and perturbed Sox9 expression patterns. Changing the network's parameters changes the periodic pattern, and in principle a sufficiently detuned network produces no periodic skeletal pattern at all.
Sources: Raspopovic J, Marcon L, Russo L & Sharpe J 2014, Science 345:566-70, 'Digit patterning is controlled by a Bmp-Sox9-Wnt Turing network modulated by morphogen gradients' [PMID 25082703](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25082703/) (abstract)

**Use Hox dose to set the wavelength of the digit pattern.** Progressive removal of Hoxa13 and Hoxd11-Hoxd13 from a Gli3-null background produces progressively more severe polydactyly with thinner, more densely packed digits. Read through a Turing framework, distal Hox dose modulates the period, or wavelength, of the digit/non-digit pattern. The resemblance of the extreme phenotype to fish fin endoskeleton suggests the pentadactyl state is a modified ancestral version of the same mechanism.
Sources: Sheth R et al. 2012, Science 338:1476-80, 'Hox genes regulate digit patterning by controlling the wavelength of a Turing-type mechanism' [PMID 23239739](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23239739/) (abstract)

### Developmental · timing — 4 routes

Keep the allele and choose when it fires. Conditional, inducible and degron systems move the lesion off fertilisation and away from lethality.

| Route | Intervention | Predicted phenotype | Evidence | Reversible | Lethality risk | Difficulty |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tamoxifen-inducible Cre - choose the hour the gene goes away** | A floxed allele of the target gene crossed to an appropriate CreER driver, with tamoxifen dosed at a chosen embryonic day. | Set by the target and the time. Two measured examples: ShhCreER removal progressively earlier removes progressively more digits; Tbx4 ablation before hindlimb-field expression blocks outgrowth, shortly after onset gives proximal hypoplasia with normal outgrowth and normal Fgf10, and later gives NO limb defect at all. | direct mouse phenotype | The DECISION is reversible up to the moment of dosing; the recombination is not. This is control over onset, not an on/off switch. | This is the mechanism that REMOVES lethality risk, by letting the gene do its early non-limb jobs before being deleted. It is the reason Shh can be studied in the limb at all without cyclopia. | low-medium. Standard, well-characterised, widely available reagents. |
| **Prx1-Cre - confine the lesion to limb-bud mesenchyme** | Cross any floxed allele to Prx1-Cre. For an inducible version, Prx1-CreER. | Target-dependent, but the decisive property is what it AVOIDS: the lung agenesis that kills Fgf10 nulls, the renal agenesis that kills Grem1 nulls, the allantoic failure that kills Tbx4 nulls, and the cardiac defects of Tbx5 nulls are all outside the Prx1 domain. | direct mouse phenotype | None - constitutive Cre. The CreER version adds the time axis. | This is the single most important lethality-reducing reagent in the design space, and the reason a viable limb-reduced mouse is reachable by routes whose germline nulls are not. | low. One of the most widely used drivers in skeletal biology. |
| **Degron knock-in - remove the PROTEIN in hours, not the gene** | Knock a dTAG degron into Fgf10, Tbx5, Shh or Grem1 by zygote editing, then dose the dam with the dTAG ligand at a chosen hour. | PROPOSED for limb genes. What IS measured is the method: in a homozygous FKBP12-F36V knock-in at Nelfb, dTAG depletion was 'non-toxic, rapid, and efficient in embryos from the zygote-to-mid-gestation stages' irrespective of administration route, and acute depletion revealed a stage-specific requirement invisible to a constitutive knockout. No limb gene has been degron-tagged in published work that we found. | proposed | The highest of any route here IN PRINCIPLE - ligand washout should restore protein. Whether a limb bud recovers after a transient depletion is an empirical question nobody has asked, and it is the most interesting unasked question on this page. | Low by design: the protein is present for every requirement outside the chosen window. | high but decreasing. The honest caveats are in the second paper: tags sometimes cause phenotypes on their own, efficiency varies by protein and route, and one of the two tagging systems showed no in vivo activity at all. |
| **RECOMMENDED: limb-restricted, stage-selected Fgf10 removal** | Fgf10 conditional (floxed) allele crossed to Prx1-CreER. Tamoxifen at a chosen day. Forelimb-restricted and hindlimb-restricted variants can be approached instead through Tbx5-conditional (forelimb) or Tbx4-conditional (hindlimb) alleles if a single-pair phenotype is wanted. | PROPOSED. We did not find a published Prx1-CreER;Fgf10-flox limb series and will not state its phenotype. The components are each measured: Fgf10 loss abolishes AER formation and gives absent limbs; Prx1-Cre recombines all limb mesenchyme by E10.5 and never the ectoderm; CreER timing converts a fixed lesion into a stage series. The honest claim is that this is the best-supported DESIGN, not a known result. | proposed | Onset is chosen; the recombination is permanent. For true reversibility the degron route is the only candidate. | LOW, and this is the entire point. The germline Fgf10 null is perinatally lethal from lung agenesis; a Prx1-restricted deletion should not touch the lung. We say 'should' because we found no published viability data for this exact cross. | medium. Every component is an existing, published reagent; the work is breeding, dosing and skeletal analysis, not invention. |

**Tamoxifen-inducible Cre - choose the hour the gene goes away.** A CreER fusion sits inactive in the cytoplasm until tamoxifen is given, at which point it enters the nucleus and recombines a floxed allele. The same genetic lesion applied at different embryonic days produces different phenotypes, because developmental requirements are windowed. This converts a binary genotype into a one-dimensional experimental variable.
Sources: Zhu J et al. 2008, Dev Cell 14:624-32 [PMID 18410737](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18410737/) (abstract); Naiche LA & Papaioannou VE 2007, Development 134:93-103 [PMID 17164415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17164415/) (abstract)

**Prx1-Cre - confine the lesion to limb-bud mesenchyme.** A Prx1 (Prrx1) limb enhancer drives Cre throughout early limb-bud mesenchyme, with recombination first detectable at E9.5 and essentially complete in all limb mesenchymal cells by E10.5, plus a subset of craniofacial mesenchyme. Cre is NEVER detected in limb-bud ectoderm. So a floxed gene can be removed from the limb mesenchyme and left intact in lung, kidney, heart and allantois.
Sources: Logan M et al. 2002, Genesis 33:77-80, 'Expression of Cre Recombinase in the developing mouse limb bud driven by a Prxl enhancer' [PMID 12112875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12112875/) (abstract)

**Degron knock-in - remove the PROTEIN in hours, not the gene.** A degron tag (FKBP12-F36V / dTAG) knocked into the endogenous locus leaves the protein normal until a small molecule is given, at which point the ligase machinery degrades it within hours. Unlike Cre, the depletion is post-translational, far faster than protein turnover after gene deletion, and in some systems washable. This is the only route on this page that could in principle switch a limb signal OFF AND BACK ON inside a single embryonic day.
Sources: Abuhashem A, Lee AS, Joyner AL & Hadjantonakis AK 2022, Dev Cell 57:1068-1080.e6, 'Rapid and efficient degradation of endogenous proteins in vivo identifies stage-specific roles of RNA Pol II pausing in mammalian development' [PMID 35421370](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35421370/) (abstract); Antczak W et al. 2026, Commun Biol 9(1), 'Degron models: a toolbox for rapid in vivo depletion of essential proteins regulating mRNA metabolism' [PMID 41851445](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41851445/) (abstract)

**RECOMMENDED: limb-restricted, stage-selected Fgf10 removal.** Take the lesion with the strongest, most unambiguous limb phenotype in the literature - loss of FGF10, which abolishes AER formation and so abolishes the limb - and apply it only where and only when you want it. Prx1-CreER restricts recombination to limb-bud mesenchyme, where FGF10 is actually made, and the tamoxifen dose sets the hour. Everything that kills the germline Fgf10 null (lung, thyroid, pituitary, salivary gland) lies outside the Prx1 domain and outside the induction window.
Sources: Min H et al. 1998, Genes Dev 12:3156-61 [PMID 9784490](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9784490/) (abstract); Logan M et al. 2002, Genesis 33:77-80 [PMID 12112875](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12112875/) (abstract); Naiche LA & Papaioannou VE 2007, Development 134:93-103 [PMID 17164415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17164415/) (abstract)

### Organism · comparative — 4 routes

Nature's own limbless vertebrates — snakes, limb-reduced skinks, cetaceans — reached it repeatedly and not by the same mechanism each time.

**Snakes - progressive enhancer decay, and a bud that is still there.** Snake limb loss is not one event. In basal snakes (boa, python) the ZRS is ~80% identical to limbed lizards and still has detectable ZPA activity in boa; in advanced snakes the substitution rate jumps and ZPA activity is gone; in corn snake no ZRS sequence was detected at all. Crucially the python hindlimb bud still initiates, and its HOXD digit enhancers are conserved - the bud reaches a distal phase with transient pre-chondrogenic condensations of tibia, fibula and footplate before arresting.

**Limb-reduced lizards - the knob is how LONG SHH is on, and the losses are not all the same.** In the Australian skink genus Hemiergis, closely related populations differ in digit number, and evolutionary digit loss is NOT a simple truncation of a pentadactyl programme. The duration of embryonic SHH expression is shortened in limbs with fewer digits, correlating with decreased posterior cell proliferation. Expression of MSX and Distal-less proteins is similar between populations, so the change is specific rather than a general developmental slowdown.
Sources: Shapiro MD, Hanken J & Rosenthal N 2003, J Exp Zool B 297:48-56, 'Developmental basis of evolutionary digit loss in the Australian lizard Hemiergis' [PMID 12955843](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/12955843/) (abstract); Wagner GP et al. 2018, J Morphol 279:1104-1119, 'Are there general laws for digit evolution in squamates? **Whales and dolphins - the bud forms, the AER forms, and then nothing maintains it.** Cetacean embryos DO initiate hindlimb buds; in dolphins the bud arrests and degenerates around the fifth gestational week. The hindlimb bud forms an AER and expresses Fgf8 initially, but neither the AER nor Fgf8 expression is maintained. SHH is absent from the bud, and the failure to establish a ZPA is associated with absence of HAND2, an upstream regulator of SHH. Separately, cetacean ZRS reporter activity is significantly lower than mouse, consistent with cessation of hindlimb-bud SHH.
Sources: Thewissen JGM et al. 2006, PNAS 103:8414-8 [PMID 16717186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16717186/) (abstract); Sun L et al. 2022, BMC Genomics 23:797, 'Evolutionary genetics of flipper forelimb and hindlimb loss from limb development-related genes in cetaceans' [PMID 36460960](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36460960/) (abstract); Gavazzi LM et al. 2024, Dev Dyn 253:859-874, 'Protein signaling and morphological development of the tail fluke in the embryonic beluga whale (Delphinapterus leucas)' [PMID 38494595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38494595/) (abstract)

**Stickleback pelvic loss - the cleanest natural proof that cis-regulation is the viable route.** Threespine stickleback populations have repeatedly lost the pelvic apparatus, the hindlimb homologue, through deletion of a tissue-specific Pitx1 enhancer. The same gene's null allele is lethal in laboratory animals. So evolution reached the phenotype by removing the gene's expression in one tissue rather than removing the gene - the same logic as the ZRS, in an independent lineage and a different gene.
Sources: Chan YF et al. 2010, Science 327:302-5 [PMID 20007865](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20007865/) (abstract)

## What we ran

Three studies, each a **simulation or a sequence analysis**. None is an observation, none is calibrated to a measured mouse limb bud, and no part of any of them was run in an animal.

### The ZRS across species: what changes when a limb enhancer is swapped

*sequence analysis · molecular scale*

**The design question it answers.** Kvon et al. replaced the mouse ZRS with orthologs and got normal limbs from some species and truncated limbs from others. What, in the sequence itself, separates the two groups?

Real ZRS orthologs fetched from NCBI, aligned here, with per-column conservation and ETS-motif positions. The contrast of interest is between the orthologs that gave normal limbs in the published knock-in and the one that truncated them. This is a correlational reading of a handful of sequences; it is not a predictor of enhancer activity and is not used as one anywhere on this page.

- Figure `zrs-conservation.png`: Sequence analysis. Per-column conservation across the ZRS orthologs retrieved, with ETS motif positions marked.

- This is a correlational, post-hoc analysis of a handful of sequences. It is NOT a predictor. With two knock-in species that gave normal limbs and two that truncated, no statistic computed here has the power to establish that any sequence feature causes the phenotype; the causal evidence is Kvon et al.'s mouse knock-ins, not this alignment.
- The sequences analysed are NOT the sequences Kvon et al. knocked in. Theirs were synthesised from supplementary Tables S2/S3, which could not be retrieved. These were re-derived from reference genomes, so they may differ from the constructs by assembly errors, strain or individual polymorphism, and by window boundaries.
- Species substitutions were necessary and change what the comparison means: the paper used boa constrictor (Assemblathon 2 assembly, not in NCBI) and speckled rattlesnake Crotalus mitchellii (JPMF01); this analysis uses Eryx miliaris (dwarf sand boa, a different basal snake, from a dedicated GenBank ZRS record) and Crotalus tigris. Thamnophis elegans was added and is not in the paper at all. Viper (JTGP01) was not analysed.
- Percent identity depends heavily on how much flanking sequence is included, and there is no single correct number. Mouse-human is 88.3% over the trimmed core, 78.6% over the full 1.3 kb window, and 85-86% over the best-aligning block alone. Kvon et al. state 73% for human vs mouse; they do not define the metric, so these numbers are NOT directly comparable to theirs and the discrepancy is a definitional difference, not a contradiction of either result.
- The core boundaries (mouse 398-1043) come from thresholds on a smoothed conservation profile. The thresholds were chosen by reading the profile's shoulder, but they are choices, and the identity numbers shift if they move.
- ETS site numbering E0-E4 from Kvon et al. is NOT reproduced. Their site coordinates were in inaccessible supplementary material, so no site here is claimed to be 'the' E1, E2 etc. Every motif call is marked inferred. The one exception in strength of evidence is the 17 bp deletion block, which was located independently by alignment and which the paper also describes as overlapping an ETS site.
- A PWM hit is a sequence match, not a measurement of binding. No ChIP, EMSA or reporter data were used here, and FIMO itself was not run - an equivalent threshold was recomputed.
- The corn snake negative ('no ZRS detected') is scoped: the whole annotated LMBR1 locus plus 10 kb either side was searched with 12-mer seeding and local alignment using BOTH the mouse and the Burmese python ZRS as queries. It does not exclude a ZRS remnant elsewhere in the corn snake genome, nor one too diverged for these queries to find.
- Rattlesnake could not be assessed at the 17 bp site: MAFFT places unrelated sequence in those columns rather than a gap, so it is reported as not assessable there rather than as retaining the site. Its ETS1 scan returns no hit in that block.
- Alignment of sequences this diverged is itself uncertain. Different aligners or parameters will move indel placement, and the snake columns are where that uncertainty is largest.
- Model fidelity is not biological validity: reproducing Kvon et al.'s observations says these sequence relationships are real, not that any interpretation of limb loss built on them is.

### The SHH–GREM1–FGF loop: where a weakened enhancer stops being a weaker limb and starts being no limb

*simulation (ODE) · tissue scale*

**The design question it answers.** The limb bud is held open by a feedback loop. If a ZRS allele lowers ZPA Shh output, is the result proportionally smaller, or is there a threshold below which the loop never engages at all?

An ODE model of the SHH–GREM1–BMP–FGF feedback loop, built from the published topology with every rate constant marked sourced or assumed. The wild-type control must initiate, sustain and self-terminate before any sweep is read. Three knobs are then swept — ZPA Shh output, AER-inducing FGF10 input, and GREM1 production — because three different routes on this page act on three different parts of the same loop.

- Figure `shh-loop-control.png`: Simulation, control arm. Wild-type parameters: the loop engages, is sustained for a bounded window, and self-terminates.
- Figure `shh-loop-threshold.png`: Simulation. Outgrowth duration against the fraction of wild-type ZPA Shh output. The threshold is a property of this model, not a measurement in an embryo.
- Figure `shh-loop-phasemap.png`: Simulation. Two knobs at once: the same phenotype class is reachable from different directions, which is the point of a design space.

23 parameters, of which 21 are assumed rather than sourced.

- This is a SIMULATION. No quantity in this file is an observation of any embryo.
- The model is a caricature of a signalling TOPOLOGY. It is not calibrated to any measured mouse limb bud: no concentration, rate, diffusion length or developmental time in it was fitted to data, and none should be read as one.
- 21 of the 23 parameters are assumed and none is sourced; the other 2 are fixed by the non-dimensionalisation. They were chosen so the wild-type arm initiates, is maintained and self-terminates -- that is, the control arm's qualitative behaviour was an input to parameter choice, not an independent result.
- A threshold in this model is a threshold in this model. It is not a prediction of the ZRS allelic series at which a mouse loses a limb, and it has no units that could be compared to one.
- The model is non-spatial: SHH, GREM1, BMP and FGF are single well-mixed levels. The real loop is spatially organised across an epithelial-mesenchymal boundary with measured gradients, and the Scherz 2004 mechanism it borrows is explicitly geometric (an expanding descendant zone). Variable Z is a zero-dimensional stand-in for that geometry.
- The model contains no SHH-independent proximal outgrowth, so it cannot distinguish a Shh-null limb -- which in mice still forms proximal skeleton -- from true amelia. Its 'no-limb' class means only 'this loop never engaged'.
- Digit number and digit pattern are outside the model. Sheth et al. 2012 (PMID 23239739) show digit patterning is a Turing-type mechanism whose wavelength distal Hox genes set; none of that is represented here, so the model says nothing about how many digits form.
- The knob s is held constant in time. In python hindlimb buds the reported defect is that SHH is weak AND transient (PMID 27773569); a time-varying s is not explored.
- Only the classifier's boundaries, not the model, decide where 'truncated' ends and 'normal' begins. Those cuts (5%, 85%, 115% of the wild-type control) are assumed.
- The outgrowth window duration is most sensitive to k_Z, a wholly assumed parameter. No sensitivity analysis over the full parameter space was run.
- The two published termination mechanisms are not equal partners in this parameterisation. The ablation arm shows the loop leans almost entirely on the Scherz 2004 descendant-zone ratchet: without it the loop never terminates, while without the Verheyden & Sun 2008 FGF-|Grem1 repression it still terminates, about 4% later. So the self-terminating behaviour shown in the control figure is mostly one mechanism, and which mechanism dominates in a real limb bud is not addressed here.

**The design question it answers.** If a truncated bud is a smaller field, how small does the field have to get before it patterns no complete skeletal element at all?

A two-dimensional BMP–SOX9–WNT reaction–diffusion model on a limb-bud-shaped domain, with its own linear-stability analysis as an internal control. Element counts come from a pre-declared objective criterion, not from looking at the pictures, and the spread across random seeds is reported because Turing patterns are seed-dependent.

- Figure `digit-turing-normal.png`: Simulation. The pattern the model makes in a full-size paddle.
- Figure `digit-turing-series.png`: Simulation. The same model across the sweep, from several elements down to none.
- Figure `digit-turing-domain.png`: Simulation. Element count against domain size, in units of the pattern's own wavelength.
- Figure `digit-turing.mp4`: Simulation. The pattern forming in the full-size paddle, from the seeded noise it starts in. No sound.

20 parameters, of which 9 are assumed rather than sourced.

- NO GROWTH. The real limb bud grows while it patterns, and raspopovic2014's and onimaru2016's simulations both run on growing meshes. On a static domain the elements form as condensations in a distal band rather than elongating into proximodistally running digit rays. This study measures element NUMBER, not element SHAPE, and the shapes here should not be read as digit morphology.

- The mouse parameter set of raspopovic2014 was not obtainable. The numbers used come from onimaru2016's CATSHARK FIN simulation of the same network, and one of them (alpha_W) had to be changed to obtain a Turing instability at all.

- The linear-stability wavelength and the simulated wavelength differ by a factor of about two (see measured_wavelength). The simulated value is the one used for the critical domain size.
- The classification into phenotype classes is a threshold rule on a simulated scalar field. It is objective and pre-declared, but 'normal digit number' here means 'five counted components in this simulation', not a mouse phenotype.
- Only one noise model, one domain shape, and one gradient shape were explored. Turing element counts are sensitive to all three.

## The chain across scales, and where it is not calibrated

The three models on this page are not independent demonstrations. They are meant to be read as one chain, and the chain has two junctions we refuse to call calibrated.

1. **A ZRS allele → ZPA Shh output** — *scenario*. The sequence analysis measures divergence and motif content. It does not predict enhancer activity. Mapping an allele onto a fraction of wild-type Shh output is a scenario parameter, not a calibrated transfer.
2. **ZPA Shh output → Outgrowth duration** — *model*. This junction is inside the SHH–GREM1–FGF model, so it is at least explicit: the model says how a reduction in Shh production shortens the window in which FGF stays above threshold.
3. **Outgrowth duration → Limb-bud domain size** — *scenario*. A shorter outgrowth window means a smaller paddle, but the model has no geometry. Converting one to the other needs a growth law we have not fitted.
4. **Domain size → Skeletal element count** — *model*. This junction is inside the Turing model, which gives a critical domain size in units of its own pattern wavelength.

> Two of the four junctions are within a single model and are explicit. Two cross between models and have no calibration behind them, so the end-to-end statement — “this allele gives that many digits” — is refused. The chain is a map of what would have to be measured, not a prediction.

## Animal welfare and the 3Rs

**Framing.**

A limbless mouse is a severe phenotype. This section is here because a reviewer will expect it and because its absence would be a hole in the page, not as an apology for the science. The relevant question for a licence or IACUC review is not whether the phenotype is severe - it is - but whether the severity is necessary for the question, and whether a less severe route answers it.

**Severity classification.**

- **Point:** European severity classification under Directive 2010/63/EU applies to the BREEDING AND MAINTENANCE of genetically altered lines likely to develop a harmful phenotype, not only to experimental procedures. A line that is limbless at birth is authorised as a harmful-phenotype line before any experiment is performed.
- **Evidence pmid:** 28696160
- **Evidence cite:** Zintzsch A et al. 2017, Lab Anim 51:573-582, 'Guidelines on severity assessment and classification of genetically altered mouse and rat lines'
- **Evidence doi:** 10.1177/0023677217718863
- **Evidence finding:** 'Genetic alterations can unpredictably compromise the wellbeing of animals ... the breeding and maintenance of genetically altered (GA) animals which are likely to develop a harmful phenotype has to be authorized.' The Working Group of Berlin Animal Welfare Officers provides a list of symptoms classified as non-harmful, mild, moderate or severe, with monitoring and refinement strategies and specific housing requirements.
- **Read level:** abstract

**Refinements in order of how much they reduce harm.**

- Do not let the animal be born. Analyse at E18.5 by skeletal preparation, at E11.5 by reporter assay, at E10.5 by in-situ hybridisation - which is precisely the protocol Kvon et al. used for everything except the two-week viability photograph. The entire ZRS allelic series was characterised on embryos. **Cost:** You learn nothing about postnatal viability, locomotion, growth or welfare - which is exactly the question a 'can you make a limbless mouse' claim turns on.
- Use a tissue-restricted conditional (Prx1-Cre) so the lesion does not also remove lungs or kidneys. This both improves the science and removes a large, certain source of suffering that is not even the phenotype of interest. **Cost:** Breeding complexity; a craniofacial domain in the driver that must be checked.
- Use an inducible allele so the lesion is applied in the window that matters. A gene removed after its requirement has passed gives no phenotype at all - Naiche and Papaioannou showed this directly for Tbx4 - so stage selection is a way of asking a sharper question with fewer affected animals. **Cost:** Tamoxifen effects on pregnancy require vehicle controls.
- Take one pair rather than four. A forelimb-only or hindlimb-only animal retains locomotion and is a materially less severe phenotype than tetra-amelia, and for most mechanistic questions it answers the same thing. **Cost:** None scientifically, for questions about one limb's network.
- Replace with ex vivo limb-bud organ culture, where signalling can be manipulated pharmacologically and lineages tracked longitudinally. Tbx4-null hindlimb buds fail to develop in vitro as they do in vivo, so explants reproduce at least some genetic lesions faithfully. **Cost:** Animals are still used to source the buds, so this is a refinement and a partial reduction, NOT a replacement, and calling it a replacement would overstate it.
- Replace with budoids - mESC-derived organoids containing AER-like, surface-ectoderm and limb-mesoderm cells, undergoing chondrogenesis-mediated symmetry breaking, at >1,000 budoids per differentiation - or with human iPSC-derived PRRX1+ limb-bud mesenchyme. These are true replacements and they are scalable. **Cost:** The readouts are cell fate, polarisation and cartilage, not a skeleton and not an animal. No dish currently makes a limb; the Underhill/Arostegui methods chapter said in 2021 that no organoid system effectively modelled limb-development complexity, and budoids (2025) are a real advance on that but not a refutation of it.

**What a licence or iacuc review would turn on.**

- Is the severe phenotype necessary for the question, or would a one-pair, conditional, or embryonic-only version answer it? For most mechanistic questions the answer is that it would.
- Is a non-animal route available for this specific readout? For cell-fate and signalling questions in 2026, increasingly yes; for skeletal and postnatal questions, no.
- What is the humane endpoint and who decides it? A limbless neonate cannot right itself, reach a nipple or thermoregulate normally.
- What are the housing requirements? The Berlin guidelines pair severity classification with specific housing and monitoring requirements rather than treating classification as a paperwork exercise.
- How many animals does the breeding scheme produce for each informative one? A homozygous conditional on two floxed alleles plus a driver is a demanding cross, and reduction is a breeding-design problem as much as an experimental one.
- Precedent exists and is documented: the Kvon et al. transgenic and knock-in work was reviewed and approved by the Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory Animal Welfare and Research Committee, mice were monitored daily and inspected weekly by the committee chair, the head of the facility and veterinary staff, and the facility is AAALAC-accredited. Embryonic samples for in-situ and skeletal preparation were dissected blind to genotype, and knock-in and knock-out experiments used a matched-littermate design.

**Our position.**

We have no animal facility, no IACUC protocol and no wet-lab arm. Nothing on this page was performed by us and no organism, embryo or cell was made by us. The welfare discussion is here because the design space we are describing is one somebody else would have to execute, and a design space presented without its costs is not a design space.

## What this page does not claim

- No mouse was made, no embryo was used, and no wet-lab experiment was run for this page.
- The three computational studies are simulations and a sequence analysis. None is an observation, and none is calibrated to a measured mouse limb bud.
- No route on this page is a protocol. Each names published evidence and what would have to be done; none has been attempted here.
- The sequence analysis is correlational over a handful of orthologs. It is not a predictor of enhancer activity and must not be used as one.
- A threshold in a model is not a threshold in an embryo.
- We ran no experiments. Every route on this page is a reading of someone else's published work, or an explicitly labelled proposal. Nothing here is a result of ours.
- ONE source was read at full text (Kvon et al. 2016, via PMC5484524, including figure legends and STAR Methods). Everything else is read at ABSTRACT level, with one exception noted below. No claim on this page is written in the voice of having read a body we did not read.
- Summerbell 1974 (PMID 4463222) is cited at LISTING level: the PubMed record resolves and its title and MeSH terms confirm a quantitative AER-excision series in chick, but PubMed holds no abstract for it, so we have read no result statement from that paper and make no quantitative claim from it. The classical premise is restated independently by Lewandoski et al. 2000.
- Garavelli et al. 2010 (PMID 21271649, WNT7A / Al-Awadi-Raas-Rothschild) also has no abstract in PubMed. We confirm the syndrome-gene association from the title and MeSH terms and state nothing further about the phenotype.
- Every PMID on this page was retrieved from PubMed and resolved to a record during this session; none is quoted from memory or from a secondary summary. DOIs are as returned by PubMed.
- SCOPE OF NEGATIVE SEARCHES. All searches were PubMed only, in English, via the pubmed MCP tools. We did not search bioRxiv, medRxiv, patents, theses or non-English sources, and PubMed's query parser ANDs all terms, so a long query returns fewer results than a short one - several of our early searches returned zero for that reason and were repeated with shorter terms. Specific negatives: (1) no molecular mechanism for Lerista limb reduction was found ('Lerista limb reduction skink evolution' returned 4 records, all taxonomic, morphometric or macroevolutionary); (2) no published genetic AER ablation with diphtheria toxin was found ('diphtheria toxin ablation apical ectodermal ridge genetic mouse' returned 4 records, none of them an AER ablation); (3) no auxin-inducible degron study in mouse embryos was found, so the degron route cites dTAG/FKBP work instead; (4) no limb field was found reported in gastruloids or trunk-like structures, searched on PubMed only, which is the wrong place to look first for a 2026 result; (5) no published Prx1-CreER;Fgf10-flox limb series was found, which is why our own recommendation is labelled 'proposed'; (6) no recombinant-limb experiment using mesenchyme from a defined limb-loss genotype was found. Each of these is an absence in OUR search, not an absence in the literature.
- Phenotype statements are quoted or closely paraphrased from the abstracts. Where a paper's own authors flag an inference rather than a measurement - Thewissen et al. on HAND2 association, Sun et al. on cetacean ZRS activity, the 41 Ma and 34 Ma dates - we mark it as theirs and do not restate it as a measurement.
- The two published human-to-mouse discordances on this page (Ushiki on CTCF/acheiropodia; Niemann on WNT3 viability) are a standing warning that cross-species transfer of a cis-regulatory or viability claim is unsafe, and we did not systematically check every other route for the same risk.
- Two founding Fgf10 papers DISAGREE about whether the limb bud initiates: Min et al. 1998 say initiation was abolished, Sekine et al. 1999 say bud formation initiated but outgrowth did not occur. We preserve the disagreement rather than reconciling it.
- Route counts are not evidence counts. 'evidence_strength: proposed' appears on four routes and means exactly what it says: nobody has done it. A design space is not a list of results.
- We have never scored a limb enhancer, have no registered calibration anywhere in our system, and have performed no functional validation of any sequence-model output we have produced. Any claim about predicting limb phenotype from sequence is a hypothesis and is labelled as one.

## Why this is on a design platform and not in a review article

The useful thing here is not the list — a specialist could write the list. It is that every row carries its evidence level, every junction between scales carries a refusal or a label, and the three models can be re-run from the repository. The question “can you make a limbless mouse?” has a dozen answers whose differences only show up when you are forced to say which phenotype, at which scale, with what evidence, and at what cost. That is what this workspace is for.
