# 14. Living machines that do work: the design space

Generated by `scripts/designspace_doc.py` from the same files the live page reads, so the two cannot drift.
The interactive version, with the figures and the filterable route list, is at <https://studio.morphoswarm.ai/#/studio/xenobot>.

**The question.** Every headline result for computationally designed organisms is about the construct moving itself, or copying itself. The question that decides whether any of it becomes a product is different: can a millimetre-scale living machine do useful mechanical work on something else — push it, carry it, corral it? This page maps every route we can source, and begins by auditing the simulation we ourselves published on the subject.

## Yes — but not by designing a gait.

The obvious way to make a living machine move something is to design how it moves: pick a body, pick a pattern of contraction, optimise. That is what the computationally designed organism literature does, and it is what our own earlier artifact in this repository did, comparing six actuation patterns on a soft voxel body. Extending it toward cargo was supposed to be this page. Auditing it became this page instead.

Write the drive as a sine with a per-voxel phase and it decomposes into sin(ωt)·cos(φ) plus cos(ωt)·sin(φ). The body's shape then traces a closed curve in the span of two vectors, and that curve is retraced rather than enclosed — reciprocal — exactly when the 2×N matrix has rank one. Reading the phases out of our own driver source: five of the six patterns assign only the phases 0 and π, so sin(φ) is identically zero, the rank is one, and the whole body is driven by a single scalar in time. At the Reynolds number a millimetre-scale construct actually occupies, below 0.1, Purcell's scallop theorem makes their net displacement exactly zero. The ranking's second and third places are not merely demoted; they are dead.

The second and third checks did not rescue it. On a refinement series built to hold the physical actuation plane fixed while the lattice refines — the control the original study lacked — the model does not converge: 41% and 54% spread across a 64-fold increase in voxels, both series turning over rather than settling, with spatial error dominating temporal by seventeen-fold. And the non-monotone body-size swing an independent audit had flagged turned out to be neither a resonance nor a numerical artifact but a single line of integer division in the encoding, which splits the actuators evenly at even body lengths and unevenly at odd ones.

The strongest evidence against that physics is not ours. Kriegman and colleagues simulated their first-generation designs on land, reported that it "resulted in highly performant but nontransferable designs" airborne for 47% of the gait cycle while the real organisms kept ventral contact with the dish, and fixed it by adding buoyancy and a drag force on every surface voxel face — raising ground contact from 52.7% to 93.3%. Our artifact uses the physics they abandoned, and they published the abandonment.

So what does work? The route this page recommends does not control a gait at all. Sculpting the construct into an asymmetric semitorus with a narrow mouth lets locomotion become gathering through geometry: the shape does the work, the construct only has to move. It is the one body plan here that was computationally designed, physically built, and then measured on external material moved rather than on self-locomotion. And the honest commercial position sits beside it on the page — for most mechanical tasks at this scale a magnetically or acoustically driven synthetic device wins today on steering, force, speed and reliability.

> Withdrawing the ranking is not withdrawing the artifact. The scallop theorem governs a free swimmer; a construct crawling on a dish bottom is rectifying against a surface and is outside its assumptions, and most published constructs are cilia-driven, where there is no body gait to rank at all. The claim is bounded: for a free-swimming or near-neutrally-buoyant construct the ranking is void and four of six patterns are exactly dead.

## If you asked us to actually do it

**The route.** Give the body a C-shaped mouth that captures loose material (`body-semitorus-mouth`)

**Why this one.**

It is the only body plan in this whole space that has been computationally designed, physically built, and then MEASURED on external material moved rather than on self-locomotion. Sculpting progenitors into an asymmetric semitorus with a narrow mouth raised offspring diameter by 149% and replication rounds by 250% over spherical progenitors, because the mouth captures, transports and aggregates loose cells. It is also cilia-driven, so it never has to solve the actuation problem that kills most of this page's alternatives.

**What it buys you that a gait does not.**

A concave mouth converts locomotion into gathering through GEOMETRY. It needs no per-region phase control, no actuator timing, and no contractile tissue graft — none of which exist in a controllable form in any living construct today. The construct just has to move; the shape does the work.

**The gait question is the wrong question, and that is this page's main finding.**

The published six-gait comparison asks which spatial actuation phase pattern moves a soft body furthest. In the regime a real millimetre-scale construct occupies — Reynolds number below 0.1 — five of those six patterns produce EXACTLY ZERO net displacement, because they assign only the phases 0 and pi and so retrace their own stroke. The live design question is not 'which gait ranks highest' but 'is this gait non-reciprocal at all', which is a yes/no test you can run on paper in a minute.

**What to build next, in order.**

1. Replicate the semitorus and measure particle clearance with a declared metric, not as a side effect. 2. Apply the rank test to any proposed actuation pattern before simulating it. 3. If you simulate, put buoyancy and drag in — Kriegman et al. already found and fixed this failure once, and the artifact we re-examined uses the physics they abandoned. 4. Evolve the ARENA (coll-evolve-the-terrain): it is the cheapest unexploited lever here and needs no new biology.

**The honest commercial position.**

For most mechanical tasks at this scale a magnetically or acoustically driven synthetic device wins today on steering, force, speed and reliability, and a synthetic swarm has already been shown capturing bacteria and microplastics on the fly with a retrieval story. The living construct's case rests on four narrow axes and should be argued only on those: self-repair, biodegradation and a yolk-limited lifespan as built-in containment, patient-derived immunological self (anthrobots), and kinematic self-replication. Nothing else on the comparator scale offers any of them.

**Runner-up.**

cell-ciliabot-building-blocks — placing cilia-inactive modules to pattern thrust by construction is gait design done with tissue instead of with timing, and it is the most manufacturable idea on this page.

## The design space: 49 routes

Every row is a published perturbation or a bounded proposal, with its own evidence level. **None of them has been attempted here.** Sources carry read levels: a listing is not a reading.

### Unplaced — 49

These routes name a scale the page does not declare (application, body, cell, collective, comparator, design-loop, fluid, lifecycle, molecular, tissue). They are listed rather than dropped.

- **Suppress multiciliated-cell fate to remove ciliary propulsion** (molecular): A construct that does NOT move itself by cilia. It becomes a passive body whose only motion is whatever an added contractile tissue produces — which is what makes a clean test of a designed gait possible at all.
- **Raise or lower cilia density through the MCIDAS/GemC1 program** (molecular): A construct with a tunable number of propulsive units per unit area — more cilia means more thrust per unit surface, which is the only throttle a cilia-driven biobot has. It still only moves ITSELF unless a shape is imposed that also sweeps material.
- **Set the direction cilia beat by biasing planar cell polarity** (molecular): A construct whose surface thrust points in a CHOSEN direction instead of the direction the tissue happens to settle on. This is the closest biological analogue of choosing a gait, and unlike a contractile gait it needs no actuator pattern at all.
- **Add Xenopus cardiac progenitor tissue as a contractile actuator** (molecular): A construct that contracts rhythmically and crawls on the dish floor by pushing against it. This is the only published xenobot actuator that works by BODY DEFORMATION, and therefore the only one the voxel-actuation gait model is even about.
- **Build from Xenopus laevis animal-cap explant (the baseline)** (cell): A ~3,000-cell ciliated spheroid that swims itself along helical trajectories for 10-14 days on maternally provided yolk, with no feeding. It moves itself; it moves external material only as a side effect.
- **Build from adult human airway epithelial cells (anthrobots)** (cell): A 30-500 um ciliated spheroid that self-propels at 5-50 um/s with motility patterns from tight loops to straight lines. It moves itself. Critically for any clinical use it is PATIENT-DERIVED, so it is immunologically self.
- **Choose ciliary propulsion over engineered contraction** (cell): A construct that moves itself with no engineered actuator at all, by surface cilia that arise from normal tissue patterning. It is far easier to make and it has NO gait to design — which also means no gait to optimise for pushing.
- **Use cilia-inactive modules to place thrust where you want it** (cell): A construct whose ACTIVE SURFACE is patterned by construction: thrust where you put working modules, none where you put mutant ones. This is gait design done with tissue rather than with timing, and it needs no actuator control at all.
- **Accept that cells freed from the embryo change what they express** (cell): A construct whose cells are NOT simply epidermis: at least 537 non-epidermal transcripts are upregulated relative to age-matched embryos, and inter-individual variability is significantly larger. Any mechanical parameter measured on embryonic tissue is therefore a guess about construct tissue.
- **Impose a shape by microsurgery and cautery** (tissue): A construct with an arbitrary non-spherical body plan that is stable for the rest of its life. This is the ONLY published way to put a designed geometry into a xenobot, and geometry is what the whole design pipeline optimises.
- **Respect the 100 um minimum concavity, or the shape closes** (tissue): A construct whose designed features SURVIVE. Below that width, aggregating stem cells close the gap by tissue contraction and the feature you designed simply is not there in the built object.
- **Build the shape by aggregating spheroids instead of cutting one** (tissue): A construct with a chosen geometry built ADDITIVELY, keeping surface cilia density and inter-module boundary fidelity. Multi-module constructs were reported to move more than single ones.
- **Rely on self-repair rather than on redundancy** (tissue): A construct that closes its own lacerations and resumes moving. A machine that repairs itself does not need the design margin a synthetic microrobot needs, which is a genuine advantage over every comparator on this page.
- **Give the body a C-shaped mouth that captures loose material** (body): A construct that GATHERS external particles rather than merely bumping them: dissociated cells are captured, transported and aggregated in the mouth. Offspring diameter rose 149% and replication rounds rose 250% (mean 1.2+-0.4 to 3+-0.8, max 2 to max 4) against spherical progenitors.
- **Exapt a drag-reducing hole into a cargo pouch** (body): In SIMULATION, a construct that carries a discrete object. The pouch topology was realised in vivo, but that built organism was never layered with contractile tissue, so no physical xenobot has ever carried a defined cargo.
- **Drive a contractile body with a travelling phase wave rather than a split** (body): A construct that moves itself AND, uniquely among the six published patterns, keeps moving in the viscous regime a real construct lives in. In our dry-friction reproduction it is the best self-mover (0.364 body lengths in 5 cycles) and the best pusher (transport ratio 0.95 of its own path transferred to the cargo).
- **Split the body into two antiphase halves (the published longitudinal gait)** (body): On a dry floor, a construct that crawls: 0.153 body lengths in 5 cycles, second of six. In water at the scale a real construct occupies, EXACTLY NOTHING. Do not build this.
- **If you do split the body, make the split asymmetric** (body): On a dry floor, 7.6x to 66x more net displacement than an adjacent symmetric split at the same resolution, material, amplitude and duration (median 23x over the 13 adjacent pairs in a sweep of nx = 3 to 16). In the viscous regime, still exactly nothing — asymmetry of LAYOUT does not make a stroke non-reciprocal.
- **Evolve the geometry to rectify actuation you cannot control** (body): A construct that moves reliably even though its actuator timing is uncontrolled: shape and tissue placement collectively derandomise the motion. Built and verified in vivo — inverting the design 180 degrees significantly reduced displacement both in silico (p<0.001) and in vivo (p<0.0001).
- **Accept that making it bigger does not make it faster in body lengths** (body): A construct whose displacement per cycle in body lengths falls smoothly as it gets longer — in our sweep the travelling wave peaks near a 5 cm model body and declines by a factor of three by 15 cm. There is no body length at which it suddenly does better.
- **Compute the Reynolds number before trusting any gait model** (fluid): A number that decides which physics applies. For every published construct it is well below 1: anthrobots at 1.5e-4 to 2.5e-2 from their own reported 30-500 um and 5-50 um/s, a nominal 1 mm xenobot at 0.1 mm/s at Re = 0.0996. Inertia is irrelevant; viscosity is everything.
- **Make the stroke non-reciprocal, or get exactly zero** (fluid): A pass/fail gate on any proposed gait, before any simulation. A reciprocal stroke produces ZERO net displacement at low Reynolds number no matter how strong, how fast or how clever it is.
- **Escape the scallop theorem by working in a non-Newtonian fluid** (fluid): A construct whose RECIPROCAL stroke does produce net motion, because the theorem's assumptions no longer hold. Demonstrated with a synthetic single-hinge 'micro-scallop' with measurement, numerics and analysis in agreement.
- **Escape it instead through the swimmer's own inertia** (fluid): A construct that swims on a reciprocal stroke even with NO fluid inertia, driven by the inertia of the swimmer itself. Shown theoretically, in simulation and in experiment for a dumbbell with asymmetric coasting times.
- **Push the construct into the intermediate-Re band where reciprocal strokes start to work** (fluid): A construct for which a simple squeeze-and-release stroke begins to produce measurable net motion. The literature puts the practical floor for jet propulsion around Re = 5: almost all animals using it swim at Re of about 5 or more, and in 2D simulation forward velocity decays rapidly below Re = 10.
- **Crawl on a substrate instead of swimming, and the theorem no longer applies** (fluid): A construct whose contact friction with the surface can rectify even a reciprocal stroke, because surface friction is not Stokes drag and is outside the theorem's assumptions. This is the regime the published xenobots are actually in: the deciliated organisms 'always kept part of their ventral surfaces in contact with the surface of the dish due to negative buoyancy'.
- **Filter designs by robustness to actuation noise before building any of them** (design-loop): A shortlist of designs that actually work when built. Noise resistance in simulation has been shown to predict whether a design keeps its behaviour in vivo.
- **Put buoyancy and drag in the simulator, or it will invent gaits that cannot exist** (design-loop): A design pipeline whose outputs are buildable. Kriegman et al.'s first pass, in their words, 'simulated designs on land' and 'resulted in highly performant but nontransferable designs', with 'timeframes (on average, 47% of the gait cycle) in which no part of the in silico design was in contact' with the simulated ground plane. After 'incorporating first-order hydrodynamics', contact with the simulated ground plane rose from 52.7% to 93.3% of the evaluation period.
- **Model cilia as a surface impulse, not as a volumetric gait** (design-loop): A simulation that matches what cilia-driven constructs actually do. This is how the self-replication work modelled its organisms, and it means the whole six-gait volumetric-actuation question does not describe a cilia-driven xenobot at all.
- **Know that the material parameters in these simulations are tuned, not measured** (design-loop): A calibrated expectation of how much any of these numbers mean. Kriegman et al. 2021 state that voxels were 'simulated with material properties manually tuned to allow for the largest stable time step of numerical integration'.
- **Refine the grid on a parity-matched series, or you measure your own encoding** (design-loop): A refinement series that measures discretisation error rather than a change of design. The published artifact refined nx 5 -> 10, which moved the actuation split from 20 mm to 25 mm and flipped its parity at the same time, and reported an 8.2x change.
- **Let a swarm build its own successors out of loose cells** (collective): A swarm that pushes loose cells into piles; piles of at least 50 cells mature into new motile spheroids. This is the published system doing mechanical work on external material, and the material happens to be more of itself.
- **Add more constructs and ask what actually scales** (collective): In our model, a swarm that moves MORE total material as N rises but does not aggregate it: the particle field's radius of gyration increases at every swarm size and every random seed we ran. A uniform swarm with no sensing and no differentiated roles sweeps; it does not corral.
- **Expect constructs to stick to each other, especially if ciliated** (collective): A swarm whose members collide, form temporary mechanical bonds and orbit each other before separating tangentially; with cilia intact they frequently become entangled and swap partners. Predicted in silico and observed in vivo.
- **Design the arena instead of the organism** (collective): In SIMULATION, wild-type spheroids achieving significantly more replication rounds than on a flat surface (p<0.0001), and in a cluttered environment the search found shapes that lift the organism above the clutter.
- **Clear particulate matter from a surface** (application): Constructs that spontaneously aggregate external objects, shown both in silico and in vivo, individually and in groups. This is the one application on the ladder that is OBSERVED rather than simulated.
- **Push a defined object in a chosen direction** (application): In our model, real transport: the travelling-wave construct transfers 95% of its own path length to an 8 g cargo and does 3.3% of its actuation energy as useful work against floor friction. The two-phase gaits transfer 51% and 26%. No published physical xenobot has done this.
- **Carry the heaviest load that still moves, not the lightest** (application): In our model, a construct whose ENERGY efficiency rises with cargo mass — 0.8% at 2 g to 8.2% at 32 g, while cargo displacement falls only from 107 mm to 78 mm, and then stalls: at 64 g transport collapses 17-fold to 4.6 mm. The intuition that a lighter payload is better is backwards for useful work.
- **Let replication multiply the work, not just the machines** (application): In SIMULATION, utility rising quadratically with time: a swarm closing microcircuits as a side effect of movement, fitted as 2.7x^2 - 43x + 182.4 with R^2 = 0.9988 over 50 simulation bifurcations and 5,024 light emitters switched on.
- **Use the construct's traversal itself as the therapy** (application): Constructs that traverse the scratch and induce rapid repair of it in vitro. The useful work is done by being there and moving, not by carrying anything.
- **Rely on the yolk running out as the off switch** (lifecycle): A construct that moves for 10-14 days on maternally provided energy stores and then sheds cells and deteriorates. Containment is a property of the material rather than a system you have to build.
- **Count on replication halting on its own, and know how many rounds you get** (lifecycle): A self-replicating swarm that stops: spheroid progenitors managed a mean of 1.2+-0.4 generations and semitoroids 3+-0.8, maximum 4, with offspring shrinking each round until they are too small to mature. Replication can also be stopped deliberately by withholding dissociated cells.
- **Keep the genome wild-type so there is nothing heritable to escape** (lifecycle): A construct with no engineered genetic material at all. Whatever it does is a property of its arrangement, which does not propagate into any organism it meets.
- **Use an externally driven magnetic microrobot instead** (comparator): A machine you can steer, that needs no feeding, that works in any fluid you can put a field around, and that stops when you turn the field off. For most mechanical tasks at this scale this wins today, and a funder will ask why it does not.
- **Use a synthetic swarm that actually captures and aggregates** (comparator): A swarm that actively captures free-swimming bacteria and dispersed microplastics on the fly and can be collected afterwards. This is the corral-and-aggregate application, delivered, with a retrieval story.
- **Move the particles directly with sound and skip the robot** (comparator): Contactless manipulation of the objects themselves. If the task is only to move particles from A to B in a container you control, a machine may not be needed at all.
- **Use an engineered muscle actuator on a printed skeleton** (comparator): A biohybrid machine with a DESIGNED, controllable actuator: optogenetic skeletal muscle giving up to 300 uN of active tension and 310 um/s of locomotion, or a cardiomyocyte-driven filament swimmer at 5-10 um/s, 81 um/s with two tails.
- **Bolt a cargo onto something that already swims well** (comparator): A cargo carrier that inherits a propulsion system already optimised by evolution for exactly this Reynolds number, with no gait to design at all.
- **Let chemistry do the propulsion** (comparator): A self-propelled carrier with no external field and no living tissue, able to assemble into chains that pick up and transport microscale objects including yeast cells.

## What we ran

Three studies: one analysis, two simulations. None is an observation, none is calibrated to a measured living construct, and nothing here was built from cells. Two of the three exist to attack our own previously published artifact rather than to support it, and one of them succeeded.

### The gait ranking is from the wrong physics

*analysis + simulation · fluid scale*

**The design question it answers.** A real millimetre-scale construct moves through water at a Reynolds number far below 1. The published gait comparison is dry friction on a floor under gravity, with inertia. Does its ranking of six actuation patterns transfer to the regime the real object occupies?

No, and the failure is qualitative rather than a reordering. Computed from the sizes and speeds the papers themselves report, anthrobots sit at Re = 1.5e-4 to 2.5e-2 and a nominal 1 mm xenobot at Re = 0.0996; the simulated artifact's own body, at its own size and speed, would be at Re = 643 in water, 3.8 orders of magnitude away. Five of the six published patterns assign only the phases 0 and pi, so the whole body is driven by one scalar in time and the stroke retraces itself: they are time-reciprocal, and Purcell's scallop theorem makes their net displacement exactly zero at low Reynolds number. A Rotne-Prager-Yamakawa bead-model Stokes swimmer confirms it, with the travelling wave at 0.034 body lengths per cycle and every other pattern at the numerical floor. The weakness is the one the page must not hide: this applies cleanly to a FREE SWIMMER, and a xenobot crawling on a dish with its ventral surface in contact is not one, because surface friction is not Stokes drag and sits outside the theorem's assumptions. Separately, most published constructs are cilia-driven, and cilia are surface thrust rather than a body gait, so this bears directly on the 2020 cardiac-driven constructs and only indirectly on the rest.

**A millimetre-scale living construct sits at Reynolds number below 0.1, and five of the six published actuation patterns are time-reciprocal, so Purcell's scallop theorem makes their net displacement exactly zero there. The published gait ranking does not transfer: its 2nd and 3rd places are both dead in the regime the real object occupies, and only the travelling wave survives.**

### It does not converge, and the flagged swing was the encoding

*simulation · design-loop scale*

**The design question it answers.** An independent audit recorded a 7.25-fold change in net displacement under grid refinement and left spatial convergence unresolved, and flagged a body-size series that swung ten-fold without trending. Does the model converge on a clean refinement series, and is that swing a gait-length resonance or a numerical artifact?

It does not converge, and the swing is neither of the offered options: it is the actuation encoding. On a parity-matched refinement, holding the outer envelope and the physical actuation field fixed while refining the lattice four-fold from 45 to 2880 voxels, the travelling wave spans 41% of its coarsest value and the longitudinal split 54%, and both series turn over rather than settling. The winner is stable across all four levels but the margin falls from 19.4 to 10.4 times and is still moving at the finest grid. The flagged swing resolves cleanly: the longitudinal pattern splits the body at index nx/2, so odd nx gives an asymmetric actuator layout and even nx a symmetric one, and sweeping nx from 3 to 16 reveals two smooth monotone branches interleaved. Two discriminators declared in advance both fail for a resonance: the alternation is absent from the travelling wave, whose phase does not depend on parity, by a median factor of 1.008 against 20.3; and the optimum frequency does not move with body length, sitting at 12 Hz for both nx = 4 and nx = 8 while the body doubles. The weakness is that only two of six patterns were refined, so this cannot offer a corrected ranking, and four levels can show a series failing to settle but cannot extract a trustworthy convergence order.

**On a parity-matched refinement series the model does NOT converge. ### Does the best self-mover also push best?

*simulation · application scale*

**The design question it answers.** Every headline xenobot result is about the construct moving itself. What body plan and actuation pattern lets it do useful mechanical work on an external object, and is the published optimisation target the right one for the application?

Our predeclared expectation was that the best self-mover would not be the best pusher. It was wrong, and we report it as a failed prediction. The travelling wave is first on every metric: 103.6 mm of cargo moved against 35.8 and 21.7 mm, a transport ratio of 0.95 of its own path transferred to the cargo against 0.51 and 0.26, and seven times the useful work per unit of actuation energy. What the study did find is that the live constraint is reach rather than push — with the cargo one clear lattice site away, only one gait of six covers that distance in the artifact's own five cycles, so a contact-gated forty-cycle arm was added to separate the two — and that all three contacting gaits stall at the same 64 g cargo with efficiency peaking just below it, so the heaviest load that still moves is the right one to carry. The swarm arm returns an honest negative: a uniform swarm sweeps but does not corral, with the particle field's radius of gyration increasing in 28 of 30 runs, and the one apparent exception — 2 of 5 seeds at the largest swarm size — tested rather than hedged and shown to be a field-size effect rather than an onset. The weakness is that all of it is in the dry-friction regime, which our own first study says is not the regime a real construct occupies; in water, four of the six gaits compared here could not push anything at all.

**In this dry-friction model the body plan that moves itself best also pushes best: the travelling wave wins on cargo displacement, on transport ratio and on energy efficiency, contradicting our predeclared expectation. The real design gate is not which gait ranks highest but whether the gait moves an external object at all, and in 5 cycles only one of six even reaches its cargo. A uniform swarm never corrals, over swarm sizes 1 to 16 in two field sizes.**

Our own regime-check study finds that a real millimetre-scale construct sits at Reynolds number below 0.1, where four of the six gaits compared here produce exactly zero displacement and could not push anything at all. Every number in this study is inside a model the first study says is the wrong regime.
- The cargo gap is one lattice site because that is the minimum that avoids Voxelyze linking the two bodies into one. That sets a 7.5 mm clear distance the construct must cross before any pushing happens, which is why arm 1 is reach-limited and arm 1b exists.
- W_actuation is the axial term only: bending and torsion rest states are not actuated so they do no actuation work, but they do store and dissipate energy that is not counted. The actuation efficiency is therefore an UPPER bound on the fraction of total input energy delivered.
- W_cargo_friction is a closed form that ignores collision damping and any kinetic energy left in the cargo at the end, so it is a LOWER bound on work done on the cargo.
- Our declared efficiency metric M4 breaks down past the stall threshold, for the reason recorded above. We found this by running the sweep far enough to stall rather than stopping at a mass where the metric still behaved.
- The swarm's particles are single voxels on the floor with the same friction and density as the construct. Nothing is calibrated to cells, debris or microplastic.
- Voxelyze has no random number generator and this driver's only randomness is our own seeded particle placement, so the seed spread reported here is spread over ARENAS, not over mechanics. Repeat runs at a fixed seed are bitwise identical.
- The swarm result covers N = 1 to 16 in two field sizes. Its one apparent exception, 2 of 5 seeds at N = 8 in the narrower field, was tested rather than hedged and turned out to track the swarm's span relative to the arena rather than its size. The threshold is bracketed between span/field 0.68 and 0.84, not located, and two field widths is not a scan over field width.

## The chain across scales, and where it is not calibrated

These studies are meant to be read as one chain from an actuation pattern to material moved. Two of its junctions are inside a single model, one is a refusal, and only the last has a measured construct behind it.

1. **Actuation phase pattern → Net self-displacement** — *model*. This junction is exact rather than fitted. The rank of the 2×N phase matrix decides reciprocity, and in the Stokes limit a reciprocal gait displaces nothing. It needs no simulator and no parameters, which is why it survives when the simulation does not.
2. **Net self-displacement → Cargo transported** — *model*. Inside the dry-friction model the ordering carries over and the gap widens: the travelling wave transfers 0.95 of its own path length to the cargo against 0.51 and 0.26. This contradicted our own predeclared expectation, which is recorded in the study rather than quietly dropped.
3. **The dry-friction model → A real construct in water** — *refused*. Refused outright. The simulated body is 40 to 50 mm with no fluid in it; a real construct is 0.5 to 1 mm in water. Every number in the cargo study is inside a model the first study shows is the wrong regime, and the refinement series does not converge in any case, so no magnitude from it is quotable.
4. **Body geometry → Material gathered** — *model*. The only junction with a built and measured construct behind it rather than a simulation: a concave mouth converts locomotion into gathering without any per-region phase control, actuator timing or contractile tissue. The page's recommendation rests here, and it rests on someone else's measurement.
5. **One construct → A swarm that corrals** — *refused*. Refused on our own evidence. A uniform, sensorless, single-gait swarm sweeps and does not corral at any size from 1 to 16. What first looked like onset at the largest swarm was the swarm reaching the edge of its dish: aggregation tracks how much of the arena the swarm spans, not how many constructs are in it.

> One junction is exact, two are inside a model whose own regime check condemns it, and two are refused. The end-to-end statement — this pattern moves that much material — is refused. The chain is a map of what would have to be measured, and the one link with a measurement behind it is the one that uses geometry instead of control.

## Welfare, containment and what leaves the dish

**Animal use.**

The Xenopus routes require fertilised Xenopus laevis eggs and embryos harvested at blastula stage. The source papers record institutional approval: Kriegman et al. 2020 under Tufts IACUC protocol M2017-53, Kriegman et al. 2021 under M2020-35. Shaping is subtractive surgery — microcautery with a 13 um wire electrode — performed on tissue explanted before a nervous system exists.

**The anthrobot route avoids animals entirely.**

Anthrobots are built from adult human lung epithelial cells and need no animal, no embryo and no genetic editing. If a programme can be run on that substrate, the animal-use question largely disappears. This is a substantive welfare argument for the human route and not only a commercial one.

**Moral status of the constructs themselves.**

These constructs have no nervous system and are not sentient on any account we are aware of. They are nonetheless novel self-organising entities with behaviours absent from the donor organism, and the field itself has raised the question rather than dismissed it. We flag it and do not resolve it.

**Containment.**

Lifespan is bounded by maternally provided yolk at 10–14 days with no feeding, replication halts after 2–4 rounds on its own, and the genome is unmodified in the baseline routes — so there is no heritable engineered material to escape. Two caveats we will not paper over: the contractile-gait route DOES inject mRNA (Notch ICD and a lineage tracer), so a designed-gait construct is not wild-type in the sense the containment argument assumes; and the AI-designed shape that makes replication useful is the same knob that makes it halt later.

**What this page does not address.**

Any in vivo deployment. Every route here is a dish, a culture or a simulation. Delivery, biodistribution, immune response in a real body and environmental release are all out of scope and none of our studies bear on them.

## What this page does not claim

- Nothing here was built from cells. No construct was made, no animal was used, and no wet-lab experiment was run for this page.
- The three studies are two simulations and an analysis. None is an observation, and none is calibrated to a measured living construct.
- No route on this page is a protocol. Each names published evidence and what would have to be done; none has been attempted here.
- The gait ranking in our own earlier artifact is withdrawn, not corrected. We are not offering a replacement ranking, because the refinement series does not converge and no magnitude from it is quotable.
- voxcraft-sim failed to build in our hands on one toolkit and one platform, and the pinned CUDA 10.2 route was untried. That is a blocker we measured, not a verdict on the engine, and no cross-engine comparison is offered anywhere on this page.
- A threshold in a model is not a threshold in a dish.
- Our three studies are SIMULATIONS. No organism, tissue or cell was measured for this page. Every mechanical parameter in them is marked assumed, not sourced, and the same is true of the published simulations they re-examine — Kriegman et al. 2021 state their voxel material properties were 'manually tuned to allow for the largest stable time step of numerical integration'.
- The scallop-theorem result applies cleanly to a FREE SWIMMER. A xenobot crawling on a dish bottom, negatively buoyant with its ventral surface in contact, is not one: contact friction is not Stokes drag and is outside the theorem's assumptions, so a reciprocal gait can rectify against a surface. The defensible claim is that the published ranking is void for a free or near-neutrally-buoyant construct, and that for a crawler its magnitudes come from an object 3.8 orders of magnitude away in Reynolds number.
- Cilia-driven propulsion is not body-deformation swimming, and most published constructs are cilia-driven. The scallop theorem still constrains the cilium's own stroke, but a surface-thrust machine has no body gait for our analysis to rank. Our regime result therefore bears directly on the 2020 cardiac-driven constructs and only indirectly on the 2021 cilia-driven ones.
- Our Stokes swimmer is a Rotne-Prager-Yamakawa bead model over shapes from a quasi-static central-force spring lattice. That lattice obeys the Cauchy relations, so its Poisson ratio is a property of the spring topology rather than an input: we measure 0.20 against the artifact's 0.35, and its axial and bending effective moduli differ by about a factor of two. We use it only to generate a shape, and we report that the Stokes ranking shifts by under 1% across a 4x change in its stiffness ratio.
- The five reciprocal gaits do not come back as literally zero but as 1e-7 to 1e-8 body lengths per cycle, which is the numerical residue of an iterative shape solve. We establish that by refinement rather than assertion — tightening the solve drops those values by four orders while the travelling wave holds to four significant figures — and their relative order must not be read as a ranking.
- The cargo and swarm studies are in the dry-friction regime, matching the artifact they extend, NOT the viscous regime our own first study says the real object occupies. They answer 'does the best self-mover also push best' within that model and nowhere else.
- Our swarm has no sensing, no communication and no differentiated roles, and every member runs the same gait. Its failure to aggregate is a statement about that swarm, not about swarms.
- Read-levels are stated per source and most are 'listing' — citation record retrieved and verified from PubMed, abstract not read by us. Only Kriegman et al. 2020 and 2021 were read in full text. Four physics citations (Purcell 1977, Najafi & Golestanian 2004, Lauga 2007, Lauga & Powers 2009) are not indexed in PubMed and are labelled search-summary: their volume, page and DOI details were NOT independently verified by us.
- Scope of the negative on the application question: searching PubMed and Consensus for a xenobot or anthrobot deliberately transporting a defined external cargo found nothing. What exists is debris and particle AGGREGATION as a reported side effect of locomotion, plus object transport in silico only. bioRxiv was not searched by keyword.
- voxcraft-sim, the GPU engine this literature actually uses, does NOT build at its pin against a modern toolkit. We ran the probe on an NVIDIA L40S in nvidia/cuda:12.4.1-devel-ubuntu22.04: both arms configured cleanly and both failed to compile, because the code calls cudaDeviceSynchronize() from __device__ functions (removed in CUDA 12, seven sites) and because nvcc cannot compile Boost.Asio's trait machinery reached through boost::process. No cross-engine comparison is offered anywhere here. We tested one toolkit on one platform; the pinned CUDA 10.2 route was not attempted. Receipt: data/voxcraft-probe.json.

## Why a page that withdraws its own result is worth publishing

This design space was commissioned to extend our own simulation and ended by retiring its headline. That is the useful thing about it. The artifact it audits was preregistered, independently reviewed, and carried its own list of what it could not establish — and it was still wrong in a way none of that caught, because nobody had asked which fluid regime the object lives in. The check that found it takes a minute and needs no simulator. Every route here carries its evidence level, every junction between scales carries a label or a refusal, and the studies can be re-run from the repository. A platform that cannot retract its own results is not a design platform.
