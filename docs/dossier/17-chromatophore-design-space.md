# 17. Programmable skin: the design space

Generated by `scripts/designspace_doc.py` from the same files the live page reads, so the two cannot drift.
The interactive version, with the figures and the filterable route list, is at <https://studio.morphoswarm.ai/#/studio/chromatophore>.

**The question.** Could you build skin that changes appearance on command? The question only becomes an engineering question once you ask what the actuator is. A cephalopod chromatophore is a real one with a real datasheet — a pigment sac pulled open by 15–25 radial muscle fibres under direct motor-neuron control, expanding in about a tenth of a second — sitting on an optical stack that works by a different mechanism and runs a hundred times slower. This page maps the routes into that system at eight scales, models three of them, and is deliberately blunt about how few of them anyone can currently take.

## The actuator is real and well-measured. Our model of it does not reproduce it, and the channel count is set by innervation rather than by pattern complexity.

The chromatophore is the part of this that is genuinely an engineering component, and it is the part our model gets wrong. Two parameters were fitted to two measured quantities — the sacculus stiffness to the steady-state expansion ratio of 2.55, and the dermal damping to the measured expansion-to-retraction asymmetry, which it reaches at 1.27 against Renard 2026's 1.34. The two fits are mutually inconsistent: at that damping the peak expansion collapses to 1.73, 32% short of the ratio the stiffness was fitted to. And the one genuinely free prediction fails outright — a 90% rise of 232 ms against a measured expansion phase of 67–83 ms, 2.8 to 3.5 times too slow. The control arm fails. A lumped linear actuator with five sourced parameters and thirteen assumed ones is the wrong object, and three independent measurements say so in three different ways.

An earlier version of this study claimed something stronger and it was wrong. It said a lumped elastic recoil was STRUCTURALLY incapable of retracting more slowly than it expands. That claim came from a damping sweep whose maximum sat at its own boundary — the domain had excluded the answer — and it is retracted here rather than quietly dropped. What replaces it is weaker and true: a three-way incompatibility between two fits and one free prediction.

The optics come out cleanly and the answer is counter-intuitive. Freezing the iridophore stack removes 88% of the achievable gamut, so the multilayer is what makes the colour. But widening its measured 425–675 nm tuning range buys 1%, while raising the reflectin refractive index from the live-cell mean of 1.405 to the measured maximum of 1.44 buys 68%, and a nearly transparent retracted state buys 61%. The limit is refractive-index contrast and the chromatophore's retraction floor, not tuning range. Removing the yellow pigment class makes the gamut BIGGER, because at the only sourced expansion ratio a retracted chromatophore still covers 52% of the skin.

On how many wires it would take: at the measured motor-unit size of nine actuators per channel, the knee is at 579 channels over a 4 × 4 mm patch carrying 3,904 actuators — about 36 channels per mm², one channel per 6.7 actuators. Across unit sizes the knee tracks the number needed simply to COVER the array within a factor of 1.00 to 5.41, so for everything but the finest target the channel budget is set by innervating the skin at all rather than by the complexity of the pattern you want. The animal is not a free parameter here: Renard et al. 2026 resolved 754 putative motor units driving 1,829 chromatophores. Those two counts are their measurement; the ratio is ours — 754/1829 = 0.4122 channels per actuator — which puts this patch at 1,609 channels — 2.78 times past the knee, and reaching 99–101% of the fidelity one channel per chromatophore would give. That last figure is easy to misread, so state it precisely: it is measured against the ceiling of the animal's own motor-unit size, not against a perfect match. At nine actuators per channel the ceiling for fine detail is itself low — fine stripes plateau at 0.64 and 1/f gravel at 0.90, against 0.95–0.97 for the coarse targets — and no channel count moves them. So the animal has every channel its motor-unit size can use, and for fine patterns a unit of nine is already too coarse. That is the sharper engineering statement, and it is the same breakdown the coverage test shows at large unit sizes.

That last number is the one with a design consequence. If the animal is already well past the knee, then a synthetic array does not need to match cephalopod innervation density to match cephalopod performance, and the engineering target drops by most of a factor of three. It rests on a knee whose uncertainty is a grid spacing, not a standard deviation — see the precision note below.

> The knee's reported spread across 30 runs is 0.0, and that is a GRID ARTEFACT rather than a precision claim: channel count was swept on a 14-point logarithmic grid, so a knee can only land on a grid point, and all 30 landed on the same one. The honest quote is "between 306 and 1094, nearest grid point 579". What the agreement shows is that the knee is stable to within one grid step — a factor of about 1.9 in either direction — which is the weaker true statement.

## If you asked us to actually do it

**The route.** Express ANY transgene in a cephalopod chromatophore (`gen-transgene-in-a-chromatophore`)

**Why this one.**

Not because it is the most interesting result, but because it is the only route that unblocks the others. Exactly two genetic manipulations have been demonstrated in any cephalopod chromatophore system, and both DELETE a pigment gene: tdo knockout in Doryteuthis pealeii (2020) and tdo+ido double knockout giving a heritable albino Euprymna berryi line (2023). You cannot build an addressable skin by deleting things. Every cephalopod route in this space that ADDS a controllable element - an optogenetic drive on a radial muscle, a designer receptor, an engineered reflectin, a reporter that labels one motor unit - requires expressing a transgene in a chromatophore, and we found no published instance of a transgene being expressed in a cephalopod at all. Counted rather than guessed, and the count is computed in `validation.cephalopod_routes_nobody_can_take` rather than asserted here: of the 30 cephalopod-scale routes on this page, twelve are ones nobody can currently take. One of those twelve is this step itself and one is our own controller-sizing study, which needs no animal. The other TEN are downstream of expressing a transgene - as is every future route of that kind. The reagents now exist to try: a germline-competent species with a closed life cycle, published single-cell atlases to mine for a chromatophore-specific promoter, and a working delivery route. Until this lands, the cephalopod half of this page is a reading list.

**What we would fund second.**

circ-size-the-controller and the measurement that feeds it. The control layer is the part of this system that is genuinely novel as engineering - a chromatophore is about four independent actuators, not one, and real pattern transitions live on a 9-dimensional manifold out of 17,305 - and it is measurable today with video and no genetics at all.

**The route we would not fund.**

Anything whose goal is a wider tunable range in the reflector. Our own optical model says the measured 425-675 nm range already spans the useful band and widening it buys 1% of gamut, while refractive-index contrast buys 68%.

**If the goal is a product rather than a result.**

Buy the non-living route. A 16x16 autonomously colour-matching optoelectronic sheet was built in 2014 and switches in 1-2 s; commercial electrophoretic panels run at 300 ppi, a pitch comparable to a real chromatophore's. On 'skin that changes appearance on command', the engineered article already wins. The biology's contribution right now is the design brief - what to aim for, and which axes are hard - not the substrate.

**What would change our mind.**

A single published transgene expressed in a cephalopod chromatophore. That one result moves the ten routes counted above from unreachable to attemptable, opens the class of routes that cannot be written down yet because nobody can try them, and nothing else on the horizon does.

## The design space: 46 routes

Every row is a published perturbation or a bounded proposal, with its own evidence level. **None of them has been attempted here.** Sources carry read levels: a listing is not a reading.

The Bragg reflector inside an iridophore and the pigment sacculus inside a chromatophore. Two mechanisms, two timescales: one is muscle in a tenth of a second, the other is protein assembly over minutes.

## What we ran

Three studies: two mechanistic simulations and one sweep over control architectures. None is an observation. The first one FAILS its pre-declared control arm against measured kinetics, and that failure is reported as the result rather than repaired away. Each study carries its controls, what it sourced against what it assumed, and a statement of what it does not establish.

### The chromatophore as an actuator: mechanics, kinetics and a datasheet

*simulation (Hill-type muscle + hyperelastic membrane) · cell scale*

**The design question it answers.** A cephalopod chromatophore is a pigment sac opened by radial muscle under direct motor-neuron control. Treat it as an actuator and write its datasheet: what stroke, what speed, what force, what does it cost to hold open - and does a physical model of it reproduce what was measured?

Two parameters are fitted to two measured quantities and the two fits are mutually inconsistent. The sacculus shear modulus is fitted to the 2.5506x steady-state expansion ratio; the dermal damping is then fitted to the measured expansion:retraction asymmetry and reaches it (1.27 against 1.34 from Renard 2026, while Hadjisolomou 2021 decomposed per region gives 3.7) - but at that damping the peak expansion collapses to 1.73, 32% short of the ratio the stiffness was fitted to. **The one genuinely free prediction fails**: the 90% rise is 232 ms against a measured expansion phase of 67-83 ms, 2.8-3.5x too slow. An earlier version of this study claimed a lumped elastic recoil was STRUCTURALLY incapable of retraction slower than expansion; that was wrong - it was read off a sweep whose maximum sat at its own boundary - and the correction is recorded in the file rather than edited out. The parameter ledger is the study's own indictment: 5 sourced against 13 assumed, with every muscle property an explicit proxy from a different Doryteuthis muscle because nobody has measured a chromatophore radial muscle. Its most useful output is negative: across every sweep the stroke is set by the elastic sac rather than by the muscle, so adding radial fibres buys almost nothing.

- This model is a one-constraint model being asked to meet three. It is fitted to the steady-state expansion ratio and then scored on expansion speed and on the expansion:retraction asymmetry, and no single setting meets more than one of them. Enough damping reaches either measured asymmetry, but at a cost of a third to a half of the expansion magnitude and a rise time that is already 2.6-3.2x too slow and gets slower. A next version needs a mechanism that slows retraction WITHOUT loading expansion - a viscoelastic sacculus whose stress relaxes during the hold, or an active brake - rather than more of the one knob this model has.
- The two measured asymmetries differ by about 3x and are preserved separately rather than averaged. They are also different measurements in different animals: Renard tracks phases of individual slice events at 20 fps in Euprymna berryi and Sepia officinalis; the Hadjisolomou figure is decomposed by us from per-region summary statistics of a startle response in Doryteuthis pealeii, and that decomposition is ours, not the authors'.
- The sacculus shear modulus has never been measured; it is the one fitted parameter, so the model cannot claim to predict the expansion ratio - it was told it.
- Every muscle property is a PROXY from a different squid muscle (mantle circular or funnel retractor). No one has measured the contractile properties of a chromatophore radial muscle, so sigma_max, V_max and the activation kinetics are assumed.
- The measured control target itself is a startle response, not a maximal contraction: Hadjisolomou et al. report what the organ did on a light flash, and state no maximum. The folklore figure of a ~7x diameter / ~50x area expansion traces, as far as we could follow it, to a secondary teaching page citing Cloney & Florey 1968; we could not read a primary source stating it, and so do not use it.
- The geometry is axisymmetric, but the organ is not: Renard et al. 2026 show expansion is routinely anisotropic, with about four independently driven petal-shaped sectors per chromatophore. A single radius cannot express that, and the model therefore cannot represent the sub-chromatophore control that the real system has.
- Radial muscles of neighbouring chromatophores make direct muscle-to-muscle contacts (Gauld et al. 2021), so an isolated single-organ model omits a real mechanical coupling.
- The dermal damping is not measured anywhere; the value reported is what the model needs, not what the tissue has.
- Holding cost is reported as mechanical stress and stored elastic energy only. We did not find a measured ATP cost for cephalopod obliquely striated muscle, so no metabolic number is given rather than a fabricated one.

### A pigment layer over a tunable Bragg reflector: how much colour is reachable, and which layer stops it

*optical model (transfer-matrix multilayer + areal pigment mixing + CIE colorimetry) · organelle scale*

**The design question it answers.** Cephalopod skin stacks a pigment actuator over a tunable multilayer reflector over a broadband scatterer. Given the measured optical parameters of each, what colours can that stack actually reach under a stated illuminant - and which layer is the one holding it back?

Freezing the iridophore stack removes 89% of the achievable chromaticity area, so the multilayer is what produces the colour - but the layer that PROVIDES the gamut is not the layer that LIMITS it. Widening the stack's measured 425-675 nm tuning range to the whole visible band buys 1%, while raising the reflectin refractive index from the live-cell mean 1.405 to the measured maximum 1.44 buys 68% and a nearly transparent retracted state buys 61%. **The study's weakness is entirely on the pigment side**: the Bragg stack's layer count, both refractive indices, both thicknesses and its tuning range are published numbers from live cells, but no measured absorption spectrum for any cephalopod ommochrome was found, so all nine pigment numbers are assumed and every hue in the gamut inherits that (9 sourced against 8 assumed). It is also a steady-state, normal-incidence model under CIE D65 and a HUMAN observer, which is wrong on three counts at once: real iridophores are strongly angle-dependent, seawater is not D65, and the animals that matter are a colour-blind cephalopod and a tetrachromatic fish. One of its four controls fails, and the failure is in the source rather than the model - the published endpoint thicknesses do not reproduce the published tuning range under the paper's own refractive indices.

**Freezing the iridophore stack removes 88% of the achievable gamut, so the multilayer is what produces the colour; but widening its measured 425-675 nm tuning range buys 1%, whereas raising the reflectin refractive index from the live-cell mean 1.405 to the measured maximum 1.44 buys 68% and a nearly transparent retracted state buys 61%. The limit is refractive-index contrast and the chromatophore's retraction floor, not tuning range. Removing the yellow pigment class makes the gamut BIGGER, because at the only sourced expansion ratio a retracted chromatophore still covers 52% of the skin.**

Note how close together the achievable set and the white point are: at the measured refractive-index contrast of 1.048 a nine-pair stack reflects only about 15 per cent at its peak, so the skin is never very saturated.

18 parameters, of which 8 are assumed rather than sourced.

- The illuminant is CIE D65, not a measured underwater downwelling spectrum. We did not find one we could read, and did not invent one. Seawater attenuates red strongly with depth, so the real gamut of a cephalopod in its own light field is smaller than this and shifted blue-green; we do not estimate by how much.
- The observer is the CIE 1931 2-degree HUMAN observer. Cephalopods are colour-blind (one visual pigment in the species studied) and the predators they hide from are fish, often tetrachromatic. A gamut area in human u'v' is a convenient common currency, not the quantity that matters to either animal.
- No measured absorption spectrum for a cephalopod ommochrome was found, so all nine pigment numbers (three edges, three widths, three optical densities) are assumed. The ORDER of the classes and the long-pass SHAPE are supported by Reiter et al. 2018's measured transmission spectra and xanthommatin localisation; the positions are not.
- The stack is computed at normal incidence only. Real iridophore reflectance is strongly angle-dependent and the lamellae are not parallel to the skin surface, so an off-axis observer sees a different colour. This model cannot say anything about the angular gamut.
- Scattering is ignored. The leucophore is a diffuse backing with a single number; pigment granules are treated as a pure absorber with no surface reflection, so the pigment can only darken and filter, never add a specular highlight.
- The one-bounce addition of the backing beneath the stack is an approximation; a full multiple-scattering treatment would raise the reflectance of the leucophore-on states somewhat.
- The Bragg peak is placed by holding the live-cell thickness RATIO constant and scaling the pair. The source reports both thicknesses correlating with peak wavelength, which is consistent with that but does not establish it; the animal may move the two thicknesses independently, which would make the reachable set larger than modelled.
- Iridophores and chromatophores run at timescales two orders of magnitude apart (chromatophore expansion ~0.2 s; neurally evoked iridophore colour shift 17 s and brightness 32 s, Wardill et al. 2012). This model is steady-state and says nothing about what gamut is reachable QUICKLY.

### How many independent control channels does an addressable skin need?

*simulation (pigment-actuator array + observer blur + projected-gradient controller) · circuit scale*

**The design question it answers.** An array of pigment actuators at a measured spatial density is driven by N independent channels, each owning a motor unit of m actuators. How well can the skin match a target pattern, and where is the point of diminishing returns? The knee is the spec sheet for anyone building this.

At the measured motor-unit size of nine actuators per channel the knee sits at 579 +/- 0 channels over a 3904-actuator patch - about 36 channels per square millimetre, or one channel per 6.7 actuators - but quote it as 'between 306 and 1094, nearest grid point 579', NOT as '579 +/- 0'. The standard deviation across the 30 runs is genuinely 0.0 and that is a GRID ARTEFACT: channel count is swept on a 14-point logarithmic grid, so a knee can only land on a grid point and all 30 landed on the same one. What the agreement shows is that the knee is stable to within one grid step. Across unit sizes the knee tracks the count needed simply to COVER the array within a factor of 1.00-5.41, so for everything but the finest target the channel budget is set by innervating the skin at all, not by the complexity of the pattern. The real animal is not a free parameter here: Renard et al. 2026 resolved 754 putative motor units driving 1829 chromatophores, which is 0.412 channels per actuator with units of about nine, so a cuttlefish sits at 1609 channels on this patch and is PAST the knee on every target. **Its biggest weakness is that the addressable element is wrong**: motor units here own whole actuators, whereas a real chromatophore is driven by about four independent sources acting on contiguous angular sectors, so these channel counts are a lower bound on what a cuttlefish has. The observer's acuity is assumed and is the parameter the knee most likely depends on, no predator acuity number having been sourced; the coverage law is biased by up to 16 per cent relative and that control is reported as FAILED, with a sensitivity arm showing the knee does not move. Two optimiser bugs were caught by declared controls before they could ship as results, and both are documented in the file with the numbers they produced. 4 parameters are sourced against 4 assumed.

## The chain across scales, and where it is not calibrated

Three models, read as one chain from a pigment molecule to a patch of skin you could address. Two of its junctions are inside a model and explicit; two cross between models with nothing calibrating them; and the first link is a model that fails its own control.

1. **Radial muscle force → Chromatophore expansion** — *refused*. This is the actuator-mechanics model, and it FAILS its control arm: 232 ms to 90% expansion against a measured 67–83 ms. The two fitted parameters are also mutually inconsistent. Nothing downstream may be read as calibrated through this junction; it is here to say where the chain breaks, not to carry a number across it.
2. **Chromatophore expansion → Skin coverage fraction** — *model*. Inside the optical model and explicit: the only sourced expansion ratio is 2.55, which leaves a retracted chromatophore still covering 52% of the skin. That single sourced number does a lot of work and is flagged where it is used.
3. **Skin coverage and the multilayer → Achievable colour gamut** — *model*. Inside the optical model, computed against CIE colour-matching functions and D65. The sensitivity ranking — index contrast 68%, retraction floor 61%, tuning range 1% — is a property of this model's layer stack.
4. **Gamut and pattern target → Channels required** — *scenario*. The channel sweep scores a reconstruction against pattern targets, not against colours a real skin can make. Joining the gamut result to the channel count would need the targets to be drawn from the achievable gamut, and they are not.
5. **Channels required → A buildable array** — *scenario*. The sweep says how many independent channels a pattern needs. It says nothing about routing them, powering them, or whether any fabrication process reaches 36 channels per mm² in a soft material.

> One junction is refused outright because the model behind it fails its control; two are inside a single model and explicit; two cross between models with no calibration. The end-to-end statement — "this many wires gives that much colour" — is not available from this page, and the first link is the reason.

## Animal welfare and the 3Rs

**Status.**

Cephalopods are the invertebrates most often brought inside research-animal regulation, and the literature treats them that way: papers on this list include dedicated anaesthesia studies framed explicitly as welfare contributions. We did not read any legal instrument and make no claim about what any particular jurisdiction requires.

**Anaesthesia.**

Magnesium chloride and ethanol have been assessed in Euprymna berryi embryos; isoflurane has been assessed dose-dependently in Octopus vulgaris and described by its authors as a contribution to cephalopod welfare. Both are pilot or dose-finding studies, not settled protocols.

**What the routes here actually cost.**

Most circuit-scale routes on this page are terminal or near-terminal: motor-unit mapping is done on freshly dissected skin, single-motoneuron stimulation needs an opened animal, and optic-lobe microstimulation is invasive brain work. The developmental and behavioural routes are not - Reiter et al. tracked 17,305 chromatophores in freely behaving animals with nothing more invasive than a camera, which is worth noting because it produced the single most useful control-layer number on this page.

**The sedation question.**

Renard et al. checked whether sedation changed their result and it did not (3.715 +/- 1.211 independent components per chromatophore sedated versus 3.683 +/- 1.224 head-fixed and unsedated, p = 0.83). That control is worth copying: it is the difference between a welfare measure and a confound.

**Vertebrate alternative.**

If the goal is a demonstration rather than cephalopod biology specifically, zebrafish and tilapia routes reach a comparable result in an animal with established husbandry, established welfare frameworks and reversible, non-invasive actuation (light on, light off).

**Non-living alternative.**

Several routes here involve no animal at all and reach the headline capability faster. That is an uncomfortable thing for a biology page to say and we think it should be said first, not last.

## What this page does not claim

- No animal was used. Nothing on this page was built, fabricated or implanted, and no skin of any kind was made.
- The three studies are simulations. None is an observation, and none is calibrated to a measurement of the system it models.
- The actuator model FAILS its pre-declared control arm against measured expansion kinetics. It is published as a failure, not as a working model, and must not be cited as one.
- A retracted structural claim is kept visible on this page rather than deleted: an earlier version asserted that a lumped elastic recoil was structurally incapable of slow retraction. That was an artifact of a sweep bounded at its own maximum and is withdrawn.
- The knee at 579 channels has grid-limited uncertainty, not measured uncertainty. A reported spread of 0.0 across 30 runs reflects a 14-point logarithmic grid, not precision.
- "The animal reaches 99–101% of the fidelity one channel per chromatophore would give" is NOT a claim that the animal is near-optimal. It is measured against the ceiling imposed by its own motor-unit size of nine, and that ceiling is 0.64 for fine stripes and 0.90 for 1/f gravel against 0.95–0.97 for coarse targets. The page must not be read as saying cephalopod skin reproduces fine detail well; it says the animal is not channel-limited.
- Five of the seven controls declared for the channel study did not run. The job emitted its sweep before its controls block and only the sweep survived; those five are declared-and-absent, which is a gap in verification rather than a clean result. Do not describe the two that did run as "the controls" — seven were declared and two were reached.
- The most consequential of those five absences is `monotonicity_in_channel_count`, because that is the control that previously caught an optimiser artefact scoring 0.0000 at N = K. Its earlier passing run does NOT transfer to these results: the knee moved from 512 to 579 once the sweep ran on the full grid, so the arm would have to be re-run against the current numbers to mean anything.
- Neither 0.412 nor 0.4122 is a figure Renard et al. publish. They report 754 motor units and 1,829 chromatophores; every channels-per-actuator number on this page is our quotient of those two counts, at different roundings of the same value.
- The route table's evidence levels are read levels on sources, not replications. No route here has been attempted by us.
- A model gamut is not a gamut anyone has measured off an animal, and the layer stack it is computed over is a model of a stack.
- A count of routes with direct cephalopod evidence flatters this page, and the `validation` block is built to stop it doing that. Every route carries a `route_kind` as well as an evidence strength, and the cross-tab is computed. The number that matters is `addressable_runtime_control`: how many routes give a dial you could actually use in a working skin - addressed to a place, changeable second by second. Read that field before anything else on this page. In a cephalopod the only entries are electrode stimulations of a dissected or opened preparation, which is not a programmable skin; it is a way of finding out how one would work.
- Cephalopod causal genetics rests on two papers. Crawford et al. 2020 and Ahuja et al. 2023 are the entire demonstrated repertoire, both knockouts of the same pigment pathway. Treating this as a 'literature' would be wrong.
- We read the full text of six sources and the abstract of most of the rest. An abstract-level read is recorded as such on every evidence item. Several numbers quoted here - the tilapia transparency timings, the Gorodetsky IR figures, the DEA area changes - come from abstracts, and an abstract's numbers are the ones the authors chose to headline.
- The two engineering sources with no PubMed record in the earlier survey (the Rogers optoelectronic sheet, the Whitesides microfluidic skin) do in fact have PMIDs, 25136094 and 22904008, which we found and verified. Much of the remaining cephalopod optics and soft-robotics literature genuinely sits in physics and materials journals; where a source has no PMID the field is simply absent and a DOI or URL carries the citation instead. See NOTES.md.
- Three routes on this page are ours rather than anyone's: the sacculus-softening route, the add-radial-muscles route and the controller-sizing route come out of our own models, and the first two depend on a sacculus stiffness that has never been measured and which our model FITS. They are labelled model_only for that reason and should not be read as predictions with evidence behind them.
- We did not survey the octopus papilla literature, the deep-sea bioluminescent signalling literature, or the behavioural camouflage literature in any depth, although sources from each are in the library. The page is about the actuator, the optics and the control layer.
- No route here has been costed, and several 'difficulty' fields are judgements rather than measurements. Where a difficulty claim rests on an absence ('nobody has done this'), the scope of the search that found nothing is stated in NOTES.md rather than in the route.

## Why a failed control arm is the point

The useful output of this page is not a design. It is that the one component here with a real datasheet — a cell whose kinetics, expansion ratio and innervation have all been measured by somebody — defeats a lumped linear model in three independent ways, and that the number of wires you would need turns out to be set by covering the skin rather than by the pattern you want to draw on it. Both of those are things you only learn by writing the model down and letting it fail against the measurements, which is what a design space is for and what a review article cannot do.
