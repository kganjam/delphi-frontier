## Where it stands

- 1123 live claims of 1257 for project `zebrafish-flight`; **134 superseded**, which is the project correcting itself and is not a defect.

- (the same database also holds `xenobot` 164, `insect-flight` 114, `limbless-mouse` 113, `coral` 58 — NOT counted above, because an earlier version of this summary counted the whole table and reported another project's work as this one's)

- live claims by evidence class: `literature` 440, `derived` 232, `measured` 200, `simulated` 171, `unknown` 50, `assumed` 27, `refused` 3


**Two criteria, and they are not the same question:**

- the **2 cm lift-attributable** threshold asks whether a wing beats no wing. The best specifiable design is **1.08× short** of it, and crosses only by extrapolating a speed below its measured range (claims 574, 575).

- **lift ≥ weight**, which is what flying means, needs ~17× the present silhouette. The best design is **4.3× short** (claim 571).


**Every lever has been pushed to a bound:**

| lever | bound | why |
|---|---|---|
| fin area | ~2.65x, ADULTS ONLY | three alleles on one bioelectric pathway. The three papers this rests on -- PMC3894163, PMC6039437, PMC7235970 -- mention `larva` and `dpf` ZERO times between them against 15, 4 and 21 mentions of `adult` (claim 1401), so the multiplier is not established below 10 mm and CANNOT BE MULTIPLIED BY THE BODY-SIZE ROW without extrapolating across the stage at which fin musculature matures |
| fin shape | nothing beyond the floor | aspect ratio saturates; induced drag -> 0 and the requirement stops falling |
| exit speed | 1.57-1.60 m/s | muscle work, and speeds add in quadrature so a run-up needs a 23 BL/s sprint |
| body size | favourable, bounded by a 5x gap in the exponent | lift/weight ~ L^(2b-1) and b is bracketed, not unknown: b=0 from mummichog FAST-START (claim 573) gives 6.0 mm at Re 642, b=0.269 from zebrafish FEEDING strikes measured off a figure (claim 1292) gives 1.1 mm at Re 50. The within-species fast-start exponent is the open quantity |
| body mass | no implementation | needs ~46% gas volume and a distributed autonomic setpoint defeated |
| surface taxi | NARROWED, and now rests on muscle POWER rather than frequency | the exemplar taxis to 1.6x its exit speed, worth 2.69x in lift, applied by claim 1404. A WILD-TYPE zebrafish cannot drive it: claim 196 needs ~49-73 Hz at 1.95 m/s against measured adult maxima of 21 Hz sustained and 52 Hz transient, and claim 37 puts the taxi ceiling at sustainable frequencies at ~0.56 m/s, which is Ucrit itself. At the 16.3 mm target the requirement is 90-526 Hz, because amplitude scales with length so f ~ 1/L and shrinking the body to supply LIFT raises the taxi frequency by the same factor (claim 1866). BUT THE DESIGN IS NOT A WILD-TYPE FISH: against the toadfish swimbladder's measured 200 Hz (claim 979), the superfast transgene already in this project's part list, 4 of the 9 amplitude x Strouhal corners are reachable -- every corner at 0.35 body lengths. Every non-superfast muscle measured here covers 0 of 9. So the open question is whether that muscle's 14.19 W/kg suffices, and the taxi's power requirement has never been computed (claim 1869, superseding 1866; subproblems S1304, S1305, S1308) |

The chain is complete in structure. Every link has a number and an evidence class, and the places it fails are named rather than bridged. **The single cheapest open measurement is a zebrafish fast-start velocity against body length over 20–40 mm.** It is not absent from the literature: biomechanics papers measure it and report it in FIGURES, and PMC3675323 states the exact normalisation in its methods while publishing no value in text (claim 1291). Searched through NCBI efetch, Europe PMC full text, Europe PMC figure packages and preprints, the nearest obtainable values are a cross-species plateau (claim 1290) and a within-species FEEDING-strike exponent (claim 1292). It governs whether the body-size lever is worth 6.0 mm or 1.1 mm.


**A cheaper question has displaced it.** The fin-area and body-size rows cannot both be applied as written: 2.65x is an ADULT measurement and the size target is 6.0 mm, which is late-larval and below the 10 mm at which fin musculature reaches adult-like morphology (claims 1400, 1401). The question that now gates the design is **does the 2.65x fin-area effect hold at 6 mm total length?** That is a photograph and an area, on mutant lines that already exist, at a standard stage -- not a search for a value published only in figures.
