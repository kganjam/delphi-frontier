## Results (simulated, declared models only)
**Reproduction gate R0: passes on 2,520 of 2,520 cells** (pattern flag, mode and `settled` equal; amplitude within 1e-6). The per-cell P8
recomputation reproduces PROTOCOL8's published 26 category changes and 28 mode flips exactly.

**Linear calculation (Q1 holds).** My independent dispersion code agrees with the frozen linear fields on 2,520 of 2,520 cells (verdict,
fastest mode, unstable-mode count, σ\* within 1e-9). Closed form vs `numpy.linalg.eigvals`: max difference 1.4e-12. **Q5 fails:** solver
B's finite-difference grid has a different unstable-mode set in 54 cells, but at ρ = 3 (24), 1.5 (18) and 1.03 (12), not mainly at
1.03. It never changes the verdict, and none of those 54 cells is unresolved, so discrete-dispersion mismatch does not explain the
solver-disagreement unresolved mass. The fastest mode differs on B's grid in 6 cells (all ρ = 3).

**Regime (Q3 holds).** A linear regime (a(0) ≤ 0.1 a_T) exists for 470 of 470 linear-unstable patterned cells at ε = 0.01, 20 of 461 at
ε = 0.1 and 0 of 460 at ε = 0.3. **305 of the 461 frozen R3 mismatches (66%) are in cells with no linear regime**: there the linear
prediction was never tested, so they are not evidence that linear theory "mispredicts".

Linear theory holds in its own regime: at t_e the measured dominant mode equals the exact linear prediction in 469 of 490 cells, and the
spectral relative L2 error is ≤ 0.2 in 401 (median 0.045). The largest single cause of testable mismatch is **the observable**: n\* (the
fastest-growing mode) is not what linear theory predicts to dominate at finite time from this initial condition. Those 106 cells are all
at ρ ≥ 1.1 (ρ = 3: 69, 1.5: 31, 1.1: 6), 76 of them Brusselator. **Q2 fails as preregistered**: post-linear links are the first break in
45 of 156 (29%), not at least half. But the full contingency table shows later links also break: the post-saturation link breaks in 132 of
the 490 cells and the growth link in 66, whatever happened earlier. Only 35 of the 106 end at the IC-weighted linear mode. 52 cells whose
chain broke ended back at n\*, so a final-state "match" hides intermediate disagreement. In 133 of 156 mismatches the final mode is lower
(longer wavelength) than n\*. `L_linear_departure` is 5 of 490 (1%), so its half of Q2 (under 10%) holds. Results under the absolute
regime definition (LIN_ABS, n = 470) are close: 88 / 5 / 0 / 45. No L3 mode had a complex eigenvalue pair. t_e = 0 and aliasing never occurred.

## What changes in how the runner's aggregates should be read
* R3 ("linear theory mispredicts the wavelength in 32% of unstable cells") mostly counts cells where no linear regime exists. Where linear
  theory can be tested it is accurate in its regime. The disagreement comes from comparing n\* with a final state.
* R1 (finite-amplitude patterns from linearly stable states) is fragile: 24 of 30 are not stationary at T and 6 change by 2T.
* Non-convergence is reported as non-convergence: 78 unresolved without the stationarity rule, 365 with it.
