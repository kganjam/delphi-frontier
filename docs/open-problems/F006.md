# F006 progress record: does attention and resource allocation improve useful progress?

Issue: kganjam/delphi-frontier#6. Branch `claude/fseries-20261101`. Author `claude-fseries` (Claude Opus 5.5), single reader, not
independently reviewed. Written 2026-11-01, Seattle PST. **This does not answer F006.** No voting or market mechanism was chosen or tested.

## Bounded action done: objective, acceptance predicates and resource constraints, fixed before any mechanism
`experiments/f006_allocation/PREREG.md` (commit `51978679`) + `AMENDMENT_1.md` (`86a15fae`, after a `gemini-4-argon` critique, before any
arm was scored; critique verbatim in `review/argon-prereg-critique.txt`). Part A, the commons-level design:
* **Decision-maker:** the lead of a research problem, choosing which ready task gets a constrained resource at each dispatch point.
* **Objective:** accepted contributions + recorded decision changes, per unit of one primary constrained resource. Weights fixed (1, 1).
* **Acceptance predicate:** the task's own predicate, frozen before dispatch, judged by a non-author who does not know which policy funded
  it. A correctly reported failed or negative result counts as accepted. A provider COMPLETED state, a vote or a reputation score does not.
  `decision_change` counts only a change of category, status, verdict or dependency edge in the record history (not a continuous nudge).
* **Resources:** GPU-minutes, CPU core-seconds, reviewer minutes, LLM tokens, each from a receipt. Policies see only ex-ante declared cost;
  the budget binds on measured ex-post cost, and overruns are charged to the funding policy.
* **Protections:** a coverage floor (every problem with a ready task gets one per round), a 20% stratified-random exploration and
  replication reserve, negative work reported with its cost, and interest / resource / blocking signals kept separate.
* **Baselines:** expert order (actual dispatch order, or one written down before outcomes), uniform random (≥ 100 seeds), stratified
  round-robin, cheapest-first. A mechanism "improves" only if it beats **all** of them at two budgets while meeting the protections.

## Part B, Arm 0: allocation of scarce refinement checks over the F003 sweep cells (run; reported either way)
A within-project instance where ground truth exists for every task, so a counterfactual allocation is scored exactly. **It tests the
evaluation machinery, not community attention.** Amendment 1 discloses that I had read PROTOCOL8's published aggregates (mostly Brusselator,
counts by ρ) before writing the `expert` rule, which is therefore labelled `expert_informed`. Tasks: 2,520 cells. Cost: N × T / dt
(ex ante). Ground truth: step-sensitive (dt 0.02→0.01, recomputed per cell; totals reproduce PROTOCOL8's 26 category changes and 28
mode flips) or `horizon_changed` (F003 2T arm): **56 positives** (32 step, 29 horizon, 5 both), 51 of them in a published R-count or
unresolved mass. Command: `PYTHONPATH=frontier_compute/vendor:. python3 experiments/f006_allocation/arm0.py <F003 analysis.json>
<spectra dir> experiments/f003_align/results/base <rows.jsonl> OUT.json [--horizon-only]`. Outputs: `results/arm0.json`, `results/arm0_horizon_only.json`.

Positives found (random policies: median [5–95%] over 200 seeds); coverage = fraction of 42 (model, ρ, ε) strata checked:

| policy | 10% budget | coverage | 25% budget | coverage | horizon-only truth 10% / 25% |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| uniform | 6 [3–9] | 1.00 | 14 [9–19] | 1.00 | 3 / 7 |
| stratified | 5 [2–9] | 1.00 | 14 [9–19] | 1.00 | 3 / 7 |
| cheapest | 2 | 0.93 | 5 | 1.00 | 0 / 3 |

Of the checks each policy funds, most find nothing (e.g. `signal` at 10%: 503 of 521 checks negative); these costs are counted.

Predictions: **B1** (`signal` beats uniform's median at both budgets) holds, and under the stricter all-baselines rule it beats every
baseline on the full ground truth. But it **loses to `expert_informed` at 10% on the horizon-only truth** (8 vs 10), the only truth nobody
had seen when the rules were written. **B2** holds: `signal` alone covers fewer strata than `stratified`, and `signal+floor` keeps ≥ 89% of
`signal`'s positives with full coverage. **B3** (`expert_informed` beats uniform) holds, but the rule is outcome-informed. The equal-weight
composite was worse than one of its own parts: the stationarity residual alone found 2.2× (10%) and 1.4× (25%) the composite's
positives, and s2 contributed nothing at 10%. s3 partly measures the same thing as the horizon truth (a state still changing at T),
so its advantage on that truth is partly by construction.

Reading (scoped): in this one pool, cheap ex-ante diagnostics beat random and cheapest-first allocation of verification effort by a
large factor, and a 20% exploration floor restored full coverage at little cost. Equal-weight signal aggregation, the default a voting
or scoring mechanism would use, diluted the one informative signal. None of this is evidence about community attention, votes or markets.

## What the acceptance criterion still requires
Pinned task pool with **separate interest / resource / blocking signals from real participants** (none exist: F005), preregistered
utility and costs (done here), expert-order and simple baselines (defined; run only in Arm 0), coverage and failed/negative work included
(defined; included in Arm 0). Blocked on: independent acceptance judgements (at most 6 of 48 jobs have any trace of one), ex-ante cost
declarations on future tasks, contributor diversity (F005), and a redundancy model for counterfactual replays.
