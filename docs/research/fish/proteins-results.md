### CORRECTION: the "disordered C-terminal tail" was an MSA-coverage artifact

I earlier read Boltz2's collapse beyond residue ~260 as a disordered cytoplasmic tail. That was
wrong, and the alignment itself shows why. Counting how many of the 71 PDB70 hits align at each
query position:

| region | mean MSA depth | Boltz2 pLDDT | ESMFold2 pLDDT |
|---|---|---|---|
| residues 1–250 | 21.4 | 77.5 | 89.2 |
| residues 261–448 | **0.5** | 41.4 | **76.4** |

MSA depth is **exactly 0.0 from residue 326 onward**. Crystallised K2P constructs are typically
truncated before the cytoplasmic tail, so PDB70 simply has no sequence there. Boltz2's low
confidence tracks *where its alignment ran out*, not where the protein is disordered — and
ESMFold2, which uses a protein language model and no MSA at all, is moderately confident (76.4)
across the same region.

What this does and does not establish: it removes the evidence I had offered for a disordered
tail. It does not prove the tail is ordered in vivo — 76.4 is moderate, and a single model's
confidence is not a measurement. The correct statement is that the Boltz2 signal was never
evidence about disorder.

This is also the scientific payoff of cracking the manifest schema: ESMFold2 was the model worth
reaching, and it is the only one of the three that is MSA-independent.
