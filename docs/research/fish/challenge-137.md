# Challenge-137: adversarial review of S10-D-D-D (frequency-matched muscle comparator)

**Claim under review:** S10-D-D-D, branch `claude/deepen-s10-d-d-juuuwn`, file `docs/research/fish/S10-D-D-deepen.md`. I did not write it.

**Claim's key number:** P_muscle at 21.9 Hz = 58–67 W/kg. The claim calculates this from the measured 2.63 J/kg per cycle at 40 Hz. It gives a bracket of 58–88 W/kg at 8% strain and a *strict upper bound* of 126 W/kg. Verdict claimed: **strengthens**. Recorded evidence level: derived.

**My script:** `docs/research/fish/challenge_137_calc.py`. Every number below that does not carry the label "measured" is a **calculation**.

**Outcome: WEAKENED.** The headline survives. The "strict upper bound" does not, because it rests on a strain the paper says was imposed on the preparation, not on the muscle.

## 2. The defect: the "strict upper bound" uses the wrong strain

I re-read Mead et al. 2020 (PMC7451861, pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov, read-level **full-text**, characters 0–100,000 of about 120,000). The last ~20 k characters, mostly references, were not read.

**What the authors say** (quoted from the full text):
- **Results:** "preparation length was varied by 8% sinusoidally". **Fig. 8 caption:** "An imposed, 80 Hz sinusoidal preparation length change."
- **Methods:** "sinusoidally varying the myotome length between 1.01 and 1.09 times the starting length". This contradicts the Results and the caption. I am keeping the disagreement, not resolving it.
- **Work used measured myotome length, not servo length:** "Myotome lengths were measured at every point in the cycle so that work loops could be generated by plotting force versus myotome length", and "we could not use the servo position, i.e., preparation length, as indicative of the myotomal fiber" because the clamped ends were compliant.
- **The only statement of actual myotome excursion:** the prestretch "resulted in myotome lengths being within ±5% of starting length during the work loops."

**My inference.** The 2.63 J/kg figure is work over a myotome strain the paper never reports as a number. The only constraint given is that myotome length stayed within about ±5%, i.e. up to about 10% peak-to-peak. The claim's rectangle σ₀ × 8% / ρ applies the *imposed preparation* strain to work that was measured on the *myotome*. That makes it a proxy, not a strict bound.

**Rectangular ceiling at 21.9 Hz, by myotome strain** (calculation):

| Myotome strain (p-p) | Ceiling | Implied 40 Hz loop fill |
|---|---|---|
| 6% | 94 W/kg | 0.61 |
| 8% (claim) | 126 W/kg | 0.46 |
| 10% (upper end of "±5%") | **157 W/kg** | 0.37 |

At 10% the ceiling is above both forward-flight flip thresholds (97 and 121 W/kg), and above the 134.4 W/kg comparator the claim replaced. The claim's KEY says "strict upper bound 126 W/kg" with no strain qualifier. At the strain the paper actually allows, the true ceiling is `unknown`, ≤ ~157 W/kg. The 88 W/kg "fill 0.70" reading and the "fill 0.77 at 8%" flip threshold carry the same strain proxy.

**Weak supporting hint** (calculation, unreliable model). I fitted a crude single-twitch work-loop model: a sin² activation pulse, Hill shortening force–velocity, twitch stress 0.91 × 76 kPa, 8% strain. It could not reach the measured 2.63 J/kg at 40 Hz; its best fit was 0.73–0.96 J/kg. The model failed calibration and I use **no number from it**. Its failure is consistent with the effective myotome strain, or the force, being larger than 8% × twitch stress implies. It does not show that.

## 3. Does the defect reverse the verdict?

No.
- The **central readings (58–67 W/kg)** come from *measured work per cycle* times frequency, and do not depend on the strain value.
- The **twitch-width data support the plateau reading.** *Authors' claim (abstract):* "full width at half-maximal stress: 11 ± 1 ms". At 21.9 Hz the shortening half-cycle is about 23 ms. *My inference:* a single twitch fills only about half of it, so the 22 Hz single-twitch work is unlikely to climb much above the 40 Hz value.
- So the hover doubling (×4.6 → ×9.2–10.6) and the forward-flight "fails" under the measured-data readings stand, on the claim's own assumptions.

What changes is the scope of the exception. The claim says the bound case "still flips" at upper f, C_D,pro ≤ 0.1. With the strain corrected, the idealised ceiling clears every forward-flight corner at upper f, including C_D,pro = 0.2 (168 W/kg needed against ≤ 157 available, ×1.07: it nearly flips). So the forward-flight conclusion is **less closed** than "strict 126" implies. It rests entirely on the measured-loop readings plus the single-twitch assumption.

## 4. Other attacks tried

**Proxy.**
- The claim uses single-twitch, 8%-imposed-strain larval myotome as the proxy for *maximal burst power of an adult pectoral muscle driven at 22 Hz*.
- Mead's 8% was chosen to protect the preparation and to sit "near to the predicted 10% in vivo strain", and was **not optimised**. Pulse number was fixed at one.
- A wing muscle would use multi-pulse bursts and its own optimal strain. Both push P(22 Hz) **up**, and neither is measured.
- The claim names both caveats. I add that the strain caveat also undermines its "strict" bound (§2).

**Hidden assumption: plateau as the floor.** If work per cycle *falls* below 40 Hz, P drops below 58 W/kg. That would strengthen the claim, not weaken it. I could not quantify it because my model failed (§2).

**Evidence level.** "Derived" is honest for the 58–67 W/kg values; the claim labels them calculations. The one mislabel is "strict upper bound": it is a bound *conditional on 8% myotome strain*, which is not what was measured.

**Source-internal disagreement, kept.**
- Mead's Discussion says "peak power was highest at a work loop frequency of 100 Hz". Its Results give maximal *net* power at 80 Hz.
- These are different metrics: peak shortening power is 498.9 W/kg at 80 Hz and 640.2 W/kg at 100 Hz.
- This does not affect the 22 Hz extrapolation.

**Counter-example search.** No new literature search was run. My scope was limited to the Mead 2020 full text above. I therefore have no independent 15–35 Hz zebrafish work-loop value, and that value stays `unknown`.

## 5. Corrected statement

P_muscle at 21.9 Hz is about **58–67 W/kg muscle** (calculated from measured work per cycle at 40 Hz, single twitch). The idealised rectangular ceiling is **not 126 W/kg strict**. It is σ₀ × ε_myotome / ρ × f with ε_myotome unreported (≤ ~10% p-p, per the authors' "±5%"), giving **≤ ~157 W/kg (calculation)**. The hover conclusion strengthens as claimed. The forward-flight corners are "fails" only under the measured-loop readings: all upper-f corners are open under the idealised ceiling, not just C_D,pro ≤ 0.1.
