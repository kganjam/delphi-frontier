**4 routes examined. 2 closed, 2 open.**

- **A. Gliding -- CLOSED.** It needs a lift coefficient near 7; the best steady value measured anywhere is 1.75. - **B. Powered flapping -- CLOSED.** It needs ~83 Hz; fish white locomotory muscle makes no power above ~12 Hz, and the superfast myosin that could reach it has never been sequenced in any animal. - **C. Latch-spring launch -- OPEN.** PROVISIONAL. The efficiency argument caps what fraction of STORED energy reaches the body, not how much is stored -- so it closes only if multi-contraction spring charging is impossible, which is unknown. The adversarial arm caught this. The number: **0.539 muscle-work-to-body-KE efficiency the launch requires even at a muscle fraction of 1.00, against a measured best of 0.39 for any fish fast-start and 0.10 for a spring-driven mechanical fish** (claim 960, `literature`).

- **D. Harvesting the wind -- OPEN.** OPEN, AND THE ONLY ROUTE WITH A POSITIVE RESULT. A wingless body with the same drag sustains in 0 of 2500 cases at every altitude floor, so this is lift, not advection. Not shown: indefinite flight, stall, structural loads, or roll-rate limits. The number: **12.5 m/s wind at 1 m above which the design SUSTAINS flight for the full 4 s simulation above 1 metre with net energy gain (50-75 of 100 controllers); 0 of 100 below it, and 0 wingless at every altitude floor** (claim 971, `simulated`).

## WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER

Each of these is a specific measurement that does not exist, not a judgement call. They are listed because a negative result should name its own falsifiers.

- **The absolute power of superfast muscle, in W/kg, at ~83 Hz.** The route needs ~53 W/kg. Young & Rome 2001 measured the frequency ceilings but the paper is outside PMC's open-access subset, established by an efetch fetch returning 5,799 bytes with no body against 157,519 for a control.

- **A sequenced superfast swimbladder myosin.** Claim 889: every sonic-fish myosin in GenBank is a partial CARDIAC chain of 237-255 aa. This is wet-lab work; no model can act on a sequence that does not exist.

- **Whether a run-up and the escape increment add in quadrature or linearly.** Three rounds of searching found no study comparing escape speed from a moving start against from rest.

- **Whether two fin-overgrowth alleles stack.** Claim 598: the only epistasis experiment is a REVERSION, and its authors favour an additive model, which would RAISE the 2.65-fold ceiling. No combination has been published.

- **Fin elastic storage, measured directly.** Claim 592 answered the question by resonance instead, because nobody has measured the storage fraction.
