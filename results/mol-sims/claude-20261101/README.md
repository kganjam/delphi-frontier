## Structure predictions on the Nebius Scientific AI MCP (`structures/`)
PDB70 MMseqs2 MSAs (`msa_search_native`; 14-122 sequences, structural database only). Boltz-2 (`boltz2_predict_native`, 3 diffusion samples, no seed accepted by the runtime). Per-model numbers are in `structures/summary.json`.

- **ShhN** (Shha 24-197, mature signalling domain): mean pLDDT **85.1** and pTM 0.82. OpenFold2 with a dummy MSA scored 40.2 on the same residues. This is the first confident fin-pathway structure here, and it was used as the MD start.
- **Fgf10a core (58-201) + Fgfr2 IIIb D2-D3 (F1RBT3 150-360)**: chain pLDDT 86.7/85.1, interface pLDDT 71.7, confidence 0.77, pTM 0.62 (best of 3). 18 of 38 receptor interface residues fall in the IIIb/IIIc alternative-exon segment (F1RBT3 314-358), which is 21 % of the construct.
- **Negative control, added after the argon review: Fgf10a + non-cognate Fgfr2 IIIc (Q8JG38 150-362).** It scored *higher* than the cognate complex: confidence 0.83-0.84, pTM 0.74-0.79, interface pLDDT 73-75. FGF10 binds FGFR2b, not FGFR2c, and Boltz-2 with this MSA does not reproduce that. **The FGF complex therefore says nothing about isoform specificity.** It is a plausible docking geometry for a ligand-receptor pair and no more. The exon-enrichment number above is not evidence of specificity. Templating from the human complex chains in PDB70 is the likely reason (argon).
- **Kcnk5b dimer (Q6PFU3 1-265 x2)**: Boltz-2 failed (`NATIVE_UPSTREAM_FAILED: All proteins with the same sequence must share the same MSA`) even though both chains carried identical A3M text (receipt in `structures/kcnk5b_dimer_boltz2_failed/`). Retried with OpenFold3 preview2 (seed 42, one sample): chain pLDDT 69.8 (OpenFold2 monomer 55.2 on the same residues), ipTM 0.48, pTM 0.56. Pore domains: 64.1 for 75-137 and 72.7 for 173-246 (OpenFold2: 44.1 and 57.4). The dimer interface is not confident (ipTM < 0.6), so no MD and no variant work were run on it.

- **Kcnj13 (Kir7.1, Q0KIZ7, 362 aa)** — the scale's one recorded structure gap (`openfold2-kcnj13`: 18 % of residues at pLDDT >= 70). Boltz-2 with the PDB70 MSA: mean pLDDT 67.7 and **53.9 % of residues >= 70** on the identical sequence (sha256 `8029209...`), pTM 0.45-0.49. The OpenFold3 **tetramer** attempt went the other way: chain pLDDT 45.8, ipTM 0.29, interface pLDDT 42.4. So the MSA improves the subunit but the assembly is still unresolved, and the open question on the studio's existing card ("does a tetrameric model change the confidence?") is answered **no, not with this MSA**.

## How much of this is template recall? (`structures/msa_identity.json`)
The argon review warned that a PDB70-only MSA invites template memorisation. Measured, per chain (closest hit ranked by identity x query coverage):

| chain | PDB70 hits | closest hit | identity | query coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ShhN | 13 | 6N7G_C | 95 % | 100 % |
| Fgf10a core | 43 | 1NUN_A | 64 % | 98 % |
| Fgfr2 IIIb D2-D3 | 121 | 3OJM_B | 78 % | 99 % |
| Fgfr2 IIIc D2-D3 (control) | 121 | 1IIL_F | 81 % | 99 % |
| Kcnk5b core | 75 | 6WLV_A | 64 % | 99 % |
| Kcnj13 | 28 | 3SYA_A | 37 % | 80 % |

Two consequences, both of which weaken earlier readings here:
- **ShhN's confidence is mostly recall.** Its closest hit is 95 % identical over the whole chain, so pLDDT 85 says "this matches a known Shh structure", not "this fold was derived de novo". The MD cross-check below is still informative, but the structure is not an independent finding.
- **The negative-control inversion has a mundane explanation.** The non-cognate IIIc construct's closest template (81 % over 99 %) is *closer* than the cognate IIIb one's (78 % over 99 %). Score order tracks template proximity, not isoform biology. That is the cleanest available account of why the control beat the real pair, and it is a reason to distrust the complex scores rather than to reinterpret them.
- Kcnj13 has the weakest template of the set (37 % over 80 %, 28 hits), which is the honest reason it stays the least resolved target even after adding an MSA.

## Molecular dynamics (`md_shhN/`, `md_shhN_rep2/`, `md_fgf_complex/`, `md_fgf_negctrl/`)
Run as Nebius AI jobs on one H100 each (OpenMM 8.6.1, CUDA mixed precision, amber14 + TIP3P-FB, PME 1.0 nm, HBonds, LangevinMiddle 301 K, 2 fs, 0.15 M NaCl). Minimise, 100 ps NVT, 200 ps NPT, then 10 ns production NPT with CA frames every 10 ps. One replica each. Results come back through the job log, which the service splits at 16 KiB **and can return the pieces out of order**; `collect_md.py` reassembles them and only accepts an ordering that parses as JSON (and that gunzips, for the final frame), so a scrambled log cannot silently become a wrong number.

- **ShhN apo, 10 ns** (469 ns/day, 54 508 atoms, all values finite): CA RMSD rises to 1.33 A mean over the first ns and 4.19 A over the last 2 ns, with Rg essentially unchanged (16.31 -> 15.71 A). The fold holds. **Correction:** an earlier version of this file read the last-2-ns slope (-0.11 A/ns) as a plateau. Measured over 5 ns blocks instead, both 10 ns replicas drift *upward* (+0.21 and +0.32 A/ns, `md_convergence_report.json`), so that negative figure was window noise. A 2 ns window is too short to read a slope from and nothing here should be quoted as a plateau. The useful number is the cross-check: per-residue CA RMSF **anti-correlates with the Boltz-2 pLDDT of the start model, Spearman rho = -0.63 (p = 2e-20)** — the residues the predictor was least sure about are the ones that move, and the 10 most mobile are the chain termini. That is an internal consistency check between two independent methods, not evidence that the fold is right.
- **ShhN replica 2 (seed 777000)** reproduces it: rho = -0.60 (p = 2e-18), and the two replicas agree on *which* residues move (per-residue RMSF correlates at rho = 0.91). They do **not** agree on convergence: the last-2-ns RMSD drift is -0.11 A/ns in replica 1 and **+0.98 A/ns** in replica 2 at the same RMSD (4.19 vs 4.17 A). So 10 ns is not converged, and the only claim these runs support is the *ranking* of mobile residues.

- **Fgf10a-Fgfr2b complex, 10 ns** (197 ns/day, 182 108 atoms, all finite): CA RMSD settles at 2.31 A over the last 2 ns and the two chains stay together, keeping **73 % of the 95 starting inter-chain CA contacts** (minimum 66 %; closest CA 4.4 A at the end). The ShhN cross-check appeared not to reproduce when pooled over both chains (rho = -0.10, p = 0.053), and that null is now **withdrawn** - see the stratified re-reading below.

- **Non-cognate Fgf10a-Fgfr2c control, 10 ns** (276 ns/day, 119 545 atoms, all finite), the specificity test: the wrong-isoform interface also holds. CA RMSD settles at 1.64 A over the last 2 ns and **62 % of its 68 starting inter-chain CA contacts survive** (minimum 56 %; closest CA 4.4 A at the end). Cognate 73 %/95 pairs vs non-cognate 62 %/68 pairs: the same range. **So 10 ns interface persistence does not discriminate the isoforms** - the only difference between the two systems is how many contacts the *prediction* started with, and the predictor had already failed its isoform control (IIIc scored higher than IIIb). Nothing here supports an FGF10-FGFR2b specificity claim. RMSF vs start pLDDT in the control is rho = -0.20 (p = 1e-4), between the monomer's -0.63 and the cognate complex's null, which does not rescue that correlation either.


### The flexibility/confidence correlation, split by chain (`md_complex_stratified.json`)
The round-2 review pointed out that pooling two chains into one rank test is the wrong test, and it was right. Splitting the same saved RMSF vectors by chain, the anticorrelation between per-residue flexibility and start-model pLDDT is **concentrated in the ligand chain's interface residues, and strong there in both complexes independently**: Fgf10a interface rho = **-0.70** (R2 0.49, n = 37, p = 2e-6) in the cognate complex and rho = **-0.70** (R2 0.50, n = 36, p = 2e-6) in the non-cognate control. 14 groups were tested; Bonferroni alpha is 0.0036, so both survive. The receptor chain shows nothing (rho +0.09 and +0.00) - it is built from a close template, scores uniformly high pLDDT and contributes almost no rank spread, which is what flattened the pooled statistic.

What this does **not** show: that the poses are right (the same pattern appears in the complex the predictor itself mis-ranked); that the relation is causal (low confidence and high flexibility may both follow from sparse MSA coverage); or that the motion is local, because RMSF was computed after superposing the whole complex, so per-chain rigid-body rocking is still inside it. Argon asked for per-chain realignment; that needs the frames, which the job log does not return, so it is not done and not claimed.

### Contact retention as a count, not a fraction
Also from the review: 73 % vs 62 % retention makes the two complexes look alike, but in absolute terms the cognate complex ends with **69.5 surviving inter-chain CA pairs against 42.4** for the non-cognate - 1.64x. Both numbers are now reported. The gap is inherited from the predicted starting poses (95 vs 68 initial pairs), not produced by the MD, and the predictor scored the non-cognate complex *higher*, so the gap cannot be read as isoform specificity without a template-free prediction to compare against.


### Was the control inversion just template proximity? No (`singleseq_control.json`)
Argon's round-2 review asked for a template-free refold, since the non-cognate IIIc construct has the closer PDB70 hit (81 %/99 % vs 78 %/99 %) and might be scoring higher for that reason alone. Both complexes were refolded with a **single-sequence a3m** (query only, no PDB70 search), everything else held fixed. Both lose confidence - cognate **0.767 -> 0.614**, non-cognate **0.844 -> 0.660** - but the non-cognate complex **still scores higher**, and the gap only narrows from 0.077 to 0.046. So the inversion is *not* explained by template proximity, and the earlier manifest line that named it as the likely cause has been corrected. The conclusion it was offered to explain is unchanged and now rests on firmer ground: these confidence scores carry no isoform-specificity signal.

The chains separate sharply without an alignment: the **Fgf10a ligand collapses** (-47.8 and -35.4 mean pLDDT) while the **Fgfr2 receptor barely moves** (-11.2 and -0.7, staying above 80). The receptor's D2-D3 immunoglobulin fold is common in the training set and is reconstructed from sequence alone; the ligand's confidence was the alignment-dependent part. Caveat, stated because it limits the control: a single-sequence input removes the MSA but **not** the model's learned structural memory, so this cannot exclude memorisation - only a genuinely template-free predictor could.


### Two 40 ns replicas: the ranking holds, convergence does not (`md_convergence_report.json`)
Two more ShhN replicas were run at **40 ns** (seeds 20261105/20261106, 467 ns/day each) to settle the convergence question the 10 ns pair left open. Both end on a near-flat final 5 ns block (**+0.06** and **-0.11 A/ns**), but that does not establish convergence and is not claimed as such: across the eight blocks of each run the block *means* wander by **3.00 A** and **2.80 A**, with the block slope changing sign 6 and 3 times. The wander is larger than any trend, so a flat final block is luck of the window and **RMSD plateau is not a usable convergence criterion for this system at this length**. Argon's objection - that a drifting protein can simply pause for 5 ns - is upheld by the data.

What does reproduce, now across **all four replicas**: flexibility vs start-model pLDDT at rho **-0.60 to -0.74** in every single run, and per-residue RMSF agreeing between **every pair** of replicas at rho **0.78 to 0.92**. The ranking of mobile residues is the robust result; the absolute RMSD level is not.

### A log-reassembly defect that produced a nonsense number (`md_log_verification.json`)
The first collection of the 40 ns runs reported a **926209 A RMSD and a negative radius of gyration**. Cause: the Nebius log service splits long lines at 16 KiB and returns the pieces out of order, and `collect_md.py` accepted any ordering that *parsed as JSON*. That is too weak - the summary's long arrays are all plain floats, so swapping two interior chunks still parses and silently splices potential energies (order -9e5 kJ/mol) into the RMSD and Rg arrays. A 10 ns run splits into 2-3 chunks, where a wrong order fails to parse; a 40 ns run splits into 7, where it does not. The reassembler now also checks that every array has the length the production time implies, that distances are positive and under 500 A, and that potential energies are negative. **All six MD runs were then re-derived from their saved logs under the stronger check and every one is CONFIRMED**, so no committed number was affected - the defect was caught by the one run that happened to be large enough to trigger it.
