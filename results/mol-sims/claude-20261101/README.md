## Structure predictions on the Nebius Scientific AI MCP (`structures/`)
PDB70 MMseqs2 MSAs (`msa_search_native`; 14-122 sequences, structural database only). Boltz-2 (`boltz2_predict_native`, 3 diffusion samples, no seed accepted by the runtime). Per-model numbers are in `structures/summary.json`.

- **ShhN** (Shha 24-197, mature signalling domain): mean pLDDT **85.1** and pTM 0.82. OpenFold2 with a dummy MSA scored 40.2 on the same residues. This is the first confident fin-pathway structure here, and it was used as the MD start.
- **Fgf10a core (58-201) + Fgfr2 IIIb D2-D3 (F1RBT3 150-360)**: chain pLDDT 86.7/85.1, interface pLDDT 71.7, confidence 0.77, pTM 0.62 (best of 3). 18 of 38 receptor interface residues fall in the IIIb/IIIc alternative-exon segment (F1RBT3 314-358), which is 21 % of the construct.
- **Negative control, added after the argon review: Fgf10a + non-cognate Fgfr2 IIIc (Q8JG38 150-362).** It scored *higher* than the cognate complex: confidence 0.83-0.84, pTM 0.74-0.79, interface pLDDT 73-75. FGF10 binds FGFR2b, not FGFR2c, and Boltz-2 with this MSA does not reproduce that. **The FGF complex therefore says nothing about isoform specificity.** It is a plausible docking geometry for a ligand-receptor pair and no more. The exon-enrichment number above is not evidence of specificity. Templating from the human complex chains in PDB70 is the likely reason (argon).
- **Kcnk5b dimer (Q6PFU3 1-265 x2)**: Boltz-2 failed (`NATIVE_UPSTREAM_FAILED: All proteins with the same sequence must share the same MSA`) even though both chains carried identical A3M text (receipt in `structures/kcnk5b_dimer_boltz2_failed/`). Retried with OpenFold3 preview2 (seed 42, one sample): chain pLDDT 69.8 (OpenFold2 monomer 55.2 on the same residues), ipTM 0.48, pTM 0.56. Pore domains: 64.1 for 75-137 and 72.7 for 173-246 (OpenFold2: 44.1 and 57.4). The dimer interface is not confident (ipTM < 0.6), so no MD and no variant work were run on it.

- **Kcnj13 (Kir7.1, Q0KIZ7, 362 aa)** — the scale's one recorded structure gap (`openfold2-kcnj13`: 18 % of residues at pLDDT >= 70). Boltz-2 with the PDB70 MSA: mean pLDDT 67.7 and **53.9 % of residues >= 70** on the identical sequence (sha256 `8029209...`), pTM 0.45-0.49. The OpenFold3 **tetramer** attempt went the other way: chain pLDDT 45.8, ipTM 0.29, interface pLDDT 42.4. So the MSA improves the subunit but the assembly is still unresolved, and the open question on the studio's existing card ("does a tetrameric model change the confidence?") is answered **no, not with this MSA**.

## How much of this is template recall? (`structures/msa_identity.json`)
The argon review warned that a PDB70-only MSA invites template memorisation. Measured, per chain (closest hit ranked by identity x query coverage):

| chain | PDB70 hits | closest hit | identity | query coverage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ShhN | 13 | 6N7G_C | 95 % | 100 % |
| Fgf10a core | 43 | 1NUN_A | 64 % | 98 % |
| Fgfr2 IIIb D2-D3 | 121 | 3OJM_B | 78 % | 99 % |
| Fgfr2 IIIc D2-D3 (control) | 121 | 1IIL_F | 81 % | 99 % |
| Kcnk5b core | 75 | 6WLV_A | 64 % | 99 % |
| Kcnj13 | 28 | 3SYA_A | 37 % | 80 % |

Two consequences, both of which weaken earlier readings here:
- **ShhN's confidence is mostly recall.** Its closest hit is 95 % identical over the whole chain, so pLDDT 85 says "this matches a known Shh structure", not "this fold was derived de novo". The MD cross-check below is still informative, but the structure is not an independent finding.
- **The negative-control inversion has a mundane explanation.** The non-cognate IIIc construct's closest template (81 % over 99 %) is *closer* than the cognate IIIb one's (78 % over 99 %). Score order tracks template proximity, not isoform biology. That is the cleanest available account of why the control beat the real pair, and it is a reason to distrust the complex scores rather than to reinterpret them.
- Kcnj13 has the weakest template of the set (37 % over 80 %, 28 hits), which is the honest reason it stays the least resolved target even after adding an MSA.

## Molecular dynamics (`md_shhN/`, `md_shhN_rep2/`, `md_fgf_complex/`, `md_fgf_negctrl/`)
Run as Nebius AI jobs on one H100 each (OpenMM 8.6.1, CUDA mixed precision, amber14 + TIP3P-FB, PME 1.0 nm, HBonds, LangevinMiddle 301 K, 2 fs, 0.15 M NaCl). Minimise, 100 ps NVT, 200 ps NPT, then 10 ns production NPT with CA frames every 10 ps. One replica each. Results come back through the job log, which the service splits at 16 KiB **and can return the pieces out of order**; `collect_md.py` reassembles them and only accepts an ordering that parses as JSON (and that gunzips, for the final frame), so a scrambled log cannot silently become a wrong number.

- **ShhN apo, 10 ns** (469 ns/day, 54 508 atoms, all values finite): CA RMSD rises to a plateau — 1.33 A mean over the first ns, 4.19 A over the last 2 ns, with a **negative** drift of -0.11 A/ns, and Rg essentially unchanged (16.31 -> 15.71 A). The fold holds. The useful number is the cross-check: per-residue CA RMSF **anti-correlates with the Boltz-2 pLDDT of the start model, Spearman rho = -0.63 (p = 2e-20)** — the residues the predictor was least sure about are the ones that move, and the 10 most mobile are the chain termini. That is an internal consistency check between two independent methods, not evidence that the fold is right.
- **ShhN replica 2 (seed 777000)** reproduces it: rho = -0.60 (p = 2e-18), and the two replicas agree on *which* residues move (per-residue RMSF correlates at rho = 0.91). They do **not** agree on convergence: the last-2-ns RMSD drift is -0.11 A/ns in replica 1 and **+0.98 A/ns** in replica 2 at the same RMSD (4.19 vs 4.17 A). So 10 ns is not converged, and the only claim these runs support is the *ranking* of mobile residues.

- **Fgf10a-Fgfr2b complex, 10 ns** (197 ns/day, 182 108 atoms, all finite): CA RMSD settles at 2.31 A over the last 2 ns and the two chains stay together, keeping **73 % of the 95 starting inter-chain CA contacts** (minimum 66 %; closest CA 4.4 A at the end). But the ShhN cross-check **did not reproduce**: RMSF vs start pLDDT gives rho = -0.10 (p = 0.053), i.e. nothing. One significant correlation on one monomer is not a general property, and it should not be quoted as one.

- **Non-cognate Fgf10a-Fgfr2c control, 10 ns** (276 ns/day, 119 545 atoms, all finite), the specificity test: the wrong-isoform interface also holds. CA RMSD settles at 1.64 A over the last 2 ns and **62 % of its 68 starting inter-chain CA contacts survive** (minimum 56 %; closest CA 4.4 A at the end). Cognate 73 %/95 pairs vs non-cognate 62 %/68 pairs: the same range. **So 10 ns interface persistence does not discriminate the isoforms** - the only difference between the two systems is how many contacts the *prediction* started with, and the predictor had already failed its isoform control (IIIc scored higher than IIIb). Nothing here supports an FGF10-FGFR2b specificity claim. RMSF vs start pLDDT in the control is rho = -0.20 (p = 1e-4), between the monomer's -0.63 and the cognate complex's null, which does not rescue that correlation either.
