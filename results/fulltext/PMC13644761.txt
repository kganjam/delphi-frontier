INTRODUCTION The maintenance of calcium ion (Ca 2+ ) homeostasis is a fundamental physiological imperative for all vertebrates ( 1 , 2 ). As a critical intracellular messenger and a key structural component of bone and exoskeletons, calcium participates in a vast array of biological processes, from neuronal signaling and muscle contraction to cell proliferation and gene regulation ( 3 ). Consequently, systemic calcium concentrations are maintained within a narrow range; in mammals, deviations can lead to severe pathologies such as hypercalcemia or hypocalcemia ( 4 ). The canonical endocrine pathway regulating this balance in terrestrial vertebrates centers on the active hormone 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D 3 and its nuclear receptor (VDR), which orchestrates transcriptional programs for active calcium uptake primarily in the intestine ( 5 , 6 ). For aquatic vertebrates, the physiological challenge of calcium regulation is fundamentally different. The gill, a large, permeable interface with the environment, serves as the principal site of ionic exchange, placing fish under direct and constant influence of ambient calcium concentrations ( 7 ). While core molecular components of calcium transport, including VDR and its downstream effector, the calcium-binding protein calbindin, which are often conserved across vertebrates ( 8 ), the evolutionary genetic mechanisms that enable fish to maintain homeostasis in environments with extreme and natural variation in dissolved calcium remain poorly understood. This gap in knowledge presents a powerful opportunity to study genetic adaptation to a physiologically extreme, yet naturally occurring, environmental gradient. The iconic turquoise lakes of the Jiuzhaigou Nature Reserve on the eastern Tibetan Plateau provide an ideal model system. These karst lakes, formed by the dissolution of calcium carbonate, are characterized by extreme hypercalcemia, with dissolved calcium concentrations around three times higher than in the adjacent rivers that feed them ( 9 ). A freshwater fish species, the Jialing fish ( Schizopygopsis kialingensis ), thrives in both these calcium-saturated lakes and the normocalcemic rivers. This juxtaposition of divergent habitats inhabited by a single species offers a natural experiment to investigate the genetic and physiological basis of adaptation to chronic ionoregulatory stress. In this study, we demonstrate that the lake-adapted population of Jialing fish has evolved a unique solution to this challenge. RESULTS Jialing fish maintain serum calcium homeostasis in a hypercalcemic environment To investigate the mechanisms of calcium regulation, we sampled Jialing fish ( S. kialingensis ) from two contrasting habitats: the calcium-saturated karst lakes of Jiuzhaigou (Sichuan, China) and the normocalcemic rivers of Longnan (Gansu, China) ( Fig. 1, A and B ). Genome sequencing and assembly provided a reference for this species (fig. S1 and table S1). Population genomic analysis based on whole-genome resequencing confirmed that the lake (Jiuzhaigou) and river (Longnan) populations form distinct evolutionary lineages ( Fig. 1C ). Mitochondrial cox1 sequences suggested an estimated divergence time of ∼410,000 years ( Fig. 1D ). To further validate the population differentiation pattern, we sequenced an additional mitochondrial marker, cytochrome b ( cytb ), from the same individuals. The cytb -based phylogenetic tree yielded a topology fully consistent with that of cox1 , confirming the reciprocal monophyly of the lake (Jiuzhaigou) and river (Longnan) populations (fig. S2). Globally, the median calcium concentration in freshwater is ∼0.10 mM ( 10 ). Typical freshwater lakes have calcium levels ranging from 0.44 to 1.0 mM (Qinghai–Tibet Plateau lakes) ( 11 ). Our measurements in the Jiuzhaigou lakes are consistent with previous studies reporting ∼2.35 mM ( 12 ), nearly 20 times the global median and 2 to 3 times higher than the upper range of the typical alpine lakes. In contrast, the Longnan rivers show calcium levels comparable to these alpine lakes. Thus, the Jiuzhaigou lakes represent an extreme end to the natural calcium gradient. As expected, dissolved calcium concentrations in Jiuzhaigou lakes [high-calcium Jiuzhaigou (HJ)] were around three times higher than in Longnan rivers [low-calcium Longnan (LL)] ( Fig. 1E ). Despite this stark environmental disparity, serum calcium levels were indistinguishable between the two populations ( Fig. 1F ), demonstrating that lake-adapted fish maintain systemic calcium homeostasis despite chronic exposure to hypercalcemic water. This acute exposure resulted in 83% mortality within 10 days, compared with only 20% mortality in native low-calcium conditions ( Fig. 2A ). Given the gill’s primary role in ionic exchange in fish, we performed comparative transcriptomic analysis of gill tissue from both populations. Histological examination revealed no gross morphological differences ( Fig. 2C ). However, RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) identified key functional disparities. Enrichment analysis showed that differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and their functional categories were predominantly associated with mineral absorption and endocrine and other factor-regulated calcium reabsorption (figs. S3 and S4 and table S2). To prioritize candidate genes for follow-up analysis, we focused on DEGs with known functions in calcium homeostasis and membership in conserved vertebrate endocrine pathways. Among these, vdrb emerged as the top candidate, exhibiting the most pronounced down-regulation and a well-established role as a master regulator of systemic calcium homeostasis in vertebrates ( Fig. 2D ). Quantitative PCR (qPCR) validated this marked suppression of vdrb expression ( Fig. 2E ), suggesting an adaptive dampening of the vitamin D signaling pathway. Genetic ablation of vdrb confers hypercalcemia tolerance in zebrafish To test the causal role of reduced vitamin D signaling, we used a vdrb knockout zebrafish line established by CRISPR-Cas9 ( 13 ). Successful knockout was confirmed by sequencing and qPCR ( Fig. 3A ). Wild-type (WT) and mutant zebrafish larvae ( n = 20 per genotype) at 3 days postfertilization were placed in six-well plates containing high-calcium (10 mM) water and monitored until 9 days postfertilization, during which WT larvae experienced progressive mortality reaching 50% lethality, whereas vdrb mutants exhibited only 10% mortality over the same period ( Fig. 3B ). Alizarin red staining at 8 days postfertilization of larvae ( n = 20 per genotype) revealed markedly reduced calcium deposition in the gills of vdrb mutants compared with those of WT siblings ( Fig. 3, C and D ), indicating reduced branchial calcium uptake. These results demonstrate that intact Vdrb signaling is detrimental under hypercalcemic conditions and that its loss likely underlies the adaptation observed in Jiuzhaigou fish. Fig. 3. Calb2a is a key VDR-responsive effector mediating branchial calcium uptake Comparative transcriptomics of Jialing fish gills identified calbindin2a ( calb2a ) as the most suppressed canonical VDR target gene in lake-adapted fish ( Fig. 4A ). Luciferase reporter assays using the promoter of the river fish calb2a gene confirmed direct vitamin D–dependent transactivation of calb2a ( Fig. 4B ), and treatment of zebrafish with 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D 3 induced calb2a expression in a dose-dependent manner ( n = 20 per genotype) ( Fig. 4C ). Critically, CRISPR-Cas9 knockout of calb2a in zebrafish ( Fig. 4D ) phenocopied the vdrb mutant, conferring a substantial survival advantage when larvae ( n = 20 per genotype) were placed in six-well plates containing high-calcium (10 mM) water from 1 to 7 days postfertilization ( Fig. 4, E and F ). These results establish Calb2a as the primary VDR-dependent mediator of transcellular calcium transport in fish gills and demonstrate that its suppression is sufficient to drive hypercalcemia tolerance. A cis-regulatory mutation drives adaptive vdrb down-regulation To identify the genetic cause of vdrb suppression, we sequenced the coding and promoter/untranslated regions of vdrb but found no difference between Longnan and Jiuzhaigou Jialing fish (fig. S5). In silico analysis predicted a conserved enhancer region ∼5 kb upstream of the vdrb transcription start site ( Fig. 5A ). The location of this enhancer is conserved among different species ( Fig. 5B ). The sequence in core regions is also conserved in different species (fig. S6), suggesting its importance. To further validate the regulatory potential of this region, we examined publicly available chromatin datasets from mouse and human. A conserved enhancer ∼6 kb upstream of the VDR transcription start site has been identified in both species, which exhibited VDR/RXR binding, 1,25(OH) 2 D 3 -inducible activity, and histone H4 (H4) acetylation upon hormone treatment ( 14 , 15 ). Within this enhancer, Jiuzhaigou fish harbored three derived single-nucleotide substitutions not found in the Longnan population ( Fig. 5A ). To test the functional impact of these variants, we used a dual-strategy approach in zebrafish. First, we generated an enhancer knockout line by using CRISPR-Cas9 system ( Fig. 5C ), which confirmed the region’s necessity for endogenous vdrb expression. Second, we performed in vivo reporter assays by injecting zebrafish embryos with a cytomegalovirus (CMV)–enhanced green fluorescent protein (EGFP) plasmid driven by either the Longnan or Jiuzhaigou enhancer variant ( Fig. 5D ). Quantitative enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) of green fluorescent protein fluorescence revealed that the Jiuzhaigou enhancer drove significantly weaker expression than the Longnan variant ( Fig. 5, E and F ). Furthermore, we generated point mutations corresponding to mutations in loci 1 to 3 of the Longnan fish vdrb enhancer. Of interest, mutations at loci 1 and 3 showed 20 and 50% reduction in endogenous vdrb enhancer activity, respectively ( Fig. 5G ). Moreover, mutations at loci 1 and 3 showed 60% reduction in endogenous vdrb enhancer activity. These results demonstrate that recent cis-regulatory mutations in a critical vdrb enhancer are responsible for its adaptive down-regulation in the lake population. DISCUSSION The maintenance of calcium homeostasis represents a fundamental physiological constraint on vertebrate evolution. The Jialing fish presents a powerful natural model to explore this constraint: populations thriving in the hypercalcemic karst lakes of Jiuzhaigou maintain serum calcium levels identical to those of conspecifics in adjacent normocalcemic rivers. Contrary to expectations of fine-tuning, our study reveals that adaptation to this extreme environment was achieved through the functional loss of the canonical calcium-regulatory pathway. We identify cis-regulatory mutations that suppress vitamin D receptor ( vdrb ) expression in the gill, leading to the downstream silencing of the critical calcium transporter calb2a , as the mechanistic basis for hypercalcemia tolerance ( Fig. 6 ). This work illustrates how the evolutionary dismantling of a deeply conserved endocrine circuit can facilitate colonization of a physiologically challenging niche. Fig. 6. In terrestrial vertebrates, the vitamin D endocrine system is the principal regulator of systemic calcium balance, with VDR orchestrating active intestinal absorption ( 16 ). In fish, however, the gill serves as the primary site of ionic exchange, directly exposing this regulatory axis to environmental selection. Our finding that adaptation is associated with the near-complete transcriptional silencing of vdrb in gill tissue, causally validated by enhanced survival of vdrb knockout zebrafish in hypercalcemic water, demonstrates a “less-is-more” evolutionary strategy. Rather than modulating pathway activity, natural selection has favored its outright decommissioning in the face of chronic environmental calcium overload, where active uptake becomes detrimental ( 17 ). We further establish Calbindin2a (Calb2a) as the key VDR-responsive effector mediating transcellular calcium transport in the fish gill, a functional parallel to its role in mammalian intestine ( 18 ). In Jiuzhaigou fish, calb2a expression is suppressed by more than 99%, resulting from the loss of upstream Vdr signaling. The direct vitamin D inducibility of calb2a and the recapitulation of the hypercalcemia-tolerant phenotype in calb2a knockout zebrafish confirm that suppression of this transporter constitutes the functional endpoint of adaptation. This positions calb2a as a pivotal, lineage-specific mediator whose down-regulation effectively decouples systemic calcium homeostasis from environmental concentration. The genetic basis of this adaptive shift lies in cis-regulatory evolution. We traced the suppression of vdrb not to coding mutations but to recent point substitutions within a conserved enhancer element. Reporter assays confirmed that the lake-fish haplotype drives significantly weaker expression, directly linking genotype to the adaptive expression phenotype. This mechanism aligns with a growing paradigm in evolutionary genetics ( 19 ), wherein changes in gene expression and not coding sequences allow rapid beak changes in the Darwin’s finches ( 20 ). Likewise, cis-regulatory changes enable rapid, tissue-specific adaptation while preserving the pleiotropic functions of key proteins, exemplified by an enhancer of Agouti that contributes to parallel evolution of cryptically colored beach mice ( 21 ), and pelvic reduction in sticklebacks by recurrent deletion of a pitx1 enhancer ( 22 ). The estimated ∼410,000-year age of the vdrb enhancer mutation suggests that it arose and fixed rapidly following lake colonization, highlighting how single regulatory variants can catalyze niche expansion into extreme environments. Beyond its evolutionary implications, this discovery bridges fundamental physiology and practical application. In human medicine, hypercalcemia, whether due to endocrine disorders or malignancy, remains a clinical challenge with limited therapeutic options ( 23 ). Our findings suggest that targeted, transient inhibition of the VDR/CALB2 axis could offer a potential therapeutic strategy to mitigate pathological calcium uptake. Some recent studies have already shown that inhibitors that selectively target VDR can serve as potential therapeutic strategies for hypercalcemia ( 24 ). In aquaculture, where transferring species between water sources of differing hardness often induces ionoregulatory stress ( 25 ), modulating this pathway through selective breeding or pharmacological intervention, could enhance resilience and productivity in variable calcium environments. In summary, we elucidate a vertebrate adaptation in which colonization of a hypercalcemic niche was achieved not through gain-of-function innovation, but through the strategic loss of a conserved regulatory pathway. The Jialing fish demonstrates that evolutionary “progress” can involve the decommissioning of systems that become liabilities in new environments. This study underscores the power of naturally occurring extreme environments to reveal fundamental principles of homeostasis, adaptation, and the predictable genetic routes to evolutionary change. MATERIALS AND METHODS Study organisms and sampling Wild Jialing fish ( S. kialingensis ) were collected during summer expeditions in 2022–2024 from two locations: the high-calcium karst lakes of Jiuzhaigou (Sichuan Province) and the low-calcium rivers of Longnan (Gansu Province), China ( Fig. 1A ). Approximately 200 individuals were captured using Sherman live traps. For each fish, blood was collected from the caudal vein, allowed to clot at room temperature, and centrifuged at 4°C to isolate serum, which was stored at −80°C. Gill tissues were immediately dissected after euthanasia. One subsample was fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for histology; the remainder was snap frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80°C for molecular analysis. WT AB strain zebrafish were obtained from the China Zebrafish Resource Center. The vdrb mutant line was provided by Z. Yin (Chinese Academy of Sciences). Fish were maintained at 28° ± 1°C on a 14:10-hour light:dark cycle and fed newly hatched brine shrimp (Brine Shrimp Direct). All experiments were approved by the Animal Experimentation Ethics Committee of Northwest Normal University. Genomic and transcriptomic sequencing High–molecular-weight genomic DNA was extracted from fin clips of adult Jialing fish [ n = 10 per population: 10 from Jiuzhaigou lake (HJ) and 10 from Longnan river (LL)] using the CTAB method and purified (Grandomics Genomic kit). Nucleic acid quality was assessed via agarose gel electrophoresis, NanoDrop spectrophotometry, and Qubit fluorometry. DNA libraries were constructed with the MGIEasy Universal DNA Library Prep Kit and sequenced on a DNBSEQ-T7RS platform by Wuhan Onemore-tech Co. Ltd. Gill tissues were collected from adult Jialing fish [ n = 3 per population: 3 from Jiuzhaigou lake (HJ) and 3 from Longnan river (LL)], with a balanced sex ratio (two males and one female) within each group. All individuals were sexually mature adults (body length, 8 to 10 cm; age estimated at 2 to 3 years). No experimental treatments were applied before tissue collection as the aim was to capture the basal gene expression differences between wild populations under native environmental conditions. Total RNA was isolated from gill tissue using TRIzol reagent (TIANGEN), with tissue ground on dry ice before extraction. Nucleic acid quality was assessed via agarose gel electrophoresis, NanoDrop spectrophotometry, and Qubit fluorometry. For RNA-seq, poly-A enrichment was performed using the Dynabeads mRNA Purification Kit (Invitrogen), followed by library preparation with the MGIEasy RNA Library Prep Kit V3.1 and sequencing on the DNBSEQ-T7RS platform. Calcium assessment Water calcium concentrations were analyzed by inductively coupled plasma mass spectrometry (ICP-MS). Serum total calcium was measured using a colorimetric o -cresolphthalein complexone assay (Calcium Colorimetric Assay Kit, Beyotime) according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Absorbance was read at 575 nm, and concentrations were determined from a standard curve. Histological analysis Gill tissues fixed in paraformaldehyde were dehydrated, paraffin embedded, sectioned at 6 μm, and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. Images were acquired using a Leica microscope. Calcium tolerance tests Longnan Jialing fish ( n = 20 per group) were exposed to either high-calcium (10 mM) or low-calcium (0.3 mM) water. Mortality was recorded daily for 10 days with 50% water changes daily. For zebrafish testing, WT, vdrb −/− , and calb2a −/− larvae ( n = 20 per well, six replicates) were placed in six-well plates containing high-calcium (10 mM) or low-calcium (0.3 mM) water. Mortality was monitored daily with 50% water renewal. Survival curves were generated and analyzed in GraphPad Prism 9.0. Alizarin red staining Zebrafish larvae (8 days postfertilization) were fixed in 4% PFA, bleached in 2% KOH/3% H 2 O 2 , and stained overnight in 0.05% Alizarin red solution. After washing, specimens were imaged under a stereomicroscope. Molecular biology techniques cDNA was synthesized from 1 μg total RNA (first-strand cDNA synthesis kit, Takara). qPCR was performed using SYBR Green master mix (Toyobo) on an ABI real-time system. Primer sequences are listed in table S3. Vitamin D3 treatment Zebrafish embryos (10 hours postfertilization) were exposed to 1,25-dihydroxyvitamin D 3 (VD 3 ; 0, 20, 40, or 80 ng/liter) or vehicle control (ethanol) for 5 days with daily solution renewal ( n = 20 per group). Expression of calb2a was analyzed by using reverse transcription (RT)–qPCR. Luciferase reporter assay The calb2a promoter was cloned into pGL3-Basic and coinjected with pRL-TK (20:1 ratio) into one-cell zebrafish embryos. Luciferase activity was measured at 48 hours postfertilization using the Dual-Luciferase Reporter Assay System (Promega). EGFP levels in zebrafish tissues were quantified using a double-antibody sandwich ELISA kit (MeiMian, China). Genetic manipulations in zebrafish calb2a and enhancer of vdrb mutants were generated by injecting one-cell embryos with a mixture of single guide RNA (sgRNA; synthesized by GenScript, China) and Cas9 protein (Takara), as previously described ( 23 ). The calb2a and enhancer of vdrb sgRNA sequence were listed in table S4. Mutants were validated by sequencing and qPCR. The vdrb enhancer region was amplified from Jialing fish genomic DNA and cloned into the pcDNA3.1(+)-c-EGFP vector using Mfe I and Nru I sites. Constructs were validated by Sanger sequencing. Plasmid DNA (50 ng/μl) was injected into one-cell zebrafish embryos. EGFP expression at 48 hours postfertilization was quantified using a commercial ELISA kit (MeiMian) according to the manufacturer’s instructions.
