Background Bevacizumab, an anti-angiogenic monoclonal antibody targeting vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF), has demonstrated significant clinical value in ovarian cancer (OC) therapy by inhibiting tumor angiogenesis, which prolongs progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS) [ 1 ]. However, acquired resistance to bevacizumab remains a critical clinical challenge [ 2 ]. Emerging evidence indicates that the therapy resistance mechanisms are closely linked to dynamic alterations in the tumor microenvironment (TME) [ 3 ]. Following VEGF blockade by bevacizumab, hypoxia within OC tissues triggers compensatory metabolic reprogramming, characterized by activation of hypoxia-inducible factor 1α (HIF-1α) and subsequent enhancement of glycolytic activity [ 4 ]. This metabolic shift leads to excessive lactate accumulation, creating an acidic TME [ 5 ]. Lactate demonstrates multiple regulatory functions in tumor biology: it modulates the TME through paracrine signaling to enhance DNA damage repair, angiogenesis, and immune evasion, while simultaneously driving vascular mimicry (VM) via lactylation modifications [ 6 ]. The VM involves tumor cells secreting extracellular matrix components to form vascular-like networks that circumvent bevacizumab-mediated vascular suppression [ 7 ]. Elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying these lactate-driven pathways in OC cells is critical for developing combinatorial strategies to overcome bevacizumab resistance and improve clinical outcomes. Endothelial cell-specific molecule 1 (ESM1), a secreted oncoprotein, plays pivotal roles in regulating angiogenesis and tumor metabolic reprogramming [ 8 , 9 ]. Our previous studies demonstrated that lactate upregulates ESM1 mRNA transcription and expression, thereby inducing OC chemoresistance and immune evasion [ 10 ]. Furthermore, hypoxia promotes ESM1 expression, which facilitates PKM2 SUMOylation to accelerate OC glycolipid metabolic remodeling and ultimately drive VM [ 11 ]. Notably, bevacizumab therapy frequently induces hypoxia and lactate accumulation in OC cells [ 9 , 12 , 13 ]. Therefore, we hypothesize that ESM1 serves as a critical mediator of bevacizumab resistance in OC by orchestrating hypoxia-lactate-metabolic reprogramming axes. Lactylation, a novel post-translational modification of proteins, involves the covalent bonding of lactate (derived from glycolysis) to lysine residues (ε-amino groups) under enzymatic or non-enzymatic conditions [ 14 , 15 ]. This modification is categorized into histone lactylation (regulating gene transcription via epigenetic mechanisms) and non-histone lactylation (modulating protein function through structural or interactional changes) [ 16 ]. Previous studies demonstrated that bevacizumab, an anti-VEGF agent, induces hypoxia-driven lactate accumulation, leading to histone H3 lysine 18 lactylation (H3K18la) in colorectal cancer, which promotes autophagy and chemoresistance [ 17 , 18 ]. Our prior study revealed that ESM1 regulates autophagy and metabolic reprogramming in OC by altering the transcriptional landscape [ 19 ]. However, the molecular mechanism by which ESM1 orchestrates lactate-mediated transcriptional regulation of downstream genes in the hypoxic OC microenvironment remains incompletely understood. In this study, we observed elevated levels of VM and lactylation modifications in OC patient samples with acquired resistance to bevacizumab therapy. Mechanistically, ESM1-mediated H4K8la emerged as a critical mediator of this phenomenon. Both in vitro and in vivo experiments demonstrated that pharmacological inhibition of ESM1 or lactate production significantly suppressed VM formation in OC. At the molecular level, ESM1 facilitated ERBB2 transcription through H4K8la modification, subsequently forming a ternary complex with ERBB2 and EGFR to activate the Akt/mTOR pathway. This cascade not only suppressed autophagy but also activated the FAK/STAT3 signaling axis, collectively promoting VM progression. Strikingly, co-targeting ESM1 with ERBB2 inhibitors (Trastuzumab) or lactate inhibitors (2DG) in xenograft models synergistically enhanced OC cell sensitivity to bevacizumab. Our findings elucidate the ESM1-H4K8la-ERBB2 axis as a novel mechanism driving VM in bevacizumab-resistant OC and provide a rational strategy to overcome therapeutic resistance through combinatorial targeting lactation-associated epigenetic reprogramming and receptor tyrosine kinase signaling. Methods Patients and specimens From 2010 to 2025, 120 surgical resection specimens of OC were retrospectively collected from The Affiliated Zhuzhou Hospital of Xiangya Medical College. Moreover, 34 patients received first-line systemic therapy combining bevacizumab (15 mg/kg, intravenous infusion, every 3 weeks) with chemotherapy following disease progression. Tumor response was evaluated at baseline and every 6 weeks thereafter using computed tomography (CT) or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) of the abdomen, pelvis, and chest, in accordance with the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST) version 1.1. Clinical responses were categorized as complete response (CR), partial response (PR), stable disease (SD), or progressive disease (PD). Overall survival (OS) was defined as the time from the date of surgery to the date of death from any cause (or censored at the last follow-up). In this study, OC patients achieving CR, PR, or SD were classified into the sensitive group. Bevacizumab resistance was defined as either disease progression within the first 3 months of treatment (progressive disease type) or SD lasting less than 6 months (stable disease type). Written informed consent for the use of imaging and clinical data was obtained from all patients prior to initiating first-line therapy. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of The Affiliated Zhuzhou Hospital of Xiangya Medical School (Zhuzhou, China; Approval No. LLYPJ2025004-01). Cell lines and cell cultures OC cells (SKOV3 and A2780) and HEK293T cells were acquired from the American Type Culture Collection (ATCC; Manassas, VA, USA) and cultured under recommended conditions in complete media: McCoy’s 5 A medium (SKOV3), DMEM (HEK293T), or RPMI 1640 (A2780), supplemented with 10% (v/v) fetal bovine serum (FBS; Gibco, Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA) and 1% penicillin/streptomycin (GIBCO, USA). All cell lines used in this study were authenticated via short tandem repeat (STR) analysis and confirmed negative for mycoplasma contamination prior to experimentation. Results High lactylation level is associated with Bevacizumab resistance and correlated with poor prognosis in OC patients To investigate the mechanisms underlying acquired resistance to bevacizumab in OC cells, we collected fresh primary surgical specimens from untreated OC patients and recurrent tumor tissues from patients who developed resistance to first-line bevacizumab therapy. ELISA analysis revealed significantly elevated lactate levels in recurrent OC tissues compared to treatment-sensitive cases (Fig. 1 A), suggesting enhanced glycolysis in bevacizumab-resistant tumors. Subsequent quantification of glycolytic enzyme expression demonstrated marked upregulation of PKM, LDHA, LDHB, PGAM1, PFK1, PDK1, ENO1, ENO2, and HK2 transcripts in bevacizumab resistant samples (Fig. 1 B), confirming a correlation between glycolytic activation and bevacizumab resistance. Emerging evidence indicates that lactate participates in epigenetic regulation via histone lactylation and modulates protein stability/activity [ 16 , 20 ]. Building on our previous finding that lactate induces ESM1 expression [ 10 ], which promotes VM under hypoxic conditions [ 11 ], we performed immunohistochemical analysis of ESM1, lactate metabolism markers, and VM structures. Strikingly, resistant tumors exhibited heightened VM accompanied by elevated ESM1, PKM2, LDHA, H4K8la, and pan-Kla levels (Fig. 1 C). Comparative analysis further demonstrated significantly higher ESM1, pan-Kla, H4K8la and VE-cad (Endothelial marker) levels in bevacizumab resistant versus sensitive patients (Fig. 1 D), with these four biomarkers (ESM1, pan-Kla, H4K8la and VE-cad) previously linked to tumorigenesis [ 8 , 21 , 22 ]. When compared to matched adjacent normal tissues from 10 randomly selected OC cases, fresh tumor tissues showed consistent upregulation of ESM1, pan-Kla, H4K8la and VE-cad (Fig. 1 E). IHC staining of an OC cohort revealed significantly elevated pan-Kla and H4K8la expression in tumor tissues versus normal counterparts, with high pan-Kla/H4K8la levels associated with poor prognosis (Fig. 1 F). Notably, high ESM1 levels also correlated with unfavorable survival outcomes in our prior studies [ 19 ]. These clinical data collectively implicate ESM1, pan-Kla, and H4K8la as critical mediators of bevacizumab resistance and poor survival in OC patients. Our previous studies demonstrated that ESM1 induces lactate production by modulating PKM2 expression. ESM1 overexpression in SKOV3 and A2780 cells, analyzed via Western blotting with antibodies against various histone lactylation forms, revealed significant upregulation of PKM2, pan-Kla, H4K5la, H4K8la, H4K12la, H3K18la, and H3K9la levels (Fig. 2 A). Notably, H4K8la, a well-established cancer-associated biomarker, has been implicated as a critical molecular mechanism bridging tumor metabolic reprogramming and epigenetic regulation [ 22 ] and serves as a key histone lactylation modification in tumor drug resistance [ 23 ]. Treatment with the glycolysis inhibitor 2-DG or the PKM2 inhibitor shikonin induced dose-dependent reductions in intracellular global lactylation and H4K8la levels in OC cells (Fig. 2 B-C). Furthermore, EP300 and SIRT2 have been independently validated as the histone lactylation writer and eraser, respectively [ 24 , 25 ]. Our study demonstrates that knockdown of ESM1 reduces nuclear H4K8la levels, which can be rescued by EP300 overexpression. Conversely, ESM1 overexpression-induced H4K8la elevation is reversed by SIRT2 overexpression. This reciprocal regulatory relationship indicates that EP300 and SIRT2 directly modulate H4K8la dynamics through their opposing enzymatic activities (Supplementary Fig. 1). Strikingly, recombinant human ESM1 (rhESM1) administration significantly elevated OC cell lactylation and H4K8la levels in a dose-dependent manner (Fig. 2 D), which was partially rescued by 2-DG or shikonin treatment (Fig. 2 E &amp; Supplementary Fig. 2). Additionally, rhESM1-enhanced lactate production in OC cells was mitigated by 2-DG or shikonin (Fig. 2 F). It was observed that rhESM1-induced VM capacity in OC cells was suppressed by 2-DG or shikonin (Fig. 2 G). Importantly, lactate metabolism inhibition by 2-DG or shikonin markedly increased OC cell apoptosis under these conditions (Fig. 2 H-I). Collectively, these results demonstrate that ESM1 facilitates OC VM through histone lactylation and H4K8la. ESM1 activates Akt cascade by facilitating its interaction with ERBB2/EGFR complex To elucidate the molecular mechanisms by which ESM1 drives OC VM formation via ERBB2, we first analyzed our previous immunoprecipitation/mass spectrometry (IP/MS) data and identified potential interactions between ESM1 and both ERBB2 and EGFR [ 11 ]. IF analysis revealed co-localization of ESM1 with EGFR/ERBB2 in A2780 and SKOV3 cells (Fig. 4 A). Molecular docking models predicted that ESM1 forms stable complexes with EGFR and ERBB2 (Fig. 4 B). Co-IP experiments further confirmed physical interactions between ESM1 and EGFR/ERBB2 in SKOV3 and A2780 cells (Fig. 4 C). Reverse pull-down assays demonstrated that rhESM1 directly binds to Fc-tagged ERBB2 and GST-tagged EGFR (Fig. 4 D). Overexpression of ESM1 in OC cells promoted EGFR-ERBB2 heterodimer formation (Fig. 4 E). To map the interaction domains, we analyzed ESM1’s structural domains: signaling peptide, cysteine-rich domain, EGF-like domain, phenylalanine-rich domain, and CTD domain (Fig. 4 F). Domain deletion mutants revealed that ESM1 primarily binds to EGFR/ERBB2 through its cysteine-rich region (residues 1–46), although secondary interactions mediated by the C-terminus may also contribute (Fig. 4 G). Molecular docking identified a negatively charged pocket near Asp7 as critical for the binding (Fig. 4 H). Site-directed mutagenesis of Asp7 to Ala or Arg (ESM1-Asp7Ala/Arg) abolished ESM1-ERBB2/EGFR interactions (Fig. 4 I). ESM1 impairs autophagasome maturation and promote VM formation through mediating the activation of the ERBB2/Akt cascade Previous studies have demonstrated that VE-PTP forms a functional complex with VE-Cadherin and p120-catenin, which stabilizes VE-Cadherin by preventing its autophagic degradation, thereby promoting VM formation [ 27 ]. Conversely, activation of the Akt pathway suppresses autophagy initiation [ 18 , 28 ]. In our previous research, ESM1 was found to inhibit autophagy initiation in OC cells [ 19 ], indicating that ESM1 promotes VM formation primarily through autophagy inhibition. To validate this finding, Western blot analysis revealed that ESM1 KD reduced Akt-mTOR signaling activation and enhanced autophagy, whereas lactate treatment and ERBB2 overexpression counteracted these effects (Fig. 5 A). Furthermore, administration of the ERBB2 inhibitor trastuzumab and the Akt inhibitor LY294002 significantly alleviated lactate- and ERBB2 overexpression-induced autophagy suppression (Fig. 5 A). Consistently, tubule formation assays demonstrated that lactate and ERBB2 overexpression rescued VM suppression caused by ESM1 KD, while trastuzumab and LY294002 further antagonized VM enhancement induced by lactate and ERBB2 overexpression (Fig. 5 B). IF staining further confirmed that ESM1 KD-induced LC3 upregulation was restored by lactate and ERBB2 overexpression, whereas trastuzumab and LY294002 antagonized the molecular effects of lactate and ERBB2 overexpression (Fig. 5 C). In summary, these findings demonstrate that ESM1 suppresses autophagy and promotes VM formation via the ERBB2/Akt signaling cascade. ESM1 promotes OC VM formation to induce bevacizumab resistance A previous study indicated that autophagy is a key mechanism underlying resistance to anti-angiogenic therapies, and enhancing autophagy is a critical strategy to reduce tumor VM formation and prevent bevacizumab resistance [ 29 ]. However, the anti-tumor efficacy of autophagy agonists remains limited in clinical applications. Here, we demonstrate that ESM1 knockdown, 2DG-mediated histone lactylation inhibition, or trastuzumab-induced blockade of the ERBB2 cascade each significantly enhances the anti-tumor effect of bevacizumab (Fig. 7 A-C) by promoting autophagy activation (Fig. 7 D). Notably, combinatorial use of these approaches yielded synergistic effects. To validate these findings, IHC staining of xenograft tumors revealed downregulated expression of ERBB2, ESM1, BCL2, H4K8la, and VE-cad as well as suppressed PCNA expression in the combined treatment group. Concurrently, PAS/CD34 double staining showed reduced VM formation capacity in tumors under combinatorial therapy (Fig. 7 E). Within these samples, we also observed CD34-positive vessels, suggesting that extensive angiogenesis accompanies the formation of VM (Fig. 7 E). In our recent study, we demonstrated that ESM1 induces angiogenesis in OC via ITGB1, thereby promoting bevacizumab resistance [ 33 ]. Importantly, VM and angiogenesis function synergistically to sustain tumor perfusion and oxygen supply, enabling adaptation to the hypoxic microenvironment [ 34 – 36 ]. These findings suggest that ESM1 orchestrates bevacizumab resistance through multiple molecular mechanisms, wherein vasculogenic mimicry and angiogenesis likely represent the direct strategies employed by tumors to circumvent anti-angiogenic therapy. ESM1 binds to ERBB2 and EGFR to activate downstream Akt/mTOR/autophagy/FAK/STAT3 signaling pathways, driving target gene transcription that facilitates VM formation and bevacizumab resistance in OC cells Discussion Bevacizumab, an anti-angiogenic therapy, has significantly improved survival outcomes in patients with advanced OC [ 37 ]. However, resistance to bevacizumab remains an inevitable challenge, and effective strategies to overcome this resistance are still lacking [ 38 ]. Due to the high incidence and mortality of OC, elucidating the mechanisms of bevacizumab resistance and developing novel therapeutic approaches are urgently needed. Bevacizumab treatment reduces tumor vascularization, leading to hypoxic-ischemic conditions that paradoxically promote cancer progression [ 37 ]. Prolonged tumor hypoxia enhances glycolysis and lactate production [ 39 , 40 ]. Emerging evidence indicates that lactate can mediate epigenetic regulation through histone lysine lactylation [ 17 ]. Our previous studies demonstrated that ESM1 promotes lactate synthesis in OC cells via mediating SUMOylation of PKM2, and activates the STAT5 signaling pathway to induce VM formation in OC cells. However, whether ESM1 directly regulates OC cell VM formation through lactate metabolic reprogramming remains to be elucidated [ 11 ]. Furthermore, in another study, we found that ESM1 promotes lipid metabolic reprogramming by modulating transcriptional profiles in OC cells. Nevertheless, the underlying mechanisms by which ESM1 orchestrates transcriptional alterations in OC cells remain to be elucidated [ 19 ]. Here, we reveal that ESM1 facilitates VM formation and bevacizumab resistance via histone lactylation-mediated epigenetic regulation. Focusing on the epigenetic role of histone lactylation in bevacizumab resistance, we identified H4K8la as a critical residue in OC resistance. Truncated ESM1 peptides effectively suppressed H4K8la modification and disrupted VM formation, attenuating bevacizumab resistance. Notably, ESM1 and H4K8la may serve as negative prognostic markers and predictive factors for bevacizumab efficacy. VM is acquired by cancer cells under hypoxic microenvironments, enables tumors to form endothelial-like vascular networks independent of endothelial cells [ 11 , 41 , 42 ]. This process addresses tumor oxygen and nutrient demands while inducing bevacizumab resistance [ 11 , 41 , 42 ]. Multiple studies indicate that hypoxia-induced metabolic reprogramming is a critical driver of VM formation in cancers [ 11 , 43 , 44 ]. However, the role of lactylation—a metabolic reprogramming-associated epigenetic modification—in VM remains poorly understood. Here, we first demonstrate that ESM1 in OC cells promotes VM formation and bevacizumab resistance via histone lactylation-mediated epigenetic regulation of ERBB2. Mechanistically, ESM1 enhances lactate production, driving H4K8la formation, which facilitates ERBB2 transcription and subsequent activation of pro-VM pathways. Notably, ESM1-truncated peptides effectively suppress H4K8la modification and VM formation, restoring bevacizumab sensitivity. ESM1, an extracellular secreted oncoprotein, plays a pivotal role in regulating VM formation and metabolic reprogramming in cancer cells [ 9 , 11 ]. Accumulating evidence demonstrates that lactate, a critical byproduct of cancer cell metabolic reprogramming, regulates mRNA transcription, protein stability, subcellular localization, and enzymatic activity through lactylation modifications [ 14 ]. Previous studies have reported that ESM1 induces tumor lactate synthesis [ 11 , 45 ]. Here, we demonstrate that ESM1 promotes ERBB2 transcription by inducing histone H4K8 lactylation. In ESM1-overexpressing OC cells, ERBB2 expression physically interacts with ESM1, facilitating the formation of ERBB2/EGFR heterodimers and driving VM formation. Notably, our study reveals that truncated ESM1 peptides potently inhibit the ESM1-mediated ERBB2 activation cascade and VM formation. Tumor cell autophagy plays a regulatory role in VM formation. Studies indicate that autophagic degradation of VE-cadherin reduces tumor VM formation capacity [ 27 ]. Our previous research demonstrated that ESM1 suppresses autophagy in OC cells [ 19 ]. Here, we show that ESM1 inhibits OC cell autophagy via the ERBB2/Akt signaling cascade. Notably, treatment with autophagy agonists suppressed FAK/STAT3 signaling and VM formation in OC cells, which may potentially enhancing sensitivity to bevacizumab. Bevacizumab resistance represents a multifaceted clinical challenge, associated with aberrant endothelial cell proliferation, tumor cell VM, hypoxic immune microenvironment dysregulation, and promoting Warburg effect in cancer cells [ 46 ]. Our study identified significantly elevated ESM1 levels in bevacizumab-resistant patients, which induced H4K8 lactylation modification to promote ERBB2 transcription and VM formation. Previous findings from our lab demonstrated that hypoxia serves as a critical upstream regulator of ESM1 overexpression [ 11 ]. Notably, hypoxia itself is a prominent side effect of bevacizumab treatment in solid tumors, further exacerbating tumor anaerobic glycolysis—a phenomenon corroborated by our in vivo experiments. However, our current model utilizes nude mouse xenograft systems, which inherently lack functional immune components. Recent studies by Li et al. revealed that BCL6 suppresses tumor-infiltrating CD4 + T cell cytotoxicity via ESM1, thereby accelerating tumorigenesis [ 47 ]. Conversely, clinical cohort analyses demonstrated that bevacizumab could ameliorate lymphocyte subset depletion and enhance CD4 + T cell expansion [ 48 ]. These findings collectively suggest that ESM1 may mediate immune-infiltration dysregulation through metabolic reprogramming or lactylation modifications, though this hypothesis requires further validation through immune-competent in vivo models. We acknowledge there are limitations in this study. ESM1 plays a pivotal role in glycolipid metabolism remodeling and promotes carcinogenesis through the ERBB2/EGFR signaling cascade, as independently validated by multiple research groups. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying ESM1-mediated ERBB2/EGFR activation are likely heterogeneous. Our findings demonstrate that ESM1 activates this cascade via histone lactylation. Furthermore, while ESM1 is known to modulate multiple signaling pathways, whether these pathways further enhance ERBB2/EGFR cascade activity remains unexplored. These unresolved questions represent critical directions for elucidating the oncogenic mechanisms of ESM1. In addition, cisplatin serves as a standard clinical treatment regimen for OC patients and is frequently combined with bevacizumab to delay tumor progression [ 49 ]. In our previous study, we demonstrated that ESM1 induces cisplatin resistance through the ERBB2/HSPB1 and SCD1/Wnt signaling pathways [ 10 , 50 ]. Moreover, both our current and prior studies have identified ESM1 as a pivotal gene driving bevacizumab resistance in OC patients, suggesting potential molecular crosstalk between cisplatin and bevacizumab resistance. Notably, multiple studies have highlighted histone lactylation modification as a key mechanism mediating cisplatin resistance in OC [ 51 – 53 ]. Integrating these findings with our current results strongly indicates that the ESM1-H4K8la signaling axis may represent not only a critical mechanism underlying bevacizumab resistance but also play a vital role in the development of cisplatin resistance [ 51 – 53 ]. However, due to the limited availability of patient samples with bevacizumab resistance in our cohort, it remains unestablished whether this co-resistance phenotype is prevalent among OC patients. Rigorous validation through large-scale sequencing and comprehensive molecular biology experiments is required to confirm these observations, which simultaneously underscores the considerable clinical translational potential of this research avenue. Furthermore, this study has certain limitations. Notably, the bevacizumab-resistant patients included in our cohort also received prior cisplatin treatment. This overlap raises the question of whether cisplatin resistance might exacerbate or even drive the development of bevacizumab resistance. Multiple clinical studies indicate that while advanced OC is highly sensitive to first-line platinum therapy, recurrence and subsequent platinum resistance are nearly inevitable [ 54 , 55 ]. The AURELIA trial demonstrated that bevacizumab significantly improves progression-free survival (PFS) in patients with platinum-resistant recurrent OC; however, its impact on overall survival (OS) remains controversial [ 2 ]. Further analysis suggests that combining bevacizumab with chemotherapy at the time of first platinum resistance maximizes the likelihood of patients benefiting from this active treatment [ 56 ]. This observation strengthens the hypothesis that platinum resistance may underlie the phenomenon whereby bevacizumab yields PFS benefits but fails to translate into an OS advantage. Therefore, definitively determining whether platinum resistance is a key mediator of bevacizumab resistance warrants significant attention in future investigations. In conclusion, our findings demonstrate that in OC cells treated with bevacizumab, ESM1-mediated histone H4K8 lactylation enhances ERBB2 transcription. ESM1 further promotes ERBB2/EGFR heterodimer formation, activating Akt/mTOR signaling to suppress autophagy and stimulating FAK/STAT3 pathway activity. This cascade facilitates VM formation and induces bevacizumab resistance. Notably, targeting ESM1 or employing truncated ESM1 peptides effectively inhibits these pathways, restoring bevacizumab sensitivity both in vitro and in vivo. Our study elucidates a novel epigenetic axis—ESM1-lactylation-ERBB2—governing OC bevacizumab resistance, providing a mechanistic rationale for combining ESM1 inhibitors with bevacizumab to overcome resistance in OC therapy.
