Background Human induced pluripotent stem cell (iPSC) derived kidney organoids are a model of the human developing kidney and have great promise for personalised medicine, from drug and toxicity screening to disease modelling and potentially the generation of replacement renal tissue. Current kidney organoid protocols can generate nephron epithelium and renal stroma, albeit not to adult maturity [ 1 – 10 ]. However, a critical challenge for these protocols lies in the variable presence of non-kidney cell types (not detected in human fetal kidney) within kidney organoids (‘off-target’ populations) [ 4 – 6 , 8 , 11 , 12 ]. These ‘off target’ cell types have previously been classified based upon similarity to known in vivo cell types. Wu et al. [ 4 ] reported the presence of melanocyte ( PMEL + MLANA + ) and neural off-target populations in one protocol (Takasato et al. [ 3 ]) and neural ( SOX2 + or STMN1 + ) or muscle ( MYLPF + ACTC1 + ) off-target populations in a distinct protocol (Morizane et al. [ 1 , 4 ]). Specific neural ‘off-target’ cells expressed both brain-derived neurotrophic factor and its cognate receptor, NTRK2 , reportedly enabling suppression of this population via the addition of K252a [ 4 ]. However, this did not remove all off-target cell types. In addition to neural and muscle cell types, the formation of cartilage has been reported both in vitro [ 13 ] and after organoid transplantation [ 14 – 16 ]. While previously reported, the presence, prevalence or identity of ‘off-target’ patterning is rarely evaluated in kidney organoid studies. Given the technical variability between experiments and line [ 11 ], this represents a major challenge to accurate disease modelling, drug screening and efforts to engineer human renal tissue. The permanent mammalian kidney (metanephric kidney) originates from the intermediate mesoderm (IM), specified in a medial–lateral position between the paraxial mesoderm (PM) and lateral plate mesoderm (LPM) of the developing body trunk [ 17 ]. This mediolateral classification represents a continuum from the midline rather than representing distinct identities with tight boundaries. The IM actually gives rise to three pairs of nephron-containing excretory organs along the rostrocaudal body axis with very similar nephron patterning in each [ 18 – 20 ]. From rostral to caudal, these are the pronephros, mesonephros and metanephros. Only the metanephric nephrons persist after birth, with pronephric and mesonephric nephrons regressing. As such, these two key body axes—rostrocaudal and mediolateral—play a large role in defining the precise cues required to specify the metanephric kidney in vivo. In the mediolateral axis, bone morphogenetic protein (BMP) and transforming growth factor beta (TGFβ are inhibited medially and expressed laterally with the IM arising in the middle of these gradients [ 17 , 21 , 22 ]. Fibroblast growth factor (FGF) signalling occurs in the midline of the extending trunk and declines laterally. In the rostrocaudal axis, retinoic acid (RA) generated by the rostral somitic mesoderm induces neural commitment [ 23 ] while RA signalling is inhibited by Cyp26a [ 24 ] in the most posterior region of extending caudal mesoderm. As such, cells exiting the primitive streak early to form the rostral trunk mesoderm experience a longer exposure to RA during body elongation. The metanephric kidney arises from the IM at the level of hindlimb formation. The contributing mesodermal progenitors exit the primitive streak late, thereby having longer exposure to FGF8, but shorter exposure to BMP, TGFβ and RA signalling during patterning [ 17 ]. The metanephric kidney also expresses Hox11 paralogs not present in the more rostral mesonephros and pronephros [ 25 , 26 ]. Kidney organoid protocols utilize this knowledge, driving differentiation through gastrulation via either BMP4/Activin A or canonical wingless related integration site (WNT) signalling, then presumably on to IM and metanephric mesenchyme (MM) under the influence of FGF9 [ 27 ]. Subsequent nephron commitment is induced via a short exposure to high WNT signalling, presumed to initiate the mesenchyme-to-epithelial transition of the nephrogenic progenitors required to form patterning and segmenting nephrons within a surrounding stroma. The presence of previously reported ‘off target’ cells within kidney organoids suggests mispatterning within the culture conditions. The possibility that these represent genuine previously unappreciated in vivo renal cell types is not supported by direct transcriptional comparisons with human foetal kidney [ 9 , 12 ]. Hence, their presence suggests heterogeneity of patterning during these protocols. While directing the differentiation of pluripotent stem cells in vitro is vastly different to early patterning events in the developing embryo, provision of the appropriate growth factors across time and space has been regarded as an effective approach. While current kidney organoid protocols employ similar signalling pathways to direct according to our understanding of embryology, the driving growth factors used do not vary greatly from many other protocols patterning to mesodermal endpoints. This includes patterning to cardiac mesoderm or somites, derivatives of the adjacent LPM and PM respectively [ 28 – 30 ]. Indeed, patterning to cardiac mesoderm is initiated by high but short term CHIR while patterning to a neuromesodermal progenitor (NMP) population, an early progenitor that can give rise to both neural (spinal cord/neural crest) and mesodermal (PM) derivatives, also involves initial patterning with WNT and FGF signalling [ 31 – 34 ]. Hence, off-target endpoints may represent a predictable widening of cell fate specification due to minor shifts in rostrocaudal and/or mediolateral patterning within culture. If this is the case, reducing ‘off-target’ cell specification may be possible by more tightly controlling growth factor activity, timing and duration across the protocol. Alternatively, ‘off-target’ differentiation may be viewed as a stochastic process arising due to an artificial environment and potentially even specifying cell states that do not exist in vivo. These cellular states may represent cells poised for several outcomes which may manifest in plasticity. Stochasticity of cell response in the dish may also result in asynchronous differentiation such that different cells in the same well do not respond to the growth factor milieu in the same way or with the same timing. We sought to fully understand the presence and origin of ‘off-target’ cell types in a well-characterized kidney organoid protocol and investigate whether ‘off-target’ specification could be predictably shifted by growth factors known to influence mediolateral and rostrocaudal fate. This was achieved by varying the initial duration of canonical WNT signalling during mesoderm specification (days 3 to 5) and subsequently varying BMP, WNT, RA and FGF signalling during commitment to IM (days 7 to 9). In total, this temporal factorial screen included 21 unique variations of the initial Takasato [ 3 ] kidney organoid protocol, with the transcriptional profile analysed at the single cell level of &gt; 150,000 individual cells across four differentiation timepoints, from pluripotent starting cell to day 27 organoid. All variable conditions, including the canonical published protocol, showed evidence of ‘off-target’ cell types with considerable variation in nephron-forming capacity. Duration of WNT signalling influenced the rate of commitment to IM, relative rostrocaudal positioning and the prevalence of ‘off-target’ cell types. BMP inhibition predictably promoted a medial shift towards PM endpoints, although these were also evident in the absence of BMP inhibition. Diffusion analysis showed a clear split of axial trajectories present from early in mesodermal commitment while endpoint analysis suggested the presence of considerable glial patterning and prolonged mesodermal progenitor plasticity. We conclude that while this in vitro differentiation protocol can be predictably shifted along mediolateral and rostrocaudal axes, there is considerable stochasticity in individual cell fate decisions within the dish along with ongoing plasticity of mesodermal populations. Results Embryological patterning perturbation screen of in vitro kidney development Signalling gradients of the WNT, BMP, RA and FGF pathways drive the specification of mesodermal regions along rostrocaudal and mediolateral axes (Fig. 1 A). Appropriate specification to IM should require a combination of appropriate positioning in the primitive streak together with appropriate signals for medial–lateral patterning. The Takasato [ 3 ] kidney organoid protocol utilises WNT signalling to trigger mesodermal commitment and FGF9 signalling to direct to IM (Fig. 1 B). The original protocol used 4 days of GSK3β inhibitor, CHIR99021 (CHIR), to mimic canonical WNT signalling, followed by 3 days of 200 ng/mL FGF9 for a 7 day differentiation to IM, although 3, 4 or 5 days of CHIR have all been reported to produce kidney organoids [ 6 , 35 – 37 ]. The protocol does not exogenously control other pathways known to be involved in mesodermal patterning along rostrocaudal and mediolateral axes, such as RA and BMP signalling. This study sought to investigate whether variations in these pathways during patterning from primitive streak/trunk mesoderm to IM would reflect expected in vivo endpoints (Fig. 1 A). Arrows indicate cell populations that are either lagging behind or leading into a different stage The kidney organoid differentiation protocol was divided into four distinct stages; initial primitive streak patterning (stage 1), IM specification (stage 2), early nephrogenesis (stage 3) and final organoid (stage 4) (Fig. 1 B). Varying the duration of exposure to WNT signalling was predicted to alter rostrocaudal position [ 6 , 37 , 39 ]. Hence, stage 1 included between 3 and 5 days of CHIR exposure (3c, 4c, 5c). Mediolateral shifts away from IM patterning were investigated by modifying the growth factors from stage 1 to stage 2. This included variations in FGF9 concentration (FGF9 100 ng/ml = F, FGF9 200 ng/ml = F200) and the addition of other signals together with FGF9 (anchored at 100 ng/ml). This included inhibition of retinoic acid via the inclusion of an RA receptor inhibitor, AGN193109 (FGF9 + AGN = FA) and extended WNT signalling together with FGF9 (FGF9 + 7uM CHIR = FC), both of which were meant to promote posteriorisation. Inhibition of BMP signalling through the use of ALK1/2/3/6 inhibitor, LDN193189 (FGF9 + LDN = FL) was proposed to mimic Noggin and promote medialisation. We also investigated the removal of all growth factors after exposure to CHIR (E) (Fig. 1 B). The duration of growth factor addition between Stage 1 and 3 was fixed at 4 days irrespective of the initial mesodermal patterning (3c, 4c or 5c) to ensure equivalent exposure to patterning factors. After stage 2, all conditions were allowed to proceed through nephrogenesis in the presence of 200 ng/ml FGF9 for 5 days and then basal media to final organoid (Fig. 1 B) with one exception. GDF11 has previously been reported to expand the nephron progenitor populations [ 40 ]. This growth factor was included from stage 2 to stage 3 (FGF9 200 ng/ml + GDF11 50 ng/ml = FG) (Fig. 1 B). Two further timepoints were collected to investigate cellular transitions after the addition of FGF9; 5 days of CHIR and 2 days of FGF9 (5c2F) and 4 days of CHIR and 3 days of FGF9 (4c3F). Both conditions were collected at stage 2 (7 or 8 days respectively) and not used for organoid formation. As technical variability has previously been identified as a major contributor to kidney organoid outcome, a single starting cell line, CRL2429-MAFB mTagBFP2 /GATA3 mCherry [ 36 ] was subjected to all 21 variations in differentiation conditions in a single experiment. Final data included three technical replicates per sample of all conditions. This is the same line that has been used in previous publications using the Takasato differentiation protocol [ 35 – 37 ]. Identifying all component cell types within the temporal factorial screen Single cell transcriptional profiling was performed on all 21 protocol variations, representing a dataset across 13 timepoints and 150,957 cells (Fig. 1 C) ( https://zenodo.org/records/18732338 ). A thorough analysis across this factorial temporal series assigned 53 distinct cell identities based on transcriptional congruence with known developmental states (Fig. 1 C, Additional file 1: Tables S1 and S2, Wilson et al. [ 38 ]). The entire factorial dataset is presented as a tSNE by individual cellular state (Fig. 1 C) or stage of differentiation (Fig. 1 D). Compared to previous studies, a broader set of endpoint ‘off-target’ cells types were identified across the timecourse together with multiple transitional mesodermal states along the timecourse with these showing indeterminate relationship to in vivo tissues. Cell classification also identified neural progenitor subtypes (including NMP), neural crest and multiple glial subcategories [ 38 ]. Endpoint muscle subtypes included myotome, myocyte and satellite cell. Tables S1 and S2 (Additional file 1) indicate the relative proportion and absolute number of each cell type present across time and condition. Immediately evident from the global dataset is early evidence of mispatterning to paraxial and neural endpoints, suggesting early stochasticity in the response to the differentiation cues. By presenting each stage as a timecourse from pluripotency through to stage 4 (Fig. 1 E), cellular heterogeneity can be seen from the commencement of the protocol with each stage revealing leading and lagging populations with respect to rates of progression across differentiation. This may indicate staggered transition through EMT from the pluripotent state or variability in mesodermal specification during that process. Changing growth factors alters patterning in the final kidney organoids The objective of the study was to examine the impact on final organoid patterning of shifts to signalling during early mesodermal patterning. Using immunofluorescent antibody staining for nephron segments, the presence of nephrons was observed in kidney organoids across most conditions, although nephron patterning was only poorly evident in the absence of FGF9 (E) (Fig. 2 A) and in BMP-inhibited organoids (FL) (data not shown). This organoid morphology was reflected in the transcriptional profile for each condition (Fig. 2 B). Across all conditions, the most abundant populations were neural crest-derived Glia-1 cells, expressing PAX3, SOX2 and MSX1 (Fig. 2 B, Additional file 2: Fig. S1). A population of ‘off-target’ cells defined as glial-ependymal cells was observed only in 3c and 4c conditions, particularly in the presence of 100 ng/ml FGF9 (Additional file 2: Fig. S1). Ependymal cells are regarded as arising from dorsal midline WNT-responsive neural progenitors [ 41 ]. Satellite-cells and myocytes were markedly enriched in BMP-inhibited (FL) and FGF9-deprived conditions (E) (Fig. 2 B). Endothelium (Endo) and ureteric segments (UTip, UOS) were very limited in cell numbers with ureteric cells almost exclusively in the more rostrally patterned 3c conditions (Fig. 2 B). Final organoids generated using F, FG and F200 contained the highest level of on target patterning (Fig. 2 B, Additional file 2: Fig. S1). Organoids generated with the addition of GDF11 (FG) showed the lowest proportion of ‘off-target’ populations after 4 or 5 days of initial CHIR. While GDF11 had been proposed to support nephron progenitors [ 40 ], this was only observed in the differentiation performed with an initial 4 days of CHIR. A comparison of F and FG conditions at Stage 3 identified a suppression of LIN28A and a reduction in neural and muscle specification (Additional file 2: Fig. S2A). Cellular heterogeneity along the timecourse of differentiation is present even in optimal conditions While the observed patterning across time by condition supports a predictable shift along mediolateral axes, mispatterning was seen even in the most optimal conditions. A direct comparison of the original protocol (F200) with a reduced FGF9 concentration (F) at stage 4 suggested minimal difference in on target patterning in response to FGF9 concentration but substantial variation in off target end points depending upon the initial duration of CHIR (Fig. 4 A). The 3c4F condition contained a large glial-ependymal population present at the expense of other glial subsets present in 3cF200 (Fig. 4 B). Across the timecourse, F200 increased the prevalence of all glial populations but showed little muscle or endothelial cells (Additional file 2: Fig. S2B). Conversely, a reduced level of FGF9, particularly with short CHIR, supported glial progenitors, endothelial cells and myocytes, with a clear increase in the prevalence of PM by Stage 2 (Additional file 2: Fig. S2B). Hence, both WNT duration and FGF9 concentration improves specification but is not sufficient to canalise commitment specifically to IM. Inferring differentiation trajectories across the differentiation timecourse To fully interpret the impact of growth factor variation, the relationship between all cellular states across the dataset was investigated at the transcriptional level. The spacing between stages, which varied from 3 (start to stage 1) to 18 days (stage 3 to stage 4), compromised many pseudotime analysis approaches, causing us to leverage multiple approaches. Projecting the whole dataset using diffusion component (DC) mapping identified lineage divergences from stage 2, a continuum between these trajectories at stage 3, leading into two axes of transition by stage 4 (Additional file 2: Fig. S4A). One direction defined the nephron lineage (DC2) and the other defined the non-epithelial endpoints, including the stromal and off-target populations (DC3) (Additional file 2: Fig. S4AB). The divergence appeared to originate during stage 2. Lineage heterogeneity present in all conditions by stage 2 As diffusion analysis suggested cellular heterogeneity from early in differentiation, additional transition [ 44 ] and RNA velocity [ 42 ] analyses were performed of all conditions at stage 2 (Fig. 5 A-H, Additional file 2: Fig. S5AB). Marker genes for the IM, PM and neural commitment correlated with the differentiation trajectory when plotted as an expression dotplot (Fig. 5 C) or mapped onto the diffusion mapping (Fig. 5 D). Investigating each condition independently highlighted the variable progression along each of these trajectories (Fig. 4 E, Additional file 2: Fig. S5B). Again, the most distinct of these was the transition towards a PM lineage induced via BMP inhibition whereas removing FGF9 led to a broadening of the cell types present. A shift in patterning from neural to mesodermal populations was evident in many of the 5c (longer CHIR) conditions, providing some evidence that longer CHIR exposure is indeed shifting away from NMP and towards IM (Fig. 4 C). The moscot [ 44 ] package was then used to predict cellular origins and destinations. This suggested that distinct early patterning could also predetermine the trajectories reached by Stage 2 (Additional file 2: Fig. S5C). Applying moscot [ 44 ] to the transition from 5c2F to 5c4F predicted that all cell states could arise from the pPSM present at 5c2F day 7 cultures, identifying different subsets with higher likelihood of transitioning to neural progenitor and mesoderm off-target populations (Additional file 2: Fig. S5C). Extending the moscot projection of cell origin from stage 2 to stage 3 again showed base media only (E) led to less precise differentiation, while the canonical protocol resulted in the most accurate transition towards on target stroma (Fig. 4 D). This analysis supported a tighter canalisation between IM-1 and IM-2 and nephron progenitor/early nephron and a clearly distinct mesodermal patterning in the FL conditions, again highlighting the medialisation towards PM induced by LDN, a suppression of progression along a renal lineage with prolonged CHIR and persistent neural differentiation even under canonical conditions. It was clear that the AdvMeso-3, Neural_Crest and Myotome represent ‘off-target’ endpoints related to BMP inhibition, early neuron was present in all conditions while IM-3 was unique to the base media (E) condition. Impact of prolongation of WNT signalling and inhibition of BMP signalling While cellular heterogeneity was a common feature of all conditions, the prolonged inclusion of CHIR after the addition of FGF9 (FC) sustained expression of FGF8 and SP5, with the 5c4FC condition at stage 2 predominantly pPSM in identity. Despite 4 days of FGF9 exposure, 5c4FC is very similar to 5c2F, suggesting that continued Wnt signalling delays further mesodermal heterogeneity (Fig. 5 G). Final organoids generated from these FC conditions did not show improved levels of nephron patterning but did show notably higher stromal compartments in both 4c4FC and 5c4FC. The suppression of BMP signalling produced the most distinct phenotypic shift across the timecourse. Using CAT to examine the progression of cell state from Stage 2 to Stage 3 in the canonical protocol (F) versus inhibition of BMP (FL), it is evident that the suppression of BMP signalling more tightly canalises the transition from PM to AdvMeso3 and neural progenitor to early neuron (Fig. 5 H). By contrast, in the canonical protocol the transcriptional relationship between most Stage 2 mesodermal states (other than IM) is less distinct predicting considerable plasticity in their individual differentiation potential. Despite this, there must be residual plasticity as FL conditions at Stage 3 show no evidence of IM or NPC and yet nephrons are seen at low frequency in Stage 4. Reproducibility, generalisability and predictability of the observed stochasticity across differentiation This entire dataset was generated from a single iPSC line in a coordinated differentiation to reduce technical variability. To investigate the reproducibility of the patterning observed, the entire screen was replicated to stage 2 for all conditions using the same cell line and profiled using bulk RNA-sequencing. The resulting expression profiles were directly compared to a pseudo-bulk analysis of the original single cell data using Pearson correlation (Fig. 6 A). PM-dominant FL replicates clustered in one clade, FC replicates in another (Fig. 6 A) while a third large clade containing two smaller subsets could be identified. Within these subsets, the no-FGF9 (E) conditions from both experiments clustered while all other 4c and 5c conditions clustered away from 3c. This supported the reproducibility of patterning with respect to initial CHIR duration, presence of FGF9, impact of BMP inhibition and prolongation of CHIR with FGF9 (Fig. 6 A). To evaluate the generalisability of mispatterning using the canonical protocol, differentiation to stage 2 (both 4c4F200 and 5c4F200) was again performed using three distinct pluripotent stem cell lines, including the line used to perform the initial screen and two other lines (AICS-0061–036 and L01B [ 45 ]). While evident to varying degrees, all three iPSC lines showed evidence of off target differentiation at Stage 2 as evidenced by expression of neural and paraxial trajectory markers (Fig. 6 B). To confirm the reproducibility of overtly on and off target differentiation, immunostaining of stage 2 monolayers subjected to 5c4F revealed positivity for PAX8 and WT1, both marking IM, while neither were present in stage 2 monolayers from 5c4FL (Fig. 6 C). Brightfield analysis of the resulting organoids also failed to show evidence of nephrogenesis in 5c4FL conditions (Fig. 6 C). Finally, to better understand the relative positioning of distinct cellular subtypes, a SOX2 greenLantern :PAX8 mCherry double reporter iPSC line was generated (Fig. 6 D, Additional file 3) and differentiated to stage 2 under 4c4F200 conditions. Prior to differentiation, this line was green, as anticipated from a SOX2 + pluripotent stem cell lawn. By stage 2, there was clear evidence of PAX8 + SOX2 − (red) cells across the monolayer, but also the presence of distinct GFP + cell clusters illustrating the presence of SOX2 + PAX8 − (green) cells within the culture (Fig. 6 D). Taken together, this data suggests that mispatterning is a generalisable consequence of the differentiation protocol that can occur in many cell lines. Similar heterogeneity in patterning is evident in other mesodermal differentiation protocols The breadth of ‘off target’ endpoints spontaneously arising within this kidney organoid protocol all share a common ancestor with the NMPs which can give rise to neural, muscle and other paraxial mesoderm derivatives [ 32 , 33 ]. NMP has not been regarded as an origin for IM, although recent studies suggest that this may be the case [ 47 ]. A recent study showed that by differentiating NMPs towards neuromuscular (NM) organoids, a population of contaminating epithelial cells arises through the mesoderm differentiation [ 48 ]. Upon integrating the day 50 NM organoids with our day 27 control and BMP-inhibited samples, we found a striking overlay of cell populations across the two protocols. In the NM organoids, the epithelium directly overlaid with nephrons, while the ‘off-target’ neural and muscle cells in kidney organoid samples overlaid with the ‘on-target’ cell types within the NM organoids (Fig. 7 A). The relative proportions of each cell type showed fewer nephron-specified cells in the NM protocol versus the kidney organoid protocol with the FL conditions showing an intermediate degree of nephron and a higher level of neural endpoints (Fig. 7 B). The NM protocol involves initial patterning with CHIR and FGF2 to NMP, followed by 4 days of FGF2, HGF and IGF [ 48 ]. Hence, this is a completely divergent approach yet a highly overlapping endpoint. This is not an integration artefact as there was a near-perfect overlay of key gene expression markers in these populations between both datasets including TTN , STMN2 and SMOC2 (Fig. 7 C). Subsetting the epithelium showed accurate nephron patterning within the NM organoids, including podocyte ( NPHS1 ), proximal ( HNF4A ), medial ( SLC12A1 ) and distal ( GATA3 ) epithelial segments (Fig. 7 D). This suggests a convergence to similar endpoints due to common transitional intermediate states. It also further supports the concept that heterogeneity in organoids is an early event suggesting a need for careful evaluation from the beginning of differentiation. Conversely, the presence and identity of ‘off-target’ cell types in these structures is understudied [ 4 , 5 , 8 , 9 , 12 ]. In this report, we proposed to investigate whether ‘off-target’ populations represent predictable imperfect axial patterning to cell states outside of the intermediate mesoderm. Using a temporal factorial analysis of perturbation to a commonly applied kidney organoid protocol, we observed cellular heterogeneity for a wide array of cellular endpoints in all conditions, including the canonical protocol. While endpoint cells were predictably influenced along known rostrocaudal and mediolateral axes, simultaneous patterning to paraxial and neural/neural crest trajectories were present from early in the timecourse. The presence of transitional intermediate cell states with apparent plasticity points to a much more stochastic process within the dish. The growth factor changes to the standard protocol selected were made based upon prior knowledge around rostrocaudal and mediolateral patterning of the mesoderm [ 17 ]. As anticipated from the literature, the inhibition of BMP signalling (4FL) increased paraxial patterning, prolonged CHIR together with FGF9 (FC) delayed progression from early mesoderm to IM, while the absence of any growth factors after initial primitive streak commitment (4E) widened the heterogeneity of endpoint commitment. While transitional mesodermal states were positively influenced towards a nephron and renal stroma trajectory by prolonged initial WNT patterning, considerable heterogeneity existed in all conditions. From as early as stage 2, a tri-lineage potential was evident indicating a field of specification much wider than IM. Increasing the duration of initial CHIR patterning from three to five days prior to addition of FGF9 was previously presumed to represent the transition from a more anterior to a more posterior IM identity by day 7. Indeed, most recent protocols for patterning directly to nephric duct shorten the initial mesodermal induction [ 49 , 50 ]. Consistent with this, the presence of ureteric epithelial populations was only observed in endpoint organoids formed after an initial three days of CHIR. Evidence for cellular heterogeneity was seen from the earliest timepoints analysed, presenting as an apparent asynchronicity of cellular response from initial day of plating. In addition, the initial duration of exposure to WNT signalling exerted a disproportionately greater influence on subsequent nephrogenic versus off-target outcomes than any modifications introduced at subsequent stages of the protocol (with the exception of BMP inhibition). As anticipated, we also observed a time dependant increase in HOX paralog expression in all cell types, but this was not uniform, supporting the concept of lagging and leading cellular states. Critically, the substantive expression of HOX11 paralogs was not evident until Stages 3 and 4, while endothelium and proximal nephron cell types remained low in HOX11 expression even at stage 4. Both a longer initial period of CHIR and an extension of this signalling through to Stage 2 shifted the cells to a higher HOX code. This implies that rostrocaudal positioning requires longer initial mesodermal patterning. We have previously directly compared published data from a variety of kidney organoid protocols and the protocol used here is already expressing more HOX11 paralogs than other protocols [ 9 ]. We have also performed lineage tracing to establish that organoid nephrons are arising from a SIX2-expressing nephron progenitor population [ 6 ]. It remains possible, however, that suboptimal rostrocaudal positioning can result in mesonephric rather than metanephric nephrons as these are transcriptionally impossible to distinguish based on current data. This time series did not show mesendodermal, endodermal or lateral plate intermediates or endpoints. We also did not observe haematopoietic populations as might have been expected for the aorta-gonad-mesonephric (AGM) patterning. What was observed were neural progenitors progressing to a variety of glial endpoints, including SOX10 positive neural crest. The very small NMP (SOX2 + T + ) cell state present in stage 1 would be predicted to give rise to the neural and PM trajectories, however the diffusion analysis at stage 2 suggests that a PSM identity preceded the observed neural, paraxial and IM fates. The conditions chosen also did not address dorsoventral patterning, however the presence of glial populations might suggest a more dorsal patterning predisposition, possibly explaining the lack of apparent sclerotome. The addition of SHH may have resulted in a more ventral positioning. The relevance of this study depends upon the reproducibility and generalizability of the stochasticity observed. The detection of a similar level of heterogeneity for differential IM, PM and neural makers in four distinct lines, and the prior observations of similar endpoints using the same protocol in different laboratories supports this as being a common observation. Of note, the spatial positioning within the culture of distinct endpoint cells seen using a SOX2-PAX8 double reporter cell line also suggests a role for cell–cell communication or paracrine signalling within the in vitro environment in the individual cellular response to the media. This is worth further investigation given the extensive literature around the impact of micropatterning on early commitment [ 51 , 52 ]. What has been identified here are a set of genes likely to be of utility to compare individual differentiations, thereby assessing their suitability for organoid generation. Such quality control approaches are critical for the field. The simplicity of many existing organoid differentiation protocols is cause for consideration. The evidence of stochasticity in the response of cells from the beginning of differentiation also suggests a need to tighten this protocol from day 1. Initial induction of ‘gastrulation’ in vitro may need to more tightly specify uniform commitment to a specific ‘position’ within the primitive streak. Wnt signalling during mesodermal patterning is actually downstream of BMP signalling with specification along the primitive streak controlled by antiparallel gradients of activin and BMP4 [ 53 – 55 ]. Bypassing this step may disrupt specification to a particular differentiation trajectory. Recent studies using micropatterned 2D gastruloids suggested a combinatorial requirement for BMP and WNT signalling for appropriate mesodermal specification [ 51 ]. This may suggest a need to tighten the very earliest stages of the differentiation protocol. By performing a temporal analysis of differentiation, this study presents a different view of the identity and origin of previously identified off-target cell types. Previously identified neural and melanocytic endpoints appear to be neural crest with glial endpoints generated from early neural progenitor patterning. Our findings suggest that the in vitro system is stochastic with individual cells able to adopt a variety of intermediate and plastic cell states that can, but do not all, resolve to kidney. Comparing these results with neuromuscular organoids, a well-established but distinct differentiation protocol, revealed the formation of the same endpoint cellular states from a distinct protocol. One interpretation of this would be that both protocols specify to a much wider region of the trunk than desired with overlap to adjacent tissue types. Another interpretation, given the distinct differences in the duration and timing of FGF and WNT signalling in each protocol, is that there is a stochastic response of the cells in the dish with considerable plasticity between NMP, neural and mesodermal endpoints. This is likely to vary between starting lines. In the kidney organoid protocol, prolongation of initial CHIR suppressed some options, but incompletely. Inhibition of BMP during FGF9 patterning (FL) deepened the PM commitment but the removal of FGF9 after stage 3 allowed mesodermal intermediate states to once again commit to intermediate mesoderm, including nephron formation. This could explain the small nephron and renal stroma populations present at stage 4 even in the FL conditions. The development of safe cellular products from iPSC is assumed to require tight specification and the development of stringent quality control parameters. While a number of protocols for differentiation to nephron-forming or ureteric epithelium-forming structures include depletion or selection protocols to address heterogeneity [ 56 , 57 ], cellular manufacture is highly inefficient if sorting is required. The heterogeneous cellular states arising within all conditions suggest that much more understanding of canalization during differentiation will be required to overcome these stochastic cell responses. The influence of topology within the dish and seeding density on individual cellular interpretations is also likely to require analysis. A recent study has described a dynamical landscape of in vitro specification from NMP to neural subtypes along the dorsoventral axis of the spinal cord [ 58 ]. This predicts considerable stochasticity in cell state transitions that include binary flips between multiple states influenced via unstable saddles between attractor states. It also reveals divergence and subsequent convergence of identity suggesting there may be multiple pathways to the same outcome [ 58 ]. The identification of variable IM states with significant transcriptional overlap implies such unstable transitional states exist in our protocol. The similarity in cellular identity between kidney and NM organoids also predicts this possibility. Future predictive statistical modelling based on such factorial datasets that may enhance our protocols for canalizing differentiation towards intermediate mesoderm outcomes. Conclusions In summary, this temporal factorial analysis of kidney organoid differentiation more thoroughly exposes the imperfection of such protocols, raising the need for further improvements from the very beginning of the differentiation process. While the existing conditions remain the most optimal, the presence of ‘off target’ populations is significant. In addition, the analysis of rostrocaudal positioning based on HOX code would suggest that the majority of early forming nephrons may be mesonephric. The impact of this on accurate disease modelling of attempts to improve nephron maturation is not clear, but should be recognized. What this data can do is provide a framework for monitoring the quality of any given kidney differentiation experiment, potentially avoiding comparison between lines that have more to do with the specific experiment rather than the biological question. Kidney organoid differentiation Kidney organoid differentiations were performed as reported in Wilson et al. [ 38 ]. In summary, an initial seeding density of 7 × 10 [ 4 ] cells into 6-well culture plates was then cultured with variations based on the Takasato protocol [ 3 ] modified to use TeSR-E6 medium [ 35 ] (E6, STEMCELL technologies). Organoids were cultured from day 0 in 1.5 ml of E6 and 7 µM CHIR for 3 (3c), 4 (4c) or 5 (5c) days. Then cells were cultured in 2 mL of TeSR-E6 with one of six growth factor combinations for 2–4 days: 2 (2F), 3 (3F) or 4 (4F and 4FG) days of 100 ng/mL FGF9, 4 days of 100 ng/mL FGF9 with one of 2.5 µM AGN193189 (4FA), 1 µM CHIR (4FC), 0.1 µM LDN193109 (4FL), or 4 days of 200 ng/mL FGF9 (4F200) or base media alone (4E). Cells were then dissociated and resuspended into a dense 2 × 10 [ 5 ]/µl cell paste with TeSR-E6. Each organoid was generated by transferring 1 µl onto the top, air-facing side of a Transwell™ membrane (Corning), with six organoids per membrane as 3 sets of 2 technical replicates. This deposition method mirrors printed extrusion of cell paste [ 7 ]. A pulse of WNT signalling was provided using 1.1 mL of 5 µM CHIR in TeSR-E6 for 1 h. After 1 h, cells were cultured with 1.1 mL of TeSR-E6 with either 200 ng/ml FGF9 and 1ug/ml Heparin Sodium, with one condition also containing 50 ng/ml GDF11, for 5 days, before being cultured in TeSR-E6 only to day 27. Media was changed every second day unless changing to a new media composition on a set protocol day. Samples were collected at the end of the four stages of differentiation as defined by changes in media composition, with the final organoids collected at day 27 of culture. Single cell RNA-sequencing, processing and quality control The single cell sequencing in this study was reported in Wilson et al. [ 38 ]. Cluster alignments tool The relationships between most similar clusters (cluster alignments) were obtained using Cluster Alignment Tool (CAT [ 43 ]). Briefly, for each of the predefined clusters, the cluster–cluster similarities were measured by Euclidean distances between the cluster-averaged gene expression (the genes were limited to the highly variable genes identified using Seurat). For a given cluster, cluster alignments r are defined to be the nearest neighbor clusters, that are not statistically significant different (bootstrapping, p-val 0.05), from the closest cluster. Filtering out alignments from the one-odd-out clusters: In its original form [ 43 ], CAT will produce alignments for all clusters, occasionally resulting in the multiple large-distance alignments for the one-odd-out clusters, i.e. cluster representing a very different cell type among otherwise related types. Such clusters can be identified by evaluating mutual ranking of the Euclidian distances among the nearest neighbours. The one odd out clusters will rank low in the rank-lists of its nearest neighbours, whereas other, more related clusters, will be ranked high in the rank-lists of its nearest neighbour. To quantify this, we introduced a rank disagreement measure. For a cluster i, RD_i = 1/n_Clusters * mean_j((n_Clusters—r_ij + 1)/(n_Clusters -r_ji + 1)), where r_ij is the ranking of cluster j in i’s ranking list and r_ji is the ranking of cluster i in j’s ranking list. Here n_Clusters is the total number of clusters in the dataset. The rank disagreement measure is normalized to range between 0 and 1, with values close to 1 signifying one-odd- out clusters. RNA-sequencing processing and analysis Cultures at the end of stage2 (days 7, 8 and 9) were dissociated using TrypLE Select (Thermo Fisher Cat #12,563,011). Following collection, the cells were pellet by centrifugation (3 min, 300 g) and RNA extracted using the Isolate II RNA Mini/Micro Kit (Bioline). Libraries were prepared using the Illumina TruSeq Stranded Total RNA library Prep Kit and sequenced on the Illumina NextSeq 500 Sequencing System (Illumina) with 150 bp paired reads.
