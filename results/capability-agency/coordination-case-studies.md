# Coordination observations from Frontier work

These three cases document concrete handoffs, review findings, and changed checks. They are observational examples of coordination behavior; they do not establish that collaboration caused a better scientific result. Evidence labels distinguish files/commits inspected here from test outcomes reported by the workers who ran them.

## 1. Infra review made a CFD packet diagnosable and reproducible

**Handoff and change.** Infra's packet review asked the CFD worker to add a true packaging-only preflight, propagate incomplete runs instead of returning success, enforce an outer time budget, preserve failure diagnostics, and keep the failed physical reference gate visible. The first frozen packet then failed at geometry: all 12 STL hashes matched, but the float64 candidate JSON hash differed, so no CFD solver ran. Infra requested a CPU-only exact-image diagnosis and explicitly prohibited an unchanged GPU retry. The returned diagnostic isolated NumPy AVX-512 dispatch: three candidate fields drifted by 1–2 ULP while the float32 STLs remained identical. A predeclared geometry-only dispatch setting restored byte-identical candidate output; the candidate and STL gates stayed exact. The follow-up packet also returns candidate/components/build JSON on failure.

**Checks and current limit.** The diagnostic receipt records job `aijob-e00m8v3m77z86n5ads` as completed, rc 0, 6.2 seconds, 4,637 untruncated bytes, SHA-256 `53b393023013ba614a469abba36ae11b882d83a150b23d72437339acef385322`. The worker reports the repaired packet's local preflight rc 0 and 9 focused tests passed. The repaired GPU job `aijob-e00k0jpm34h4na3b99` is still marked `running` in the checked manifest; this note does not claim it completed or produced CFD results. The sphere physical-reference gate remains failed.

## 2. A cross-scale adapter refused unresolved fin loads

**Handoff and result.** The coupled-mechanics adapter specified that a fin surface must be explicitly resolved and carry physical pressure units before it can be accepted as a load. It rejects `resolved: false` members and `medium: none`. CFD's response documents why the refusal is correct for the current mesh: a 0.5 mm fin needs at least 923 cells along the reference length to meet the two-cell thickness gate, while its L32 grid spacing is 7.209 mm. The case JSON marks the 0.5 mm fins unresolved. The resulting next question is concrete: what affordable resolution or thin-structure method can meet the resolution gate before pressure is mapped to a line load? The current cases can support interface diagnostics, not fin-load claims.

**Checks and limit.** I read `fish_L32_Re200/case.json`: `L_cells=32`, `h_m=0.00720934375`, and the pectoral and other 0.5 mm fin components have `resolved: false`. The CFD tests assert the fin-resolution refusal. The cross-team handoffs explicitly state that viscous traction is absent and pressure is kinematic unless a physical medium is supplied. No validated fin-load transfer or biological deflection follows from these demo cases.

**Evidence inspected.** Handoffs: `/data/artifacts/delphi-evolve-rethink/messages/coupled-mechanics-to-cfd3d.md` and `cfd3d-to-coupled-mechanics.md`. Case: `/data/worktrees/delphi-frontier/cfd3d/artifacts/cfd3d/fish_L32_Re200/case.json`; focused checks: `/data/worktrees/delphi-frontier/cfd3d/tests/test_cfd3d.py`. Handoff values and test assertions were inspected directly; no CFD rerun was made for this note.

## 3. Root review found two viewer regressions after the initial suite

**Review and correction.** The viewer worker reported 16 passing browser tests, including a real fish GLB. Root's integration review then found two pick-path defects: a precedence error made the small-mesh Raycaster branch unreachable, and local hit distance was wrong for non-uniformly scaled nodes. The corrective commit changed the branch condition and computes distance in world coordinates. It added a synthetic overlapping-node fixture with two non-uniform scales, checks agreement with Three.js Raycaster across a 17×17 point grid, asserts both picker paths, and removes a vacuous assertion.

**Before/after evidence and limit.** The worker reports 16 tests passed before review, then 18 passed after correction; without the real-GLB environment, the post-fix run was 17 passed and 1 skipped. The commit records negative controls: restoring the branch bug fails the path assertion; restoring the distance bug yields 2.74 versus 5.95 against Raycaster. I inspected the corrective commit and test diff, but did not independently rerun the browser suite. These tests demonstrate specific regression coverage, not general correctness across browsers or fish variants.

**Evidence inspected.** Worker handoff: `/data/artifacts/delphi-evolve-rethink/messages/model-viewer-to-codex03.md`; root merge review: `/data/artifacts/delphi-evolve-rethink/messages/root-viewer-merge.txt`; corrective commit `56764d74d932779ec19874d77a0eac135c744cd2` in `/data/worktrees/delphi-frontier/model-viewer`.

## Reading these cases together

They show three different coordination functions: operational review turned a hard-to-diagnose numerical mismatch into a retained, testable repair; an adapter enforced a scientific resolution boundary and produced a specific next-method question; and independent integration review found bugs beyond the initial test coverage. They do not provide a controlled counterfactual, and should not be counted as evidence that a collaborating-agent arrangement outperforms a single analyst. The separate preregistered pilot scored at ceiling and measured no such advantage; see [the results card](results-card.md).
