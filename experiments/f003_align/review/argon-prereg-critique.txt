1. **First-broken-link attribution hides nonlinear selection when $L_3(t_e) \neq n^*$.** The chain stops at the first inequality against $n^*$. If $L_3(t_e) \neq n^*$, the cell is classified `L_fastest_not_dominant`, regardless of whether $m(t_e) = L_3(t_e)$ or $m(T) = m(t_e)$. You never test whether the *correct* finite-time linear prediction $L_3(t_e)$ survives nonlinear growth. This guarantees Q2 is mismeasured: cells where both IC-weighting and nonlinear selection matter are wholly absorbed into the first bin. Track the transitions $n^* \to L_3(t_e)$, $L_3(t_e) \to m(t_e)$, $m(t_e) \to m(t_g)$, and $m(t_g) \to m(T)$ as a $2^4$ contingency table, not a short-circuiting waterfall.

2. **`LIN_OK` and $t_e$ are circularly defined via the nonlinear final state $a_T$.** Defining the "linear regime" as $a(t) \le 0.1 a_T$ ties a local perturbative property of the Jacobian around $u^*$ to the unknown global nonlinear saturation amplitude $a_T$. Near a supercritical bifurcation, $a_T \propto \sqrt{\rho - 1}$ is tiny, so $0.1 a_T$ may be smaller than $\varepsilon = 0.01$, falsely triggering `finite_amplitude_start`. Conversely, for large $a_T$, $0.1 a_T$ may already have cubic terms $O(1)$ relative to linear terms. Define linear regime validity by the ratio of the nonlinear remainder $\| \mathcal{N}(u) - J(u-u^*) \|_2$ to $\| J(u-u^*) \|_2$ (e.g., $\le 0.1$), or fix an absolute threshold relative to $|u^*|$.

3. **Temporal aliasing and empty-set risk for $t_e$ and $t_g$.** Recording spectra only every $1\tau$ is too coarse when $\sigma^* > 1$: the amplitude can jump from $< 0.1 a_T$ to $> 0.5 a_T$ in a single $\tau$ step, making $t_e = 0$ (where $s(0) \equiv |w(0)|$ trivially, rigging Q2's `L_linear_departure` $< 10\%$ prediction) or leaving $t_e$ undefined if $a(0)$ is near $0.1 a_T$. Furthermore, if initial transient decay occurs before growth, "last time $\le T$ with $a(t) \le 0.1 a_T$ before $a(t) \ge 0.5 a_T$" can still select $t_e = 0$. Detect threshold crossings $a(t) = 0.1 a_T$ and $0.5 a_T$ online at every $\Delta t = 0.02$ step.

Use a $\delta$-band around the peak (e.g., modes within $5\%$ of max amplitude) or report $|\Delta n|$ alongside strict equality.

Project $(u,v)$ onto the unstable eigenvector, not just $|w_{n,u}|$.
