The authors' own caveat. In this point-mass model with constant coefficients the dynamics depend on mass only through wing loading m g/S, so at fixed (WL, AR) mass does not change range; mass enters the Pareto set only through the A* objective, which favours small mass trivially. Release speed 1.5 V_stall makes release kinetic energy (equivalent height >> 1 m for most designs) dominate the range, so ranges rise with wing loading mainly because of the release-speed convention. Both are model/assumption artefacts, not biological findings. The high-wing-loading Pareto designs zoom-climb from the 1 m release (peak height shown above) because release kinetic energy exceeds the potential-energy scale; that is a release-convention artefact, not a statement about fish. A release-height-only (fixed speed) policy would need a sourced speed.
