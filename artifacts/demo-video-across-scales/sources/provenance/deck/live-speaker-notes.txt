SLIDE 2: Newton made gravity calculable.

Suggested delivery: Newton’s law made gravitational attraction calculable. Once a model and its assumptions are stated, we can derive predictions rather than relying only on descriptions. Lean adds a different capability: it checks formal deductions inside a mathematical model.
For point masses, or the appropriate spherically symmetric idealization, F = Gm₁m₂/r². At fixed masses, replacing r by 2r gives F/4. This is an analogy, not a reported Delphi theorem. Combining gravitational and motion models supports trajectory prediction; observations establish how well those models apply.
For the biological example, let similar geometry and constant density give m ∝ L³ and S ∝ L². Then wing loading mg/S ∝ L. With steady lift and fixed air density and lift coefficient, V = √(2mg/(ρSCL)), so V ∝ √L. These relations alone do not establish an absolute maximum size. A speed, power, material or other constraint must also be specified.
Lean checks a theorem relative to its actual formal statement, imports and axioms. A successful proof does not establish that an empirical assumption is true or that the formalization captures the intended biology. No new Lean program was compiled for this slide.
Primary references: NASA: Newton’s law of gravitation; Lean reference: validating proofs. Biological design context: supplied product platform, fishGrounds and wingTrack.
Suggested delivery: Biology is not governed by one convenient equation. Mechanisms interact across scales; feedback, history, variation and environmental context affect outcomes. A mathematical model selects variables and relationships that are useful for a particular question.
The network is conceptual. It does not imply Delphi has implemented or validated every coupling. The three lenses illustrate geometry and mechanics, dynamic processes, and variation or probability. M ∝ L³, dc/dt and P(x) are representative notation, not three newly proved biological claims.
Delphi’s contribution is to connect the original claim to its scope, a mathematical representation, linked formal work where available, and empirical evidence. Several models may explain the same observation. Discriminating predictions and experiments help decide which representation is useful.

SLIDE 3: From patterns to precise relationships.

Suggested delivery: The output is a library of conditional relationships. These examples make the content of that library visible: growth rules, force balance, geometric partitions, packing, pattern formation and evolutionary thresholds.
The counts are built from the repository: 1,777 extracted claims and 194 claims carrying a formal mathematical statement come from docs/claims.json; 92 of them are theorems, meaning a mathematical statement whose proof is established (73 compiled in Lean 4 with no sorry and standard axioms only, 19 with a proof recorded in the literature). Separately, the versioned Lean law registry (lean/registry/) holds 61 laws, 36 of them accepted after human review; the rest are proposed, candidate, contested or retired. A theorem is a proof status, not a project: a proof shows a statement follows from its assumptions, not that organisms satisfy them. The attached deck lists spiral, cell-division and cell-packing families among its formalized work; the platform’s offline Lean filter explicitly includes Turing and Hamilton.
Growth preserves form — thompson1917-ch11-03. Fixed relative growth. r = r₀ exp(bθ). A logarithmic spiral follows.
Cells balance forces — thompson1917-ch07-04. Equal interface tensions. 120° + 120° + 120°. Three interfaces meet at balance.
Division has a geometry — thompson1917-ch08-02. Fixed shape + target volume. min A(new wall). Choose the least-area partition.
Packing organizes tissue — thompson1917-ch07-09. Equal cells + idealized packing. Hexagonal geometry. Local contacts constrain form.
Diffusion can create pattern — turing-ddi. Suitable kinetics + diffusion. λ(k) > 0 for some k. A spatial perturbation can grow.
Selection has a threshold — hamilton-rule. Under the relatedness model. rb > c. Benefit to relatives exceeds cost.
Scope: Logarithmic growth refers to an idealized constant relative growth rule, not every shell or horn. The 120° junction assumes three equal tensions at equilibrium. The partition is an idealized minimization at a prescribed volume; the drawing is a two-dimensional equal-area example. Hexagons depict idealized equal-cell packing, not all tissues. A Turing instability requires suitable reaction kinetics, diffusion and a permitted spatial mode: different diffusivities alone are insufficient. Hamilton’s rule depends on how relatedness, costs and benefits are defined; the bars illustrate r = 0.5, b = 3 and c = 1 and are not measurements.
The diagrams illustrate these relations. They are not proof certificates or new experimental results.
Source: supplied product platform, DelphiVisuals.LAW_ART and leanLibrary. Open the Delphi law catalog.

SLIDE 4: How it was built: the stack, a measured swarm and its collaborators

Suggested delivery: three things built this project: the compute and models we ran, the swarm of agents that ran them, and the outside agents and people who argued with us.
The stack column is the frontier node’s own usage record (swarm/frontier/built.json, observed 2026-11-01 07:39 UTC), quoted, not re-verified here. It covers the jobs and model runs that left a receipt, not every worker or account. Total tokens and spend are unknown and are not shown. A Nebius job that ended COMPLETED means the container exited, not that a scientific check passed. GPU utilisation is a range over sampled windows, because the record infers no project-wide average. No BioNeMo or BioNeMo NIM execution is established by the scoped receipts in this ledger. The one hosted OpenFold2 receipt explicitly reports upstream-source-not-nim.
The middle column is the measured four-arm comparison in test-data/agent-comparison/, pre-registered before the first run (PREREG.md). Each arm had the same model, prompt, tools and budget and had to write one pre-registered test plan per claim naming one real public dataset; plans were scored mechanically by fetching every URL and DOI. What it does not show: The single agent costs a fifth (experiment 1) to a tenth (experiment 2) of a swarm arm; its shortfall is mostly blank plans (context exhaustion), not worse dataset picks (DEVIATIONS.md, experiment 2: 8 of 8 shortfalls blank). Verified means the URL resolves to a page that names the dataset, not that the dataset can decide the claim. n=20 per experiment, one model (Sonnet 5.5) in every arm, same provider as the builder; no blind assessor from another provider scored the plans. Experiment 1 decentralized arm wrote 1 fabricated identifier on a first attempt (corrected after a board gap note), so under the strict all-attempts count it fails the hard constraint and is excluded from the best-collective-arm margin in experiment 1. A sister project with deterministic specialists (delphi-evolve) reported no collective advantage; the gain here is in the regime where context and attention are the bottleneck.
Below it, one concrete run through the stack: Thompson’s three cell-packing claims from chapter 7 (walls meet at 120°, regular hexagons only where cells are equal, hexagonal on average). A worker fetched 8 Wikimedia Commons micrographs, sent each to Cellpose (cpsam_v2, Cellpose 4.2.1) on the Nebius Scientific AI Platform (933 cells, about 4.5 s per image; every operation id, model checksum and input hash is in claim-sources/cell-geometry/results/*/cellpose.json), and measured junction angles and neighbour counts locally against a hexagonal lattice, a brick wall and a random Voronoi partition. 5 tissues from 5 species had enough interior cells. Say plainly what it is: exploratory, not pre-registered, one image per tissue. The ‘six on average’ reading is Euler’s formula, which the Voronoi control also satisfies, and an outside Moltbook agent’s segmenter-bias check showed Cellpose pulls brick walls toward 120°, so the 120° margin is inflated. Verdicts are in test-data/claim-tests/thompson-growth-form/thompson1917-ch07-04, -08 and -10.
Collaborations: the right column is parsed from COLLABORATIONS.md. Outside agents on Moltbook asked for a segmenter-bias null, separate expected and disconfirming observations, formalization tags and seeded assessment batches; each change is recorded in the file named in that table, and the agent is a dct:contributor in the provenance of the resulting nanopub. MCP usage comes from mcp/usage/*.json; the server keeps no per-user logs, so client names cannot be tied to people.

SLIDE 5: A flying fish becomes a set of constraints.

Suggested delivery: A flying fish is a useful test of the method because multiple constraints meet in one recognizable shape. The platform explicitly grounds its fish design in three Thompson claims. We show all three, then identify two supporting scaling laws in its catalog and the separate engineering lift equation.
thompson1917-ch02-10 — Flight-speed scaling, v ∝ √L for similar forms under fixed aerodynamic conditions.
thompson1917-ch02-06 — Powered-flight demand scales as L^3.5 compared with a muscle-supply scaling of L³. The platform reuses the speed relationship; its glider does not test the historical powered-flight claim about birds.
thompson1917-ch16-03 — Hollow structures distribute material efficiently for bending resistance. The printed body is modeled as a thin hollow shell; its wall thickness is a design choice, not a measured fin-ray or bone thickness.
thompson1917-ch02-01 — Weight grows as the cube of length under geometric similarity and constant density.
thompson1917-ch02-03 — Cross-sectional load capacity scales as L² while weight scales as L³, under constant material strength and similar geometry.
Engineering balance: ½ρV²SCL = mg is the platform’s steady-lift relation, not an additional claim of a compiled Lean theorem.
At S = 52.9 cm² = 0.00529 m², CL = 0.5, ρ = 1.225 kg/m³, g = 9.81 m/s² and a maximum target speed V = 8 m/s, m = ρV²SCL/(2g) = 0.0105692 kg, or 10.57 g. The 10 g reference gives approximately 7.78 m/s. These are calculations from reference inputs, not measured flight performance.
The 107.5 mm pectoral input and pelvic position 0.48 × standard length come from the supplied platform; the cited specimen row was not independently re-extracted. Fish (1990) and Park & Choi (2010) supply empirical context. Darcy Thompson’s scaling does not imply that no larger fish can ever glide: limits depend on geometry, airspeed, aerodynamic coefficients, material and power assumptions. Launch, water taxiing, ground effect, dynamic stability and control need additional models and physical tests.
All three direct design claims are included. Relatedness, Turing patterning and the other catalog examples on slide 4 are not presented as established constraints on this fish design.
Sources: supplied platform FISH_LAWS, LAW_ART, fishGrounds and liftEnvelope; Fish (1990); Park & Choi (2010). Open Delphi Design studio.
