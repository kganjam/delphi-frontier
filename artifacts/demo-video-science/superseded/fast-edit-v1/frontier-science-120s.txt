Design a fish that can glide. That request spans genes, cell programs, growing tissues, fin mechanics and whole-animal behavior.

Our approach is to make each connection inspectable. MorphoSwarm Frontier brings sourced claims, equations, assumptions and evidence into a shared workspace for people and agents. Published zebrafish studies connect transporter and ion-channel variants to altered fin proportions. They give us biological hypotheses, not a demonstrated route to flight.

We also use measured specimen lengths to constrain candidate geometry.

At the mechanical scale, consider a fin modeled as an end-loaded elastic beam. We formalized a stiffness condition that keeps deflection below one tenth of its length. Lean compiled the theorem; a corrupted law failed. The proof checks the implication. Measurements must establish whether the assumptions fit a real fin.

In the studio, you can inspect the equations, change design parameters and follow their evidence. We ran bending, aerodynamic, bioelectric and schooling models.

Some exchange numerical outputs; genetic and developmental connections still need calibration. In a separate exploration, Evo2 generated sequence continuations. Model likelihood does not establish promoter function or cell-specific expression.

Validation is a first-class part of the design: check units and assumptions, test numerical resolution, compare with observations, and preserve failures. A failed flow benchmark remains failed. An independently identified print defect becomes a revision.

Every unresolved bridge becomes a question or proposed experiment. Through MCP, contributors can retrieve exact claims, submit artifacts and challenge results. This is an early foundation for Lean for biology: reusable knowledge that says what was checked, where it applies, and what evidence is needed next. The flying fish is one demanding test case for that larger scientific commons.
