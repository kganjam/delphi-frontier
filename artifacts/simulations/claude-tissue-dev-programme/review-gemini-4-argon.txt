### 1. Statistical & Mathematical Errors
*   **Un-transformed AIC comparison (Critical):** In Section 0, you compare a linear fit ($L = a + c\cdot\text{SL}$) with a power law ($L = b\cdot\text{SL}^\alpha$) using $\Delta\text{AIC}$. If the power law was fit via log-log OLS (indicated by the "0.081 $\log_{10}$" residual in Sec 4) and the linear model on raw scales, their log-likelihoods are on different spaces and **AIC is not comparable** without a Jacobian transformation of the likelihood.
*   **Regression dilution & phylogenetic pseudoreplication (Major):** Fitting OLS to allometric data where the predictor ($SL$) has measurement error attenuates the slope $c$; Reduced Major Axis (RMA) is required. Worse, pooling multiple species within groups (e.g., "Flying halfbeak mixes two species") violates independence. Without phylogenetic generalized least squares (PGLS) or species-level random effects, bootstrap CIs and profile likelihoods (Sec 0, 4) are anticonservative and invalid.
*   **Bound-hitting fits invalidate inference (Major):** In Sec 4, the best fit runs to an unphysical boundary ($p \approx 7\times 10^9$), and in Sec 5, $\rho$ hits its upper bound (54.6) at $l = 0.1, 0.3\text{ cm}$. At a parameter boundary, the score function is non-zero, asymptotic $\chi^2$ distributions for $\Delta\text{deviance}$ (3.84 cutoff) fail, and simulated elasticities are artifacts of the box constraint.
*   **Extrapolated intercept interpretation (Moderate):** In Sec 0, $a$ is interpreted as "fin length laid down before the observed size range," yet $a = -0.33\text{ cm}$ for flying fish. A negative length is physically impossible and proves the linear model is misspecified below $1.68\text{ cm}$ SL; $a/SL$ cannot be used to decompose early vs. late growth.
*   **Inconsistent slope estimates (Minor):** On the exact same 49 rows, the asymptotic slope $dL/dSL$ is estimated as $0.729$ in Sec 0 (linear OLS) and $0.81$ in Sec 4 (log-residual nonlinear fit) without explanation of the discrepancy.

### 3. Factual Biology Errors
*   **[FACTUAL BIOLOGY ERROR] Fin ray addition is not uniform domain growth:** Section 2 models fin ray patterning via uniform domain growth, which produces synchronous frequency-doubling ($2 \to 4 \to 8 \to 16$ via peak splitting). In teleost pectoral fins, lepidotrichia (fin rays) are **not** formed by simultaneous splitting of existing primordia across a uniformly expanding fold; they are added sequentially at the margins (typically anterior/posterior edges) or condense sequentially, while *distal* growth occurs via actinotrichia/lepidotrichia segment addition and ray bifurcation (branching distally, not inserting new basal rays). The Crampin doubling regime is biologically irrelevant to basal fin-ray number.
*   **[FACTUAL BIOLOGY ERROR] Pelvic fin as a "specificity control" (Sec 5):** In four-winged flying fishes (Exocoetidae, e.g., *Cypselurus*, *Cheilopogon*), the pelvic fins are *also* hypertrophied into gliding wings and likely share the same appendicular outgrowth regulatory network. Using the pelvic fin as a negative/specificity control for a systemic or local morphogen perturbation is biologically confounded.

### 4. Weaknesses in Proposed Experiments
*   **Infeasibility of transgenics/FRAP in Beloniformes (Sec 1, 2, 4, 5):** Flying fish (Exocoetidae) are pelagic, open-ocean spawners that cannot be easily bred or maintained through a full life cycle in standard lab facilities, let alone injected for CRISPR/Tol2 transgenics (required for tagged-ligand FRAP or inducible transgenes).
*   **Uncontrolled developmental allometry in dose series (Sec 1, 5):** Heat-shock, nutrition, or pharmacological perturbations over the months required for a fish to grow to $SL = 24\text{ cm}$ will cause systemic toxicity and alter body growth rate ($s$), confounding the $r/s$ ratio. Bead implants (Sec 5) deplete in hours/days and cannot sustain a gradient up to $24\text{ cm}$ SL.
