Here are 12 flaws in the preregistered protocol that threaten its validity, falsifiability, or interpretation, along with concrete fixes required before freezing:

**1. E1 (Passive Control) is Vacuous and Pre-Tested**
*   **Flaw:** Your `pre_freeze_disclosure` reveals you already measured a passive drift of $\sim 2\times 10^{-11}\text{ m}$ over 1 s. Setting the E1 tolerance to $10^{-9}\text{ m}$ over 0.6 s (6 cycles at 10 Hz) guarantees a pass; because the geometry and gravity are strictly symmetric and float32 link forces are accumulated symmetrically or near-zero horizontally, E1 only tests trivial floating-point asymmetry under zero drive. Furthermore, you exclude Mesh 3 from E1, where condition numbers and summation order errors are worst.
*   **Fix:** Add **Mesh 3 at dt 0.5** to E1, and add an **actuated zero-net-motion control** (e.g., uniform in-phase expansion $a_i=1, \phi_i=0$) to E1 with a stated symmetry-breaking tolerance, to test solver drift under active large-amplitude float32 link forces and stick-slip chatter.

**2. E2’s Refinement Metric ($R$) Underestimates Discretization Error and is Anchored to the Coarsest Mesh**
*   **Flaw:** Defining $D$ at the coarsest level (Mesh 1, dt 0.5) and $R$ as the maximum *stepwise* difference ($\max |d_{j+1} - d_j|$) is mathematically flawed. Stepwise changes between non-dyadic meshes (16 to 24 is a $1.5\times$ factor, whereas 8 to 16 is $2\times$) are artificially smaller, and a slow monotonic drift where $|d_3 - d_1| > D$ could still pass if individual steps are $< D$. Worse, if Pattern A and B converge to the *same* value on Mesh 3, E2 still passes if $D_{\text{mesh 1}} > R$.
*   **Fix:** Define $D$ at the **finest** resolution (Mesh 3, or Mesh 2 dt 0.25), and define $R$ as the **total envelope** across the series: $R = \max_{X \in \{A,B\}} \max_{j,k} |d_X(j) - d_X(k)|$. Require $D > R$ at Both Mesh 1 and Mesh 3.

**3. E2’s Pattern B is a Time-Reversible Standing Wave (Strawman Comparison)**
*   **Flaw:** Pattern B (`seg:n=2,axis=x,mode=alt`, front and back halves in antiphase, $\phi \in \{0, \pi\}$) has reflection-time symmetry: reflecting across $x=0$ is equivalent to shifting time by half a period ($t \to t + T/2$). Up to initial-transient memory and stick-slip bifurcation, its steady-state displacement $d_B$ is zero by symmetry. Thus $D = |d_A - d_B| \approx |d_A|$, turning "pattern dependence" into a trivial test of whether Pattern A moves at all.
*   **Fix:** Either rename E2 to *"Directed vs. Symmetric Actuation"* or replace Pattern B with a non-trivial asymmetric pattern (e.g., a 3-segment sequential wave or a wave with $k=2$) to genuinely test nontrivial pattern dependence.

**4. E3’s 1% Relative Tolerance is Vulnerable to Chaotic Stick-Slip and Zero-Divisors**
*   **Flaw:** In Voxelyze, internal link updates iterate over a 1D voxel array in memory order ($x$, then $y$, then $z$). Reflecting a pattern across $y=0$ alters the floating-point summation order in `float32` link forces. Because Coulomb static/kinetic friction switching (`mu_s = 1.0`, `mu_k = 0.8`) is a hard discontinuity, a $10^{-7}$ float32 rounding difference at the stick-slip threshold produces macroscopic trajectory divergence over 6 cycles. Furthermore, if $|d(\text{original})|$ is near zero (e.g., if oblique wave $M$ stalls on Mesh 3), the relative error $e$ blows up.
*   **Fix:** Replace the pure relative tolerance with a hybrid tolerance: $|d(\text{mirror}) - S d(\text{original})| \le \max(0.01 |d(\text{original})|, 10^{-5}\text{ body lengths})$. Explicitly note whether Voxelyze’s voxel iteration order is symmetric; if not, a failure at fine dt/mesh may be chaotic amplification of float32 non-associativity across the friction threshold, not a geometric bug.

**5. "Equal Total Actuation" Ignores Spatial Low-Pass Filtering across Meshes**
*   **Flaw:** You state that evaluating $a_i$ and $\phi_i$ at voxel centres makes it the "same function at every mesh level," and you normalize $\langle a_i^2 \rangle = 1$. However, for a travelling wave $T_i(t) = A \sin(k x_i - \omega t)$, a voxel of size $\Delta x$ averages strain over its volume. At coarse meshes (or high $k$ in the sweep, like $k=4$ on an 8-voxel body, which is the Nyquist limit of 2 voxels/wavelength!), discrete sampling at voxel centres aliases phases and changes the effective mechanical work and spatial gradient of strain. Also, for $B$ (`seg:n=2`), a sharp discontinuity at $x=0$ has different interfacial link density per volume across meshes.
*   **Fix:** Specify whether normalization $\langle a_i^2 \rangle = 1$ is computed analytically on the continuous field or discretely per mesh *after* sampling at voxel centres. State explicitly how phase discontinuities (at $x=0$ in Pattern B) are tie-broken if a voxel centre ever lands on a boundary.

**6. Impulsive Velocity Shock at the Start of Measurement ($t = t_{\text{settle}}$)**
*   **Flaw:** Actuation is $T_i(t) = A a_i \sin(2\pi f t + \phi_i)$ turned on at $t_{\text{settle}}$. For any voxel with $\phi_i \neq 0$, $T_i(t_{\text{settle}}^+)$ jumps instantaneously from $0$ to $A a_i \sin(\phi_i)$. In an explicit Euler spring-mass system, a step discontinuity in rest-length injects an artificial high-frequency elastic shockwave and immediate floor slip at $t=0$ of the measurement window, which may not fully decay in 1 cycle and varies wildly with mesh resolution (since wave speed scales with mesh).
*   **Fix:** Multiply the actuation envelope by a smooth ramp over cycle 1 (the declared transient cycle), e.g., $w(t) = \sin^2(\frac{\pi}{2} f t)$ for $0 \le t < 1/f$, and $w(t) = 1$ for $t \ge 1/f$, or prove that the shock decays before cycle 2 across all meshes.

**7. Open-Ended Settle Criterion ("Restated, Not Read as a Defect") is Gameable**
*   **Flaw:** The protocol says: *"If not met the control is restated with a longer settle, not read as a defect."* This violates preregistration: it allows infinite retries and changing `settle_s` post-hoc if floor chatter happens to exceed $10^{-6}$ on Mesh 2 or 3. Moreover, the settle criterion only checks net drift $|x(0.30) - x(0.25)| \le 10^{-6} L$, which could pass by aliasing if the body is oscillating at 20 Hz with a period of 0.05 s!
*   **Fix:** Measure the settle criterion as the **maximum peak-to-peak COM excursion** $\max_{t_1, t_2 \in [0.25, 0.30]} |\text{COM}(t_1) - \text{COM}(t_2)| \le 10^{-6} L$ (not just start-to-end difference of the window), and fix a hard cap on settling retries (e.g., if 0.3 s fails, try 1.0 s once; if 1.0 s fails, E1 FAILS).

**8. Global Damping ($\zeta_{\text{global}} = 0.1$) Scales Unphysically with Mesh Refinement**
*   **Flaw:** Need to watch out for how Voxelyze parameterizes `zeta_global`. In Voxelyze, `zeta_global` is a ratio of critical damping of a *single voxel* mass-spring ($c_{\text{global}} = \zeta_{\text{global}} \cdot 2 \sqrt{m k}$). Because voxel mass $m \propto \Delta x^3$ and link stiffness $k \propto E \Delta x$, the damping force per unit volume scales as $\frac{\sqrt{m k}}{\Delta x^3} \propto \Delta x^{-1}$! Refining the mesh from 8 to 24 increases the effective physical drag per unit volume by **$3\times$**. Your mesh refinement series in E2 and E3 is therefore changing the physical PDE at every mesh level!
*   **Fix:** Verify Voxelyze's `CVX_Material::setGlobalDamping` formula in commit `4287e365`. If it scales with voxel size $\Delta x^{-1}$, you must scale `zeta_global` proportionally to $\Delta x$ across Mesh 1, 2, and 3 (e.g., $\zeta_{\text{global}} \in \{0.1, 0.05, 0.0333\}$) to keep the physical drag constant per unit volume, or else your mesh refinement series cannot converge to the continuum limit.

**9. Unspecified Compiler Floating-Point Contraction (FMA) Breaks Determinism**
*   **Flaw:** You specify `FLAGS='-O3 -std=c++11 -Wall'` and claim *"identical inputs give identical outputs."* In GCC/Clang, `-O3` enables `-ffp-contract=fast` by default, which emits Fused Multiply-Add (FMA) instructions depending on the host CPU architecture (e.g., AVX2 vs. ARM64 vs. older x86). Given chaotic Coulomb friction switching, a single FMA difference changes the trajectory and invalidates E3 and receipt reproducibility across hosts.
*   **Fix:** Add `-ffp-contract=off -fno-fast-math` (and `-mfpmath=sse -msse2` if x86_64) to the build flags, and lock the compiler name, version, and CPU microarchitecture in the protocol.

**10. Ambiguous Definition of "Recommended dt" and Stroboscopic Sampling Alignment**
*   **Flaw:** E2 defines `dt scale {0.5, 0.25...} x recommended dt`, and displacement is measured from "end of cycle 1 to end of cycle 6" (i.e., $t = 0.1\text{ s}$ to $t = 0.6\text{ s}$ after settle). Since Voxelyze's `recommendedTimeStep()` is an irrational floating-point number derived from $\sqrt{m/k}$, $0.1\text{ s} / dt$ is not an integer. Whether the runner steps *past* $0.6\text{ s}$, stops *before* it, or interpolates, introduces an $O(dt)$ phase error at cycle boundaries.
*   **Fix:** Write down the exact formula for `dt` or round `dt` down so that one cycle period ($1/f = 0.1\text{ s}$) is an **exact integer multiple** of `dt` at all dt scales and mesh levels.

**11. Missing Exact Coordinate Definitions for Pattern Evaluation**

**12. E1 Gate Does Not Cover Simulation Divergence in E2/E3**
