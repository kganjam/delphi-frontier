### 1. R4: Torsion-Rigid Assumption Misses Aeroelastic Twist (Washout/Divergence)
* **The Error:** Pure spanwise bending only tilts the lift vector (losing $\approx 1 - \cos(\theta)$ in $C_L$, which is trivially $<1\%$ for a $10\%$ tip deflection). In reality, swept/tapered fins and printed wings with a shear center behind the quarter-chord experience **aeroelastic twist**. A $2^\circ$ washout at the tip alters $C_L$ by $\sim 15-30\%$, not $<1\%$. Furthermore, biological Exocoetidae fins have interconnected, corrugated membrane-ray architectures (with V-shaped cross-sections) that vastly increase $EI$ and $GJ$ when spread under load; scaling a single *bluegill* (sunfish) ray from Alben 2007 by $\times 15$ is biomechanically invalid for a flying fish.
* **<1-hr Fix:** Add a 1D torsional spring/rod equation ($GJ \frac{d\theta}{dy} = T(y)$) coupled to the VLM pitching moment about the structural axis (assume elastic axis at $0.4c$, $G \approx E/2.7$ for Alpha Grey resin, $J \approx \frac{1}{3} c t^3$). Update the VLM panel normals with $\theta(y)$, not just dihedral $\frac{dw}{dy}$. Drop the bluegill scaling and cite actual flying fish fin morphology (e.g., Davenport 1994 or Park & Choi 2010) as a qualitative bound.

### 2. R2: Uncoupled Ballistic Trajectory & Unphysical Low-$Re$ Drag Polar
* **The Error:** Assuming a constant $C_L = \min(C_{L,\max}, C_{L,(L/D)\max})$ from an $8\text{ m/s}$ hand launch at $1.35\text{ m}$ is non-physical. At $8\text{ m/s}$, a $25\text{ g}$ glider with a enlarged $1.8\times$ wing generates $L \gg W$, causing it to **zoom climb, bleed airspeed, stall, and dive**, not glide at constant $L/D$. Your $17.9\text{ m}$ range is a kinetic-energy mirage. Additionally, at $Re_c \approx 1.5\times 10^4 - 3\times 10^4$, printed thin fins do not follow $1.5 \times C_{f,\text{Blasius}}$; laminar separation bubbles and leading-edge separation on thin/sharp plates make $C_{D0} \approx 0.03 - 0.05$ and $e \approx 0.6 - 0.7$.
* **<1-hr Fix:** Replace the algebraic/constant-$C_L$ range equation in R2 with a 30-line `scipy.integrate.solve_ivp` point-mass ODE ($\dot{V}, \dot{\gamma}, \dot{x}, \dot{z}$) using the **fixed trim $C_L$** (e.g., $C_L = 0.24$ or $0.55$ from R5), penalizing $C_{D0}$ by adding a low-$Re$ profile drag floor of $+0.025$ to the wing and $+0.015$ to the body.

### 3. R1: "v3b" Camber/Incidence Confounding & Missing Body-Wing Interference
* **The Error:** A mean normal $x$-component of $-0.095$ means the *chord plane itself* is rotated $-5.4^\circ$ nose-down, not that the wing has negative camber. Saying $C_L(0^\circ) = -0.066$ vs. $0.23$ for the flat wing is comparing two different geometric incidences, plus a cambered surface with an reflexed/upward-sloping trailing edge moves the center of pressure forward and destroys lift. Also, running a thin-surface VLM without the fuselage (which is $\sim 15-20\%$ of the span wide) overpredicts lift and misses the fuselage upwash/carry-over loss.
* **<1-hr Fix:** Compute the root-to-tip local chord vector ($\vec{r}_{TE}(y) - \vec{r}_{LE}(y)$) of the `v3b` CAD mesh; subtract its mean pitch angle from your VLM reference $\alpha$ so $\alpha=0^\circ$ is defined relative to the root chord, not the global CAD axes. In the VLM, zero out (or scale by $0.5$) the circulation on the inboard spanwise panels that lie inside the fuselage width.

### 4. R2: Linear $0.85\times$ Neutral Point Shift for a $0.7\times AR, 1.8\times S$ Wing
* **The Error:** Assuming $\Delta x_{NP} = 0.85 \Delta x_{\text{wing}}$ holds only when wing area ($S_w$), aspect ratio ($AR$), and downwash ($\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}$) are constant. When you scale pectoral area by $1.8\times$ and $AR$ by $0.7\times$, the tail volume coefficient drops by $\sim 45\%$ and downwash at the pelvic fins increases substantially ($\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \propto C_{L\alpha}/AR$). Your actual NP is much further forward or aft than the linear rule predicts, invalidating your ballast masses and the claim that "every config fails the hand-launch mass limit."
* **<1-hr Fix:** Replace the $0.85\times$ rule in R2 with the exact two-surface NP formula inside the optimizer loop:
  $$x_{NP} = \frac{C_{L\alpha,w} S_w x_{ac,w} + \eta_t C_{L\alpha,t} \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) S_t x_{ac,t}}{C_{L\alpha,w} S_w + \eta_t C_{L\alpha,t} \left(1 - \frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha}\right) S_t}$$
  using Helmbold’s $C_{L\alpha}(AR)$ and $\frac{d\epsilon}{d\alpha} \approx \frac{2 C_{L\alpha,w}}{\pi AR_w}$.

### 5. R5: Uncoupled Strip Theory in 6-DOF Misses Pitch & Roll/Yaw Damping Derivatives
* **The Error:** Applying quasi-steady forces at each fin's quarter-chord in PyBullet without rotational velocity terms ($\vec{v}_{\text{local}} = \vec{v}_{CG} + \vec{\omega} \times \vec{r}_i$) or chordwise pitch damping ($C_{mq}$) makes the glider artificially undamped in pitch and Dutch roll. With an initial pitch rate dispersion of $2-4\text{ rad/s}$ at launch, an undamped $0.159\text{ m}$ glider will tumble or oscillate wildly, creating false collision statistics.
* **<1-hr Fix:** In your PyBullet force-application loop, ensure the local velocity at each surface's $3/4$-chord point (not $1/4$-chord, to capture Kutta-Joukowski pitch damping naturally!) is computed as `v_34 = v_cg + np.cross(omega, r_34 - r_cg)`, and use that vector to compute local $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

### 7. R5: School Collision Study Without Wake Vortex Interaction
* **The Error:** At $0.25\text{ m}$ lateral spacing and $0.226\text{ m}$ span, wingtips are separated by **only $2.4\text{ cm}$** ($0.1\text{ spans}$). The induced upwash and roll moment from a neighbor's tip vortex at $y = 1.1 b/2$ is strong enough to roll a $25\text{ g}$ glider by $>20^\circ$ in $0.2\text{ s}$. Simulating a "school" with zero aerodynamic coupling is just running 8 independent Monte Carlo trajectories in the same rendering window.
* **<1-hr Fix:** Add a point-vortex horseshoe kernel between the 8 agents in PyBullet: each fish $j$ trails two semi-infinite tip vortices of strength $\Gamma_j = \frac{L_j}{\rho V_j b'}$ ($b' = \frac{\pi}{4}b$). Sum the Biot-Savart induced velocity $\vec{w}_{ij}$ at the left and right mid-semi-spans of fish $i$ to add an induced $\Delta \alpha_{\text{left}}$ and $\Delta \alpha_{\text{right}}$ (generating both induced lift and rolling moment).

### 8. R2/R5: Missing Ground Effect (Biological vs. Glider Discrepancy)
* **The Error:** Flying fish achieve $L/D \approx 10-15$ and extended ranges primarily by skimming within $0.2 - 0.5\text{ spans}$ of the water surface (wing-in-ground effect, WIG), which reduces induced drag by $30-50\%$. Your R2/R5 simulations end at $z=0$ from a $1.35\text{ m}$ drop ($z/b \approx 6\to 0$), yet you compare fin sizing and $L/D$ to biological flying fish that evolve for WIG and intermittent caudal-fin taxiing.
* **<1-hr Fix:** Multiply the induced drag factor in R2 and R5 by McCormick’s ground-effect factor as a function of instantaneous altitude $z$:
  $$\phi(z) = \frac{(16 z/b)^2}{1 + (16 z/b)^2}$$
  and report the last $0.2\text{ m}$ of altitude separately to show whether your glider actually benefits from WIG before impact.
