# SPEC-016 — Narrated hackathon demonstrations

Deliver two coherent cuts, 60 and 120 seconds, using MiniMax narration, actual
interface captures and recorded simulation media. The short cut is a compact
story, not a sped-up copy. The longer cut covers the principal design, evidence,
experimental planning, collaboration and infrastructure surfaces.

The frozen script and scene plan live in `artifacts/demo-video/storyboard.json`.
It gives scene timing, narration, headlines, visual direction and source anchors.
Changes to spoken scientific claims require source checking before synthesis.

## Story and judging evidence

- Problem significance: multiscale organism engineering exposes missing causal
  bridges, rather than pretending a whole organism has been designed successfully.
- Scientific impact: recorded simulations, controlled model comparisons,
  prospective identifiability analysis and an independently caught print defect.
- Decentralized agency: separate contributions and review, exact versions, public
  MCP and reusable evidence. Do not claim all work was autonomously decentralized.
- Collective capability: show the actual independent correction. The recorded
  matched-budget comparison was tied at its ceiling; no general advantage is shown.
- Execution: real product surfaces and actual output clips. Source citations,
  model conditions and retained failures remain readable.
- Collaboration: invite other agents to reuse the public MCP. Do not imply an
  external team has already reused it without a receipt.

## Media requirements

- Final MP4s: `frontier-60s.mp4` and `frontier-120s.mp4`, 1920×1080 preferred,
  H.264/AAC, each below the website's 64,000,000-byte video limit.
- Target durations within 0.25 seconds; no clipped narration. Use measured speech
  duration to place pauses; avoid unnatural speeding to force a fit.
- MiniMax model, voice, synthesis parameters, returned usage and nonsecret request
  receipts are retained. Never record credentials. Dollar spend stays unknown
  without a billing receipt.
- Include subtitles, posters and a manifest of source and output hashes.
- Text stays readable: headlines at least 56 px, captions at least 30 px, generous
  safe margins. Show one idea at a time; pause on evidence/result cards.
- Real UI captures record route, release SHA, time and viewport. Real simulation
  clips retain their model/status labels. Changed playback speed is declared.
- No invented simulation frames or dashboards. Camera moves and transitions are
  presentation effects, never new scientific trajectories.
- Distinguish simulation, prediction, proposal and observation. Current fish
  models are not biologically validated organisms or flight-tested prints.
- Name actual Nebius, Google, OpenFold, Evo2 and local OpenResearch use precisely.
  BioNeMo/NIM execution is not established and must not appear in a "used" list.

## Acceptance

1. Inspect each final media container: duration, resolution, frame count, codec,
   audio stream, file size and decode success.
2. Inspect representative frames from every scene and all transitions; confirm
   readable text, no clipped UI, no credentials and correct source/model labels.
3. Check narration/subtitle alignment and audio levels, and review the spoken
   claims against source records. Record any scope not checked.
4. Rehash all inputs and outputs; publish both downloadable videos and posters
   through Frontier's guarded artifact surface, with source/provenance links.
5. Verify public HTTP and playback/download behavior on the actual deployment.
