# M4 — What a nervous system would have to implement, and what these simulations do NOT establish

Project: flying-fish · Scale: **neural** · Evidence kind: argument over simulated models (M1–M3), not observation.
Companion models: `engineering/neuro/fast_start.py` (M1), `caudal_cpg.py` (M2), `glide_stab.py` (M3); outputs in `artifacts/neuro/`.

## 1. The control chain and the neural function each link requires

| Phase | Control problem | Minimal neural implementation required | Candidate substrate in teleosts (from the cited literature) | Model that represents it here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Detect threat | Threshold detection on a looming, or abrupt mechano-acoustic, stimulus | A threshold detector, with feed-forward inhibition, on angular image size or its rate (visual), or on a pressure or particle-motion transient (lateral line / inner ear) | Mauthner cell plus its presynaptic inhibitory interneurons (Preuss 2006; Korn & Faber 2005; Eaton 2001) | M1 trigger: `theta_thr`, `p_resp` |
| Decide | All-or-none commit, side selection, escape trajectory | Winner-take-all between the left and right command neurons; mapping from stimulus angle to turn magnitude; some randomness (50–90% away responses) | Reciprocal M-cell inhibition; the brainstem escape network sets the timing and size of the agonist/antagonist contractions (Eaton 2001; Domenici 2011) | M1 escape-trajectory (ET) mixture, `p_away` |
| C-start | A single large contralateral bend in about 10–40 ms | A command spike reaching every ipsilateral motor pool at once; inhibition of the contralateral side | M-axon to spinal motor neurons and commissural inhibition | M1 `lat_M_ms`, `t_stage1_ms`, `omega_max` |
| Burst to the surface | A rostro-caudal travelling wave whose frequency and amplitude set by descending drive; steering in pitch | A spinal CPG with left–right alternation and an intersegmental phase lag; a descending drive that sets the frequency; a pitch steering command | Spinal CPG interneurons; reticulospinal drive (behavior-cpg's oscillator form is the abstraction) | M2 chain (reused behavior-cpg) + EBT thrust |
| Taxi | A very high tail-beat frequency with only the lower caudal lobe in the water | The CPG has to run at the highest frequency the muscle can follow; it may need a different motor-pool recruitment than swimming does | Unknown for Exocoetidae | M2 lobe thrust; M1 taxi phase |
| Glide: pitch | Hold the attitude or angle of attack against gusts | Rate sensing, an attitude reference and a delay-compensated controller acting on the pelvic and pectoral fin muscles | Semicircular canals (rate), otoliths (gravito-inertial vector), vestibulospinal and fin motor pathways; for posture control in larval zebrafish see Ehrlich & Schoppik 2017 | M3 pitch loop |
| Glide: roll | Hold the bank angle against an airframe that is open-loop unstable in roll/yaw | A short-latency rate reflex plus a **bank reference that is not just the otolith signal**. In flight the otoliths sense the aerodynamic side force, i.e. sideslip, not bank angle, so otolith-only bank control works only for some fin geometries (M3). A visual horizon or dorsal light response is the robust reference | Semicircular canals; visual and optomotor pathways (tectum, pretectum); dorsal light response | M3 lateral loop: vision vs otolith vs rate-only |
| Re-entry | Fold the fins, pitch down, restart swimming | A sequenced motor programme triggered by water contact | Unknown | Not modelled |

## 2. Quantitative requirements implied by the models (seed 20261101; numbers from artifacts/neuro/*/manifest.json, all runs exit 0)

- **Latency (M1).** From looming onset to the Mauthner command takes a median of 0.59 s (5–95%: 0.27–1.25 s). That brackets Preuss 2006's goldfish means of 142–716 ms, and it is set by the looming threshold, not by spike conduction. In the latency sweep the probability of capture is flat from 5 to 20 ms of Mauthner-path delay (0.71 to 0.74) and rises only beyond about 40 ms (0.79 at 40 ms, 0.88 at 80 ms, 0.96 at 160 ms). A fast identified-neuron pathway matters once the alternative is a slow, multi-synaptic route.
- **Power, not rhythm, sets the emergence speed (M2, M1).** The reused CPG locks wherever |(N−1)·2πδν| ≤ c; this matches the numerical integration in all 48 cases. Without a power limit, Lighthill thrust tracks Bainbridge's empirical speed–frequency relation within about 15% over 8–30 Hz (not fitted). The binding constraint is muscle power. At 50 / 150 / 250 W per kg of muscle the submerged equilibrium is 2.4 / 3.5 / 4.2 m/s. Holding 10 m/s underwater would need 1,600–4,900 W/kg. In the Monte Carlo the median emergence speed is 3.4 m/s (5–95%: 2.5–4.4). No neural command can make up that difference, so the speed has to come during the taxi.
- **Taxi (M2, M1).** Lobe thrust is limited by power and by residual wetted drag. Its top speed ranges from 6 m/s (50 W/kg, 10% wetted) to 19.5 m/s (250 W/kg, dry). Take-off at 1.1 × stall speed (11.9 m/s) needs at least about 150 W/kg with ≤ 2–5% residual wetted drag. Over the sampled priors only 4% of the fish that emerge take off: wetted fraction, taxi time and muscle power are the leading rank correlates. After take-off, the existing glide model gives 16.6 m (release at h0 = 0.1 m), against 24.0 m with its own h0 = 1 m convention and 25.1 m for the fixed 12 m/s, 1 m launch on the glide-fixed-launch card.
- **Pitch (M3, r3-daane geometry).** The airframe is passively stable in pitch for static margins from 0 to 30% of the MAC (computed NP 56.9 mm, inside the repo bracket of 55.6–58.3 mm). With feedback, the **canal-rate loop** sets the delay limit: at nominal gain the loop goes unstable at a vestibular delay of about 40–45 ms. A visual attitude delay of up to 200 ms is tolerated if the canal delay is 10 ms. Under 2 m/s gusts over 5 s, feedback prevents every pitch failure, while the passive airframe fails 17–20% of the time.
- **Roll (M3).** The lateral open loop is unstable in most configurations swept (spiral or Dutch roll). With a small caudal fin (k_v = 0.05) there is a directional divergence that no roll feedback fixes. With k_v ≥ 0.15, vision plus canal feedback is stable to a vision delay of 150–200 ms. Otolith-only "bank" sensing works only in a narrow band of dihedral and fin area, because in flight the otolith senses side force (that is, sideslip), not bank angle. Under 2 m/s gusts over 5 s the failure rates are: passive 99%; canal-rate damping only 57–62%; otolith plus canal 77–82%; vision plus canal 35% at 30 ms vision delay, rising to 84% at 150 ms. Under 1 m/s gusts every active configuration stays below 10%. **The requirement is a short-latency rate reflex plus a visual bank reference whose latency stays below about 60–100 ms.** Gain should be modest: about 3× nominal destabilises.
- **Emergence angle is a command, not a prediction.** Its distribution in M1 (20–45°) reproduces the assumed steering command γ*, because the pitch-rate cap never binds at these depths.

### 2b. Added by the Nebius jobs (see artifacts/neuro/j1-m1-sobol, j3-m5-mcell)

- **What to measure first (J1, Sobol over all 32 M1 inputs, 2 × 139,264 runs, all controls pass).** Take-off (3.4% of fish that emerge) is controlled jointly by muscle power (total index 0.81), residual wetted drag during taxi (0.67) and taxi duration (0.67); the sum of first-order indices is only 0.25, so it is an interaction effect. Emergence speed is controlled by muscle power (0.59) and the swimming drag factor (0.33). Capture is controlled by predator speed (0.83). Mauthner-path latency within 5–20 ms explains about 3% of capture variance. Within its plausible range, the fast identified-neuron pathway matters far less than muscle physiology and drag.
- **A spiking decision neuron (J3, M5).** This is the first simulated neuron in the project: a leaky integrate-and-fire M-cell with feedforward inhibition, driven by a looming encoder. Three encoders were tested: Preuss κ, the LGMD η function and pure angle. Each reproduces the abstract-level signatures (response probability 0.70–0.91, a response angle that does not depend on approach rate, latency rising with l/v) in about 1–1.6% of its parameter space. Those data therefore **cannot discriminate the encoders**; per-l/v response-angle measurements would. All consistent sets need large membrane noise (σ ≈ 0.45–0.6 of threshold), which is what makes the decision probabilistic, and they mildly favour strong feedforward inhibition (g_I/g_E ≈ 1). The consistent κ sets respond at a median 8° (5–95%: 5.5–18.6°). That is smaller than M1's assumed 15–40° threshold, and job J5 propagates it into M1. The lower tail sits on the 5° stimulus onset, so it is a protocol floor.

## 3. What a simulated controller does NOT establish about a nervous system

1. **It does not show that fish use these computations.** A PD controller that stabilises the model shows that *some* controller with these delays and gains *could* work. Many neural implementations, and many non-neural ones (passive fin compliance, morphology), are compatible with the same closed-loop behaviour.
2. **Only one reduced neuron is simulated (M5); no synapses, circuits or muscle fibres.** The CPG is a phase-oscillator abstraction (Ijspeert 2007 form, as in behavior-cpg). The Mauthner cell is reduced to a threshold plus a latency. The muscle is reduced to a low-pass filter plus a power cap. None of these is fitted to Exocoetidae recordings.
3. **No parameter is calibrated to flying fish.** Latencies, turn rates and escape trajectories come from goldfish, zebrafish and multi-species reviews. Tail-beat frequencies during taxi are recalled values (Edgerton & Breder 1941) that were not re-read here. Muscle power is an assumed range. Sources marked `recalled_not_reread` in `sources.json` are wide priors, not measurements.
4. **The sensors are idealised.** Delays are pure transport delays, and noise is white Gaussian. The low-frequency dynamics of the canals, otolith dynamics (the otolith signal is the instantaneous specific force only), visual flow processing and lateral-line sensing in air are not modelled.
5. **The plant is linear and quasi-steady.** M3 uses small-perturbation derivatives (Helmbold, downwash, tail volume, strip-theory roll), and the caudal fin is assumed to be the only vertical surface (area and arm swept). There is no unsteady aerodynamics, no ground effect (which Park & Choi 2010 show matters near the surface), and no fin flexibility beyond ff-organ's −0.54% lift-slope factor.
6. **The predator is not a model of an animal.** It is a pure-pursuit point with an assumed speed. The capture probabilities compare model variants only. They are not survival estimates.
7. **Nothing here links genotype to behaviour.** There is no developmental or genetic route from any gene in the project to these circuits.

## 4. Experiments that would calibrate or falsify these models

- M1: high-speed video at sea of flying-fish emergence (downward drone plus an underwater camera), giving the speed and path angle at emergence and the taxi speed history. Captive juveniles given looming stimuli: latency distributions and escape trajectories. **Falsifier:** emergence speeds of 8–10 m/s or more, with taxi only a small speed increment, would contradict the power-limited M1/M2 chain.
- M2: tail-beat frequency and amplitude against speed during burst swimming and taxi (video); in-situ work-loop power of axial white muscle at the habitat temperature. **Falsifier:** measured muscle power at or above about 1000 W/kg, or a taxi that does not accelerate the fish.
- M3: free-flight attitude records (onboard IMU on a released model; video of animals) under natural gusts. Perturbation tests that occlude vision versus unload the otoliths in a tethered preparation, and fin EMG latencies after an imposed roll. **Falsifier:** robust roll recovery when vision is blocked, in a coordinated glide, would mean a non-visual bank cue that this model lacks.
